# Stylized textures from recipes replace artists

All graphics of an open-world engine can be generated by code, if two conditions are met. The first is stylization, in which generators compete in silhouette, colour and layout, not in photographic detail. The second is discipline, which makes every asset a pure function `(program, parameters, seed) → blob`. The owner decided on the style: **full 3D with a free perspective camera (third person, camera behind the vehicle), rendering at native resolution and a visual bar of at least GTA III (2001) or World of Warcraft (2004)**. This means textured surfaces with a clear stylized character, painterly stylization after WoW, and a dense city with textured buildings, cars and pedestrians after GTA III. Both photorealism and isometric 3D pixel art are rejected. Pixel art is rejected because stable pixels without shimmering exist only with a non-rotating orthographic camera, and because it does not reach the set bar anyway. Low internal resolution remains only as an optional performance switch. I recommend choosing techniques by category. **Materials** are tiling sets (albedo with "painted" light, normal from height, AO and wear from curvature) tuned by a global palette. Their source image comes either from code, or once from an AI image-generation model, and is stored in the repository with provenance. In both cases it goes through the same deterministic post-processing in code. Geometry, animation and the world remain purely code-generated. **Hard shapes** (buildings, cars, props) are assembled with constructive operations and split grammars, **organic shapes** (rocks, bodies) arise via SDF and surface nets, and **trees** by space colonization. **Characters** are a skeleton from parameters with a body of capsules and motion from a few poses, springs and IK. The **world** is a hierarchy of grids (region, district, cell, scatter) in which each level reads only coarser levels. The hardest, and therefore riskiest, are faces and painterly-looking textures: this is where procedural output most easily gives itself away as "generator noise". Cross-platform determinism is guaranteed only by the CPU with our own maths and a hash RNG. The GPU may therefore generate only purely visual data. Architecturally, the generator adds no new layer. It is **another source provider in the `asset` module** next to loose files and the pack, so `world` cannot tell whether a cell came from disk or from a generator. The milestones are rearranged to nine, and the style is verified against the GTA III and WoW bar on the reference scene in M2 and on the first generated assets in M3.

## Stylized textured 3D, not photorealism or pixels

The rejection of photorealism is confirmed by all precedents found. The city in The Matrix Awakens was assembled by rules in Houdini from **"thousands of modular pieces"** (7,000 buildings on 16 km²). It added face scans, 4D capture and MetaHumans, and the realism is credited to Nanite, Lumen, ray-traced shadows and TSR ([CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/In-Focus/2021/The-Matrix-Awaits-in-The-Matrix-Awakens-An-Unrea.aspx)). Horizon Zero Dawn procedurally *places* all vegetation, but it places hand-made assets ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). Substance Designer can make photorealistic materials purely procedurally, but production mixes in scans, and uninterrupted procedural noise is, according to practitioners, **"a clear giveaway"** ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2261087)). Faces are, according to Epic, **"orders of magnitude harder than realistic environments"** ([Unreal tech blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/tech-blog/creating-realistic-digital-humans-using-ue4-livestream-recap)). From the AAA pipeline, layout, terrain, roads and placement carry over to a stylized target, and in stylization it is easier, because the primitives may be simple.

Small teams choose stylization explicitly because of the cost of content. Astroneer, with two artists, chose a low-poly style because it "removed the need to create textures entirely" and an asset takes "a day or two" ([Astroneer blog](https://blog.astroneer.space/?p=7)). The two-person team behind Sable replaced missing detail with **light, fog and outlines** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Tiny Glade started as a weekend wall generator, and its authors speak of procedural rules as hand-made art ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)). Teardown has almost no textures, one byte per voxel indexes a 256-entry palette ([Voxagon](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/12/03/spraycan.html)). The GTA III and WoW bar is however higher than purely flat low-poly: it requires **textured surfaces with a readable material** (brick, plaster, asphalt, bark, skin, cloth). The recommended style therefore combines both streams. From stylized games it takes strong silhouettes, a controlled palette, fog and light as the main carriers of mood. From the bar it takes textured surfaces, whose stylization (exaggerated edges, baked light and shadow, painterly colour transitions) is more attainable for a generator than photographic detail.

The concrete style chain looks like this. Geometry is of medium density with a strong silhouette: bevelled edges, exaggerated proportions, readable roof and body shapes. Materials are tiling sets generated by operators, in which the albedo carries **baked "painted" light** (darkening of cavities by AO, lightening of edges by curvature, a top-to-bottom light gradient and a hue shift in shadow). It is complemented by a normal derived from height. Lighting is stylized: wrap diffuse through a ramp LUT, hemispheric ambient light, cascaded shadows, generator AO and screen-space AO, and aerial perspective with fog by time of day. Colour coherence is governed by a **global palette and grading through a LUT**, not by manual checking of every asset. Fine outlines are an optional style parameter (Sable considers them a "really useful tool" for readability), not a pillar. The lesson from HD-2D applies here too: the Octopath Traveler team first tried a purely pixel style, which looked cheap, and built the result on modern lighting ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/project-octopath-traveler-developers-answer-project-started-troubles-developing-hd-2d/)). Stylization without good light will not meet the bar.

### Pixel art remains only as a performance switch

3D pixel art (rendering at low resolution with nearest-neighbour upscaling) is an established technique. Without shimmering, however, it works only when the camera snaps to the texel grid, which is possible only under orthographic projection, where all pixels shift equally ([Holland](https://davidholland.netlify.app/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering)). Under perspective, pixels at different depths shift differently and **"no single snap fixes all depths"**. The only proposed procedure, Texel Splatting from March 2026, has an open disocclusion problem and nobody has shipped it in a game ([arXiv 2603.14587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14587)). The owner wants a free perspective camera and the GTA III and WoW bar, so **pixel art as a defining look is rejected**. Only *render scale* remains optional, that is, a lower internal resolution with integer upscaling for weaker GPUs. A Short Hike also offers players an adjustable pixel size ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)). In our engine it is a performance choice, not a style goal. The camera is thus not an open question, and both the generators and the renderer are designed for free perspective.

### Texel density and readability set the generator budget

Native resolution implies a texel density that all generators must respect. At a vertical FOV of 60° and 1080 lines, a metre at distance *d* gets about 935/*d* pixels. At 7 m (camera behind a car) that is roughly 134 px/m, at 10 m 94 px/m and at 20 m 47 px/m. At 1440p a third more. **I recommend a global constant of 128 texels per metre for environments and 256 for vehicles and characters**, which tend to be near the camera. A 512² tiling set thus covers a 4 m wall. Memory follows from this. A set of albedo (BC7) + normal (BC5) + ORM (BC7) at 512² is about 0.75 MB, and with mips roughly 1 MB, so 200 materials fit into approximately 200 MB. At 1024² it would be four times as much. Tile repetition on large surfaces is hidden by macro variation (low-frequency noise in world coordinates tints the albedo) and, for natural materials, by stochastic tiling (see below). Character readability is stricter than with pixel art: **a 1.8 m character has about 170 px at 10 m and a face of about 23 px, and at 3 m in conversation about 75 px**. Faces thus do not vanish in pixels and are among the hardest tasks of the whole project.

The biggest trap of purely procedural content is not solved by stylization alone. Kate Compton described it as "10,000 bowls of oatmeal": every bowl is mathematically unique, but a person perceives them as the same ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2016/09/21/bowls-of-oatmeal-and-text-generation/); [Rabii & Cook, FDG 2023](https://pyrofoux.github.io/why-oatmeal-is-cheap/why_oatmeal_is_cheap_fdg2023.pdf)). Tiny surface variation, which a generator produces for free, does not change the perception of difference. The variation budget therefore belongs in the **silhouette, colour blocks and layout**: roof shape, building mass, palette by district, placement of landmarks. Microdetail only supports the readability of the material.

## Each asset category has its own cheapest generator

There is no universal generator. Tiny Glade puts it directly: **"there is no single universal algorithm"**; each element has its own generator with a pipeline for creation, assembly, morphing and deformation ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)). The following table summarizes the recommendations. The "When" column says whether the generator runs at cook time (archetypes, results are cached and shipped), at streaming time (cells on workers) or in every pixel. The "Bar difficulty" column is my estimate of how hard it is to reach the GTA III and WoW level in the given category.

| Category | Technique | Libraries | When | Bar difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Materials | source: operator graph **or** a one-off AI image with provenance → common post-processing (tileability, palette, height → normal, AO, curvature → wear masks → variants) | own (port of OpenKTG) | cook; macro variation per pixel | medium, painterly look high (an AI source lowers it) |
| Buildings | split grammar (CGA) over oriented boxes, kit pieces as parametric functions, WFC for facades, masks in vertices | own; Manifold only for cutouts | stream (mass), cook (kit) | medium |
| Cars and props | cross-section lofts, lathe, sweep, extrude + bevel, part slots with symmetry, wear from curvature | own half-edge, par_shapes | cook | high for cars |
| Rocks, organics | SDF + smooth-min + fBm, surface nets, triplanar material | own, meshoptimizer | cook and stream | low |
| Vegetation | space colonization, pipe model, branch sweep, leaf cards with generated alpha, impostors | own | cook (library of N seeds per species) | medium |
| Characters | skeleton from parameters → body from capsules (SDF smin) → weights from smin; clothing as offset layers; face as generated texture + blendshapes | own | cook | **highest** |
| Animation | gait phase + springs + two-bone IK/FABRIK + active ragdoll | own, Jolt via joltc | runtime | high |
| Terrain | offline macro height map with erosion + point-wise detail noise + levelling under roads; material splat by slope and height | own | cook (macro), stream (detail) | low |
| Roads, plots | highways offline, streets from a tensor field in the district, blocks from a planar graph, OBB split | own | stream with district cache | low |
| Placement | density dithering against a threshold matrix in world coordinates (HZD pattern) | own | stream | low |

### Materials: operator graph, baked light and palette

Take the operator library from the demoscene. In .kkrieger, Farbrausch stored instead of data **"the individual steps by which artists created textures and meshes"**, and the whole game was 96 KB ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.kkrieger)). All their code is open source under BSD or as public domain. The cleanest reference is OpenKTG from 2007, written "for clarity, not speed", with 16 bits per channel, premultiplied alpha and sampling rules matching D3D10+ and GL. The authors explicitly recommend it as a basis for texture generation in pixel or compute shaders ([fr_public/ktg](https://github.com/farbrausch/fr_public/tree/master/ktg)). Its set (Noise, Cells, ColorRemap, CoordRemap, Derive, Blur, Ternary, Paste, Bump, LinearCombine) ([gentexture.hpp](https://github.com/farbrausch/fr_public/blob/master/ktg/gentexture.hpp)), together with hashed gradient noise with analytic derivatives, fBm, domain warping and Voronoi edges after Quilez ([morenoise](https://iquilezles.org/articles/morenoise/); [warp](https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/); [voronoilines](https://iquilezles.org/articles/voronoilines/)), covers stone, plaster, wood, bricks, rust and cracks. Keep intermediate results in 16 bits and quantize only at the end. Eight bits show banding after several remaps and blurs. The noise hash must be integer, no `sin`-hash, otherwise results differ between APIs.

The order of material layers copies the "smart materials" from Substance, just written in code. The base is a height from fBm, onto which comes structure (Voronoi cells or bricks with variation by cell ID). From the height we derive the normal (central differences scaled by the ratio of height range to texel size), cavities and AO (the difference between the blurred and original height) and curvature (Laplacian). Masks from curvature and AO then drive **worn edges, dirt in cavities and moss on top surfaces**. The research lists these derived maps as standard image processing and found no primary source for them. The painterly look of WoW arises by not using these masks only for PBR but **baking them directly into the albedo**: cavities darker and toward a cool hue, edges lighter and toward a warm one, a mild light gradient from above and colours picked from ramps of the global palette (cosine palettes or a 1D gradient LUT). The result is then lit with a soft stylized ramp that does not override the baked light. This is the riskiest place in the entire material part. A human-painted texture carries deliberate strokes and simplifications, and pure fBm looks like noise. I therefore recommend generating "strokes" explicitly, as elongated Voronoi cells oriented along the main direction of the material with quantization to 3–5 ramp shades, and watching quality with review sheets next to the owner's references. Where the code path does not reach the bar, the AI source described below steps in.

The owner decided that a material may have a second source too: **an image generated once by an AI model**, stored in the repository as a source asset with provenance (model, prompt, seed, date) in a sidecar file next to the image. The hybrid targets exactly the weakest point of the code path, the painterly character, which an operator graph imitates worst. The research collected no sources on AI texture generation, so what follows is a pipeline proposal, not documented results. What matters is that **the AI image is an input, not an output**. The model is not run at build time, because its output cannot be reproduced across versions and platforms. The stored file is therefore as fixed for the pipeline as a hand-painted original, and the hash of its bytes enters the cache key. From it leads the **same deterministic post-processing as from the operator graph**: tiling (offset by half and blending the seams with a mask along the edges, possibly a histogram-preserving blend), remapping into the ramps of the global palette and grading so the AI image does not stand out from the others, deriving height from brightness and from it normals, AO, curvature and roughness, procedural wear and dirt masks, and colour and structure variants by seed. Both paths thus end in the same format and the same lints (brightness range, palette ramps, texel density, repetition on a 3×3 tile) and the rest of the engine does not see the difference. In practice the AI source supplies the painterly "handwriting" and code supplies coherence, building context and variability. The linter also rejects an image without complete provenance. The AI source is not used for geometry, animation or the world.

The cheapest and best masks are made by the generator itself, because it knows the geometry. Tiny Glade makes plaster wear around windows from a **single float attribute in the vertex that carries the signed distance to the window edges**, and the shader combines it with noise ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade)). A wall generator can thus write into the vertices the distance to an opening, distance to a corner, height above the ground and a top-surface flag. From these the shader composes dirt at the ground, smoothed corners, moss on ledges and streaks running down below windows, without unique UVs. A tiling texture thus receives the context of the specific building, and that is the difference between a "wallpaper" and a building in GTA III. For unique baking of AO and curvature on hero assets (landmarks, the player's car), create unique UVs via xatlas, which segments, parametrizes and packs the atlas automatically ([xatlas](https://github.com/ducphamhong/xatlas)). For the rest, AO in vertices is enough.

UVs are generated directly by the building generators (box projection in world units for each face, cylindrical for columns), because they know the face semantics. Triplanar mapping is needed by SDF rocks and terrain slopes. Normals in it must be blended correctly, otherwise they point the wrong way on some sides or are mirrored ([Golus](https://bgolus.medium.com/normal-mapping-for-a-triplanar-shader-10bf39dca05a)). Biplanar saves the third sample at the cost of manual gradients through `SampleGrad` ([Quilez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar)). For terrain, planar projection from above is enough, with triplanar only above a slope threshold. Repetition of natural materials (grass, soil, rock, bark) is removed by the histogram-preserving tiling of Heitz and Neyret, which blends three samples without ghosting and loss of contrast ([Heitz](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/)). It suits only stochastic textures, though, not bricks and tiles ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/procedural-stochastic-texturing-in-unity)). Structured materials are generated instead with variation by the hash of the cell ID. Virtual texturing in the Far Cry 4 style ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1021761/Adaptive-Virtual-Texture-Rendering-in)) does not belong in v1, because terrain with a few splat layers and macro variation meets the GTA III bar without it.

GPU organization is dictated by SDL_GPU: 16 samplers per stage and no bindless ([SDL_sysgpu.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libsdl-org/SDL/main/src/gpu/SDL_sysgpu.h)). Store materials therefore as a **`Texture2DArray` for each combination of format and size** (albedo BC7, normal BC5, ORM BC7, at 512² and 1024²) and material parameters (layer indices, tint, projection scale, palette row) as a storage buffer indexed by material ID. Then 4–6 samplers are enough for hundreds of materials. Do BC compression on the CPU at cook time, because SDL_GPU has no GPU encoder. Verify BC format support on Metal at runtime via `SDL_GPUTextureSupportsFormat`. The palette is a texture of 256 × N rows, as Teardown uses it ([Voxagon](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/12/03/spraycan.html)). Swapping a row recolours a whole district or sets the time of day, and that is exactly variation at the level of colour blocks versus the oatmeal effect.

### Hard shapes are built with operations, organic ones are derived from SDF

Buildings, cars and furniture have sharp edges, exact dimensions and known semantics. Constructive building therefore gives a sharper result, exact UVs and an order of magnitude fewer triangles than SDF meshing. I recommend our own index-based half-edge mesh with the operations extrude, inset, bevel, loft between profiles, sweep of a profile along a curve with rotation-minimizing frames, lathe and mirror. Each is estimated at 100–500 lines of C. A small bevel on all edges is essential for the stylized look, because it catches light and makes the silhouette softer and "painted". Primitives are provided by the single-header `par_shapes.h` (MIT) ([par_shapes.h](https://github.com/prideout/par/blob/master/par_shapes.h)). Use booleans sparingly (door cutouts, damage) via **Manifold with the official C interface `manifoldc`**, which promises a "guaranteed manifold output without exceptions" and carries vertex attributes and material IDs through booleans ([Manifold](https://github.com/elalish/manifold)). Build it with `MANIFOLD_PAR=OFF`, so that a second thread pool does not run and the output order does not depend on TBB. The README does not guarantee bitwise match across platforms, and it must be verified with our own test.

Build buildings with a grammar. CGA shape by Müller et al. works on oriented "scopes" with operations of mass modelling, decomposition into faces, splitting and repetition along axes, and occlusion queries for context-sensitive facades ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1141911.1141931)). In C this means a set of functions `split(axis, sizes)`, `repeat(axis, size)`, `comp(faces)` and `insert(kit_piece)` over boxes. Kit pieces (window frame, cornice, column, awning, shop sign, roof tile) are not models from a DCC but small parametric generators. The density of GTA III is supplied exactly by the kit: shop windows, fire escapes, air conditioners, signs and antennas are dozens of small generators that the grammar distributes according to the district style. Townscaper shows the power of composition: an irregular quadrilateral grid, corner tiles assembled like marching cubes and real-time WFC with module priorities ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). Stålberg used roughly 500 hand-modelled tiles. For us every tile must be code. It is feasible, but it is the largest amount of "artistic" code in the project.

For cars, the research found not a single documented shipped example of fully code-generated vehicles. Publicly known procedures assemble hand-made parts. I therefore recommend a **parametric kitbash**: a parameter structure (wheelbase, track, cabin height, body profile splines, part slots), the body as a loft of cross-sections along the longitudinal axis, wheels and rims as lathe, pipes and trims as sweep, panels as extrude with bevel. Handle panel gaps, window edges and lights with an edge-distance attribute and painted texture, not geometry. The paint receives baked reflections and wear from curvature. Against the oatmeal effect only a hand-designed *program* for each archetype helps (sedan, taxi, van, bus, sports car, police), not one universal generator. Cars are the most closely watched asset in a GTA-like game, and so the second largest art risk, after characters, lies here.

Organic shapes are the domain of SDF. Quilez's catalogue of distance functions and operators is the de facto standard ([Quilez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/distfunctions/)). Games that render SDF directly (Dreams, Claybook, Tiny Glade), however, had to write their own GPU renderer. On a rasterizer over SDL_GPU, SDF is therefore only an *authoring* format and is converted to triangles by meshing. **Naive surface nets** produce fewer than half the faces compared with marching cubes (73,133 quads against 154,168 triangles in the benchmark) and are "about as easy as naive Minecraft meshing" ([0fps](https://0fps.net/2012/07/12/smooth-voxel-terrain-part-2/)). Dual contouring adds sharp edges for the fractured faces of rocks, at the price of QEF solving and possible self-intersections ([Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2018/04/15/dual-contouring-tutorial/)). For LOD seams between chunks, the Transvoxel tables are under MIT ([Lengyel](https://github.com/EricLengyel/Transvoxel)). A rock is thus a rounded box or ellipsoid, a smooth-min union, plane cuts for layers and ridged fBm displacement, meshed through surface nets and textured triplanarly. Its AO and curvature are evaluated directly from the SDF and written into the vertices.

Generate trees by space colonization (Runions et al.), which creates "surprisingly realistic" branching from attractors scattered in the crown envelope, with parameters matching the crown shape ([algorithmicbotany.org](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.html)). Branch radii are computed by the pipe model, the geometry by a sweep of circles shared with buildings, and leaves are provided by cards with a generated alpha texture of leaf clusters. In WoW stylization crowns are more large shaped volumes than individual leaves, so fewer cards with a stronger silhouette suffice. Write the hierarchy level and the wind phase into the vertex colours. Each species has a library of N seeds at cook time, which are instanced.

All geometry post-processing is covered by **meshoptimizer** (MIT, C interface for all algorithms): reindexing, vertex cache, overdraw and fetch optimization, simplification including an attribute-aware variant, meshlets and `clusterlod.h` ([meshoptimizer](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer)). Collision has a big advantage in a code pipeline. The generator knows it built a box or cylinder and **emits analytic collision shapes for free**. Do convex decomposition of complex shapes only at cook time via CoACD, which has `--seed` for reproducibility ([CoACD](https://github.com/SarahWeiii/CoACD)). V-HACD is archived and itself points to CoACD ([V-HACD](https://github.com/kmammou/v-hacd)). Write octahedral impostors for distant trees and buildings yourself as a cook step rendering N views into an atlas; the research found no ready C library.

### Characters are the hardest category: body and motion yes, face with risk

A character is built in the order skeleton → body → weights, and this very order makes automatic skinning a trivial task. A parameter vector (height, limb proportions, shoulder and hip width, mass, age, a seed for small asymmetries) determines the rest positions of the joints. From the same vector come the stride length, the lengths of IK chains, the ragdoll capsules and the segment masses, so the animation fits the body automatically. The body is an SDF union of capsules and ellipsoids with smooth-min, meshed by surface nets and simplified. Each primitive belongs to one bone, and so **the normalized contribution of a primitive in the smooth-min is directly the skinning weight**, with no solver. Clothing arises as offset layers of the body (offset SDF) with their own materials. WoW stylization with exaggerated proportions (larger hands, shoulders, head) helps the generator, because a pronounced proportion is more readable than anatomical detail. For foreign meshes there are two published methods of automatic weights. Pinocchio computes weights by heat diffusion over the surface and automatically rigged 13 of 16 unseen bipeds ([Baran & Popović](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ibaran/papers/2007-SIGGRAPH-Pinocchio.pdf)). Geodesic voxel binding handles even non-manifold meshes interactively ([Dionne & de Lasa](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/15001)). Do not use MakeHuman as a source. Its morph targets are under AGPL, and only the exported fixed models are under CC0 ([MakeHuman docs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/maketarget/targets.html)).

The face is where we need to be honest. At 23–75 px per face a palette is not enough, but we do not need photorealistic anatomy. I recommend a stylized head with a low polygon count and pronounced planes (nose, cheekbones, brow ridges) and a **face texture generated as a 2D drawing**. Eyes, eyebrows, lips, and shadows under the nose and chin are parametric 2D SDF shapes with soft transitions, baked into the albedo as if a human had painted them. Expression is provided by a few procedural blendshapes (blink, smile, frown, jaw opening, a few visemes) as displacement fields with radial falloff around landmarks, plus blinking and saccades. The research found not a single shipped game that fully generates human faces at WoW level. This is therefore the least supported recommendation of the report, and the owner should judge faces first among the characters, on contact sheets of twenty seeds.

Motion builds on the precedent of Overgrowth. At GDC 2014, David Rosen showed "interactive and fluid animations with a very small number of keyframes" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020049/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)). According to secondary sources it is roughly 13 poses in total, spring-like interpolation and leg IK that is "just trigonometry" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation-gdc-video-talk/538228)). These details need to be verified in the video. For us the poses are tables of joint angles generated by code. They are blended according to the gait phase, which advances with speed and stride length. A foot in the stance phase is locked in the world, and in the swing phase it flies to a predicted landing point whose height is determined by a raycast. The pelvis sways at twice the step frequency and leans into acceleration. The result passes through **critically damped springs parametrized by half-life**. Holden gives exact closed-form formulas for them that are stable at any `dt` (`damping = 4·ln2 / halflife`), and inertialization, which replaces the crossfade of two poses ([Holden, Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). Analytic two-bone IK handles legs and arms, FABRIK the spine, neck, tails and chains with multiple end effectors, for example hands on a steering wheel and a gaze on the road ([Aristidou & Lasenby](https://www.andreasaristidou.com/FABRIK)). Handle animals as Spore does, that is, motion described against semantic markers ("foot", "end of spine") and solved for each morphology through IK ([Hecker et al.](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)). Rain World shows that the physical body can consist of a few spheres while the limbs are purely cosmetic ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)). For distant pedestrians this is the ideal LOD. Gestures and specific actions (getting into a car, fighting, phoning) will not meet the GTA III bar with walking alone. Each action is a small program of poses and IK targets, and their number determines how much the animation work costs.

Euphoria, which RAGE has used since GTA IV, has not been licensed since 2017, and nothing is publicly known about its internals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))). The core of "Euphoria-lite" is reproducible in three layers. The base is a kinematic procedural pose. Above it lies a driven ragdoll whose joint motors track the pose and locally go slack on a hit. At the top is a balance layer that takes a step when the centre of mass leaves the support polygon. SIMBICON shows that the main lever of balance is the **placement of the swing foot** according to the position and velocity of the centre of mass ([Yin et al.](https://cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/Simbicon.htm)). Uncharted 4 also builds physics *on top of* animation, not instead of it ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024087/Physics-Animation-in-Uncharted-4)). Jolt has joint motors and `SwingTwistConstraint` for shoulders ([Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)), and the C wrapper joltc exposes `Ragdoll_DriveToPoseUsingMotors` ([JoltC](https://github.com/SecondHalfGames/JoltC); [oxijolt-sys](https://docs.rs/oxijolt-sys/latest/oxijolt_sys/)). This closes the open physics question from the first report: **Jolt via joltc**, because it provides an active ragdoll from pure C. Motion matching and learned motion matching require a mocap database ([Holden et al.](https://www.theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)) and DeepMimic reference clips and RL training ([Peng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.02717v2)), so both are out. Only the surrounding tools carry over: a spring controller with trajectory prediction, inertialization and SoA poses. Design the runtime after ozz-animation (SoA, pure functions) ([ozz](https://github.com/guillaumeblanc/ozz-animation)), but write it in C. Bone matrices are uploaded into a single storage buffer per frame and skinning runs in the vertex shader.

### The world as a hierarchy of grids, where each level reads only coarser ones

Every successful chunked generator separates content by spatial reach. Minecraft has an explicit pipeline of states (structure_starts → structure_references → biomes → terrain → features → light → full) and leaves distant chunks frozen at an early stage as proto-chunks ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)). Community notes give the reach of features as 3×3 chunks and of structures at most 17×17 ([delvr](https://gist.github.com/delvr/bfac43cd48675ec74b905ffe640a6a11)). As early as 2003, Greuter et al. hashed cell coordinates into a city seed ([Greuter](https://svn.sable.mcgill.ca/sable/courses/COMP763/oldpapers/greuter-03-real-time.pdf)). For our engine this yields four levels. **L3 region (4–8 km)** contains coasts, mountains, highways, centres and the district map. **L2 district (~1 km)** has the main roads and the tensor field of streets. **L1 is the streaming cell (128–256 m)** with blocks, plots, buildings and props. **L0 are scatter tiles** for vegetation and small details. One rule applies: **the content of level k may read only levels ≥ k, or the same level within a fixed radius, never a neighbour's finer data.** Because L2 and L3 are pure functions `(world_seed, level, x, y)`, two cells that independently compute the same district get a bitwise identical result, and roads on a cell boundary connect by construction. A work-stealing scheduler can then generate cells in any order.

Split terrain into two parts. Erosion is non-local and iterative, so it belongs in an offline tool over a coarse global height map (4–16 m per texel). The virtual pipe model of Mei et al. computes it quickly on the GPU even for large terrains ([Mei et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/52631892.pdf)). During streaming, point-wise fBm detail is added that does not depend on chunk boundaries. Under roads and plots the height is deterministically levelled according to the signed distance to the road axis and the road height profile computed at L2, so both sides of a cell boundary agree. Ghost Recon Wildlands built terrain and roads in Houdini with a small team, but notes that generation in World Machine **"was not fully deterministic"** ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/landscape-and-material-pipeline-of-ghost-recon-wildlands/)). Determinism must therefore be designed in from the start; you cannot add it to a pipeline afterwards.

Roads have two families of algorithms. Parish and Müller extended L-systems with global goals (connect population density centres) and local constraints (snap to an intersection, shorten a segment near water) ([SIGGRAPH 2001](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=116923); [phiresky](https://phiresky.github.io/procedural-cities/presentation.html)). Growth, however, depends on order, so it belongs offline or at L3 for highways. Chen et al. lead streets as streamlines of a tensor field that the designer edits indirectly ([Chen et al.](https://www.sci.utah.edu/~chengu/street_sig08/street_project.htm)). The field can be evaluated at any point, and therefore suits a district at L2. The junction points between districts are determined by the hash of the shared edge. The faces of the planar graph are blocks, and blocks are divided into plots by a recursive OBB split, which Vanegas et al. list next to the straight skeleton ([Vanegas et al.](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i2pp681-690)). Straight skeleton is numerically fragile, OBB split is local and costs microseconds to milliseconds per block. The building grammar then runs on each plot. WFC suits bounded domains (facade, roof, block furnishing, interior floor plan). Deciding whether a valid solution exists is NP-hard, even though contradictions are "rare in practice" ([WFC](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)). On failure, therefore, retry with `seed+1` at most N times and then use a guaranteed fallback module. A plot is owned by the cell in which its centroid lies.

Take placement from Horizon Zero Dawn: **"fully data-driven, deterministic and locally stable"**, over 500 asset types, over 100,000 objects in a scene and an average of about 250 µs on the GPU ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). Density is an expression over the cell's data channels (height, slope, district ID, road SDF, plot mask, moisture from macro baking). A point arises where the density exceeds a threshold from a dither matrix anchored in *world* coordinates, and is shifted by the hash of its world cell. The result therefore depends neither on cell boundaries nor on order, and editing one place does not reshuffle the rest of the world. Roads and buildings write exclusion masks before vegetation. Large trees suppress undergrowth through a mask with a radius equal to their footprint. The same mechanism places city furniture along pavements (lamps, hydrants, bins, benches), which gives GTA III its density. Landmarks are "stamps" that reserve a polygon at L2 before roads are generated. Generate interiors lazily, only at the door, and leave most buildings as shells, as GTA does.

The hierarchy also solves LOD. A distant cell does not have to go through the whole pipeline. Phase A (graph, plots, footprints) is enough, and from it extruded building mass serves as an HLOD proxy with the average facade colour. Detail is thus **not generated and then simplified, but never created at all**. Persistence is small thanks to determinism. Every generated entity has a stable ID `hash(level, coordinates, subsystem, local index)` and a cell stores only a diff (removed IDs, overridden properties, manually added instances). Designer interventions at higher levels (a moved road, a new stamp) are stored as generator *inputs*, not as diffs. Vanegas et al. show that plot correspondence can be maintained even across an edit of the road network ([EG](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/v31i2pp681-690)).

## Determinism holds only on the CPU and only with our own maths

Cross-platform determinism is not an academic requirement. A save from a Mac must refer on Windows to the same tree and plot IDs, golden tests must hold on all three CI machines, and a cache keyed by inputs must mean the same result. Bruce Dawson summarizes the sources of differences as follows: FPU state per thread, unspecified precision of intermediate results, reordering of `a+b+c` under `/fp:fast`, **FMA contraction, which leaves the product unrounded**, estimate instructions rcp/rsqrt differing by vendor, and transcendental functions, which IEEE does not specify. He recommends using only IEEE operations, turning off contraction, writing your own transcendental functions, and stresses that a one-ULP difference can flip a branch, so epsilons do not fix divergence ([Dawson](https://randomascii.wordpress.com/2013/07/16/floating-point-determinism/)). Fiedler adds practice: Battlezone 2 had sin/cos differences between AMD and Intel, and Shawn Hargreaves's replays broke between Xbox and PC even after a compiler upgrade ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/)). Libm differs between macOS, glibc and MSVC in the last ULP. That is why deterministic physics libraries ship their own polynomial sin/cos ([alice-physics](https://docs.rs/crate/alice-physics/1.2.0/source/src/det_math.rs)). The correctly rounded functions of CORE-MATH are making their way into glibc ([glibc patchwork](https://patchwork.sourceware.org/project/glibc/patch/20250131191844.2582716-14-adhemerval.zanella@linaro.org/)), but neither Apple nor MSVC will adopt them, so you have to vendor them.

The GPU guarantees no bitwise match at all. The Vulkan spec defines the precision of transcendental functions only as error bounds in ULP or absolute ([Vulkan spec](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/1.3/html/chap37.html)), so two conformant drivers may return different bits. Halide therefore turned off bit-exact tests on GPU backends ([Halide #8566](https://gh.r-mj.com/halide/Halide/pull/8566)). GPU placement in HZD was deterministic on a **single** console GPU. For three APIs and three vendors this gives a hard rule. **Authoritative content (heights, graphs, plots, placement, meshes, collision, material textures, anything with an ID or a hash in the cache) is generated on the CPU.** The GPU may produce only derived visual data that is never written back anywhere, that is, grass blades, micro-noise, impostors and review renders. Their cache key contains a backend tag, and they are tested by image comparison with tolerance, not by hash. Material textures are generated on the CPU at cook time. At 512² and dozens of operators per material this is seconds of work per core, and it is cooked only once.

The concrete rules for the C17 code of generators are these. The `gen_*` targets are built with `-ffp-contract=off -fno-fast-math` (clang, gcc) and `/fp:precise` (MSVC). `<math.h>` is forbidden in them and CI checks this like the header rule. Instead we use `det_sin`, `det_exp` and `det_pow`, composed only of +, −, ×, ÷ and sqrt, or vendored CORE-MATH sources. Discrete decisions (counts, branching, topology, intersections in the road graph) are computed with integers or fixed point, and floats serve only for continuous attributes. SIMD intrinsics with estimate instructions are forbidden, and the results of parallel work are written into pre-allocated slots and assembled in index order, never in completion order. The default `-ffp-contract` differs between Apple clang, upstream clang and gcc, so set it explicitly and verify it in CI.

Randomness must be stateless. At GDC 2017, Eiserloh showed that a hash function `(position, seed)` is at the same time a stateless RNG ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024365/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Noise)). Use his later **SquirrelNoise5**, because Peter Schmidt-Nielsen found a weakness in the code from the talk (Squirrel3) ([Squirrel3 notes](https://swiftpackageregistry.com/heckj/Squirrel3)). For high-quality streams, Philox from Random123 is on offer, which passes BigCrush and gives ≥ 2⁶⁴ independent streams ([Salmon et al.](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~hari/teaching/bigdata/random123sc11.pdf)). The seed is derived by the chain `world_seed → hash("tree.oak.bark") → (cell_x, cell_y, lod) → subsystem stream_id → index`. String IDs instead of enums ensure that reordering an enum does not reseed the world. A separate `stream_id` for each subsystem in turn ensures that one extra random number in roads does not reshuffle trees. A float from an integer is obtained with the expression `(u >> 40) * 0x1p-24f`, which gives the same bits everywhere. Output match is verified by a CI job that generates a fixed set of seeds on macOS-ARM64, Linux-x64 and Windows-x64 and compares the hashes. That job belongs in M0, not in the time when there is already something to break.

## An asset is a function: recipe, seed and hash instead of a file

The proven pattern from the demoscene to Houdini cities is the same: **a small vocabulary of operators, store the recipe and not the pixels, regenerate anything at any time and have a short live-editing loop**. The Matrix Awakens regenerated the whole city until the last day ([CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/In-Focus/2021/The-Matrix-Awaits-in-The-Matrix-Awakens-An-Unrea.aspx)), and Horizon puts the rules in a graph editor, so iteration stays fast ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)). A graph editor, however, targets artists. When the generators are written by AI in C, a library of operators (`op_noise`, `op_cells`, `op_blur`, `op_remap`, `op_extrude`, `op_loft`, `op_split`) provides the same composability, while the code stays diffable and greppable. A read-only call-graph visualizer is enough.

The division of work copies the roles from the project decisions. The **program** is a C function in a `gen_*` module, written by Claude, with the stable signature `gen_run(const params*, seed, arena*) → blob`. The **parameters and seed** are a text recipe in `content/` (a simple key-value format in git) that the owner can edit as art director. The **style constants** (palettes and ramps, 128/256 texels per metre, angle and width of edge bevels, strength of baked light, storey height 3.2 m, door height, triangle budgets) live in one shared recipe that all generators read. This is the art bible in the form of code and the main tool of coherence. The loop looks like this: the owner writes a specification and supplies references (screenshots from GTA III, WoW or his own sketches), Claude writes the generator, a tool renders a contact sheet, Claude compares it with the references and fixes it, the owner approves and the output hash is recorded as a golden.

The cache key is a Merkle hash: `H(generator_version ‖ generator_source_hash ‖ canonical_parameters ‖ seed ‖ OUTPUT_hashes_of_dependencies ‖ source_image_hashes ‖ library_versions ‖ [backend])`. Source AI images enter the key by the hash of their bytes, not by the prompt, because regeneration by the model would give a different result. Parameters are serialized in a fixed field order and floats as raw bits. The *outputs* of dependencies are hashed, not the inputs. An edit that does not change the result thus invalidates nothing beneath it (early cutoff in the Bazel style). The versions of meshoptimizer, Manifold and the BC encoder belong in the key, because their upgrade can change the output. A source hash is better than a binary hash, because it is the same on all platforms. The cache lives in `cache/` in the project folder, following the rule of not littering the system.

Hot reload is cheapest for generators in the whole engine, because generators are stateless and their "state" is the cache. In the dev build the `gen_*` modules are built as shared libraries and loaded by the same loader as the game DLL. An alternative is cr.h, a single-header MIT library tested on Linux, macOS and Windows ([fungos/cr](https://www.Github.com/fungos/cr)), which someone has already used for live reloading of a city generator in an editor ([polymonster](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.polymonster.co.uk/blog/live-reloading)). After a source change the watcher rebuilds the module, the source hash changes, the affected records are invalidated and the workers regenerate the visible assets. A recipe change does the same without a rebuild. Node hashing ensures that moving one slider recomputes only a few operators. The goal "a change shows up within a second" is an estimate and nobody has measured it.

Headless review renders are decisive for the AI loop. The tool `gencli render --asset car.sedan --seeds 0..15 --out sheet.png` renders through SDL_GPU into an offscreen texture a contact sheet with the game's stylized lighting. It adds fixed camera setups (front, 3/4, top, close-up and from the distance of a typical camera behind a car), a 45° turntable and debug views (albedo without light, normals, AO and curvature, wear masks, texel density, triangle density, collision shapes). A sheet of 16 seeds side by side is at the same time a test against the oatmeal effect: if they look the same, the generator does not have enough variability in silhouette. The second mandatory view is the **asset next to the owner's reference** at the same scale and light, because the GTA III and WoW bar is judged by comparison, not by description. For materials, a sphere and a cube with a 3×3 tile serve (repetition check), and for world cells a top view with layers (roads, plots, district IDs, scatter density) and a street-level view.

Lints are machine-checkable style rules. They check that albedo lies in the palette ramps and the allowed brightness range, texel density, triangle budget and texture size per archetype, dimension limits from the style constants (doors, storeys, road width, kerb height), degenerate triangles and manifoldness where required, the presence of collision, a monotonic LOD chain and cell generation time. The last lint has a hard basis in speed. At 200 km/h (55.6 m/s) a car crosses a 256 m cell in 4.6 s and a 128 m cell in 2.3 s. A cell, including the prefetch ring, must therefore be generated in the background faster than the car reaches it. Golden tests are twofold: hashes of CPU outputs for a fixed set of `(asset, seed)`, identical on three OSes, and golden images for each backend with a perceptual tolerance, because GPU output will not be bitwise identical. The research did not verify a concrete metric (FLIP, SSIM).

## The generator is another source provider, not a new layer

The architecture from the first report can carry procedural generation without a single new layer, as long as one decision is respected: **the generator is a source of bytes for the `asset` module, just like loose files or the pack**. The `asset` module gets a second port next to `asset_type_handler`, namely `asset_provider` (`can_provide(id)`, `provide(id, arena, job)`). It is implemented by `vfs_pack`, `vfs_loose` and `gen_provider`, which `runtime` registers in priority order. The strategies in `world` ask for the root cell ID `(x, y, lod)` and the resource DB resolves it. In the pack file it finds a hand-edited or cooked cell, in the cache a previously generated cell, otherwise it schedules a generator job. In all cases the result is **the same relocatable blob**: SoA arrays of instances, the cell's unique meshes and references to archetype and material IDs, which are resolved by the same chain. `world` cannot tell where the cell came from, and that is exactly what decision D8 requires.

The L3→L0 hierarchy thus fits into the refcounted resource graph in the Horizon Zero Dawn pattern. An L1 cell is a resource that depends on the L2 district resource, which depends on the L3 region resource. The district thus stays in memory as long as some cell holds it, and is released with the last one. Memoization from Minecraft and protection against double generation come free in the graph that the first report already proposed ("no object is ever loaded twice"). Phases A and B of a cell are two resource types: layout data with a wider radius, and meshes and instances with a narrower one. Designer inputs (landmark stamps, painted masks, district overrides) are ordinary files in `content/world/` and enter the L2 and L3 keys.

The split into cook and runtime copies GTA's split into archetype and instance. **Archetypes are outputs of cook programs**: material sets, car models, libraries of trees with N seeds, building kit pieces, characters, impostors and convex decompositions. They are heavy and partly GPU, so they are generated at build time, cached and shipped in the pack. **Instances and cell layouts are outputs of runtime programs**: street graph, plots, building mass assembled from kit pieces, scatter and terrain detail. They are light, purely CPU and deterministic, and so they are generated in the game at runtime from the seed. A unique building mesh is an archetype owned by the cell and is released with it. The estimate of "units of milliseconds per building" comes from engineering judgement, not from measurement. A cell with 100 buildings on one thread thus takes hundreds of milliseconds, which fits comfortably into a budget of 2.3–4.6 s with a prefetch ring and 8 performance cores. The cell generation time lint must verify this.

Dependencies are held by three rules. First, **generators do not depend on `core`, `gpu`, `world` or `render`**. They are pure functions over an arena that the caller passes in. Parallelism is supplied by `runtime` by launching one job per asset or cell, so the generator itself does not see threads and is trivially testable. Second, neither `asset` nor `world` links `gen_*`. The wiring is done by `runtime` as the composition root, as with the other neighbours under the Bitsquid rule. Third, runtime generation in the shipping build runs on the CPU only. GPU code (impostors, erosion, review renders) lives in `tools/`. C++ libraries (Manifold, meshoptimizer, xatlas, Jolt, CoACD) are behind C headers, as decision D4 requires, and only their translation units need a C++ compiler. The `anim` module from the first report changes content: instead of sampling clips it will contain procedural gait, IK, springs, verlet and ragdoll driving. The `render` module stays with the deferred pipeline after GTA V from the first report, because fill-rate matters again at native resolution and deferred carries stylized lighting well: a G-buffer with albedo, normal and AO, a ramp LUT for diffuse, hemispheric ambient light, CSM, SSAO, fog and aerial perspective, grading through a palette LUT and an optional outline pass. **Render scale** (lower internal resolution with upscaling via `SDL_BlitGPUTexture`) ([SDL_gpu.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libsdl-org/SDL/main/include/SDL3/SDL_gpu.h)) is only a performance switch for weaker GPUs.

## Nine milestones: style is verified in M2, the city appears in M4

The following plan is an implementation plan after the analysis is finished, not an experiment. Each milestone builds a permanent part of the engine and ends in a verifiable state on all three OSes. Three things change compared with the first report. Determinism and golden hashes start in M0, because they cannot be added retroactively. Assets as programs (cache, hot reload, review, lints) and the material pipeline become the core of M3. And the "synthetic city" from M4 is now a real generated city. Characters and animation get their own milestone, because without artists and mocap they are a separate system and the hardest art task.

## Conclusion

The main shift compared with the first report is that procedural generation does not lie *above* the engine but *in its data path*. RAGE's split into archetype and instance turns out to be a natural boundary between cook programs and runtime programs. The refcounted resource graph in turn naturally stores the hierarchy of districts and regions that chunked generators need. Determinism stops being a property of rendering or physics and becomes a build rule for one type of target, enforced by CI. The GTA III and WoW bar changes where the hard work lies. The engine, the world and placement are well supported and low risk. Quality will be decided by materials and characters, where procedural output competes with hand painting and where the only reliable measure is comparison with a reference on a contact sheet.

Three uncertainties remain and all are artistic, not technical. The first are faces and painterly textures: the research found no shipped game that fully generates them at this level, and uninterrupted procedural noise is a known giveaway. The hybrid AI source reduces the risk for textures, but the research did not verify the quality of the post-processing that unifies AI images with the palette. The second are vehicles: no game has yet shipped fully code-generated cars, and it is on them that we will see whether the parametric kitbash is enough. The third is the oatmeal effect at city scale: better noise will not help against it, only hand-designed programs for each archetype and district. For the project budget this means that most of the work will not be consumed by the engine. It will be consumed by the library of generators, which are in fact authored art written in C, and by the review loop between Claude and the owner, which decides quality more than any algorithm in this report.
