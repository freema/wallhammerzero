# Pin down identity, edits and versions first

Wallhammer Zero's procedural core is already ahead of every engine surveyed on the two properties that are hardest to retrofit. Generators are bit-identical across three operating systems, and the content-addressed cache needs no import layer. What the design lacks is the machinery *around* that core, the part that production engines learned the hard way. Five things are missing. Authored objects and rules have no stable identity separate from content hashes. Nothing guards against implicit generator inputs or forgotten version bumps. There is no queryable dependency registry with reverse lookups. The cache has no crash-safety, garbage-collection or bake/pack lifecycle. Hand edits have no defined behaviour when a generator changes underneath them. The last gap matters most, because **no studio or engine publicly documents a solved way to carry hand edits across a generator version change**. Unreal's newest attempt concedes that "procedural instances have no stable identity by default", and No Man's Sky buried player bases twice doing it wrong. Our anchor-keyed delta model is the right base, but it needs explicit rules: input-space anchors, an orphan policy, drift detection, per-cell version stamps, a frozen state and a version-bump diff view. Most of these are cheap if written into ARCHITECTURE.md now and built in M0c, M3a and M7. If they are discovered after saves exist, they get expensive. The rest of this report says what to add, why, and where it lands in M0–M10. It does not restate the parameter schema, recipe trace, presets, contact sheets, reviewer calibration or generator hot reload, which the roadmap already plans.

## The core design already beats Unreal, Unity and Houdini on determinism and derivation

All major engines separate source from derived data and treat the derived side as a disposable cache. Unreal's DDC is "disposable" and rebuildable, looked up from fast to slow tiers and filled on a miss ([Epic: Using DDC](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-derived-data-cache-in-unreal-engine)). Godot writes `.import` sidecars and hides products in `.godot/imported` ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/import_process.html)). Unity 6 keeps SourceAssetDB and ArtifactDB under `Library/` ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/kr/current/Manual/asset-database-contents.html)). Bevy and O3DE run a separate processor that writes a parallel processed tree ([Bevy 0.12](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-12/)). Wallhammer's "recipe + seed → Merkle hash → blob" is a DDC with **no import step at all**: the source is a tiny text recipe in git, so the whole `.meta`/`.import`/`Library` layer simply does not exist. That is a structural simplification, not a missing feature.

On determinism the gap runs the other way. Unreal PCG became production-ready in 5.7 ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/11/12/unreal-engine-5-7-foliage-pcg-and-in-editor-ai/)). Its point seed "is computed from the position" ([Epic: PCG data types](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/procedural-content-generation-framework-data-types-reference-in-unreal-engine)). The GPU path is Beta, and its spawned instances are not persisted and have no collision, navigation or HLOD ([Epic: PCG GPU](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-with-gpu-processing-in-unreal-engine)). Epic makes no cross-platform determinism promise, and practitioners fall back on stable sorts, quantization or server-side generation ([bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-unreal-pcg-determinism-different-results)). Tier A with `detmath`, named RNG streams and the stable-sort rule already in CONVENTIONS ch. 5 exceeds this. So does the anchor-key ID, which survives position jitter. Houdini studios keep results stable by versioning HDAs as `namespace::name::2.0`, letting old and new versions coexist, rather than by determinism ([SideFX](https://sidefx.com/docs/houdini/assets/versioning_systems.html)). Our versioned domains are the same idea, with the stronger guarantee on top.

The node-graph question is also settled in our favour, and the evidence did not change that. The industry trend is hybrid graphs whose nodes are code (UE Custom HLSL, Houdini VEX, Unity's experimental Graph Toolkit in 6.2) ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unity-s-graph-toolkit-experimental-available-today-in-unity-6-2/1664909)). Graphs win on watching intermediate steps and on partial recooks. Code wins on diffs and merges: "you can lock but there's no merge" ([r/gamedev](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/gamedev/comments/17wslpv/programmers_has_visual_scripting_helped_you_in)). The planned recipe trace and per-operator hashing already replace the graph's two real advantages. **Unreal's HiGen, where coarse grids feed finer ones through a one-way cache with radii growing per level, is structurally our L3→L0 hierarchy** ([Epic: generation modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)). The gaps are therefore not in the generation model. They are in pipeline plumbing and in edit survival.

## The asset pipeline lacks identity, an input contract and a cache lifecycle

### Content hashes are cache keys, not identities

The industry has converged on stable random IDs that live next to the source: Unity GUIDs, Godot's `uid://` (extended to `.uid` sidecars in 4.4), and O3DE source UUIDs plus product sub-IDs. Godot explicitly rejected checksums as identity "because identical files still need distinct references". It also rejected a central UID database because of merge conflicts ([Godot 4.4 UID article](https://godotengine.org/article/uid-changes-coming-to-godot-4-4/)). Wallhammer uses the Merkle hash as the cache key, which is correct, but has no identity for **authored** things: a recipe file, a preset, a named landmark building, a mask, or a delta's target rule. Without it, renaming or moving a recipe breaks every reference to it. That is exactly the problem Godot spent several releases fixing. The recommendation is that every recipe, preset and authored world input carries a 64-bit `uid` field inside its own text header. Our formats are our own, so unlike Godot we need no sidecar. References between recipes use the uid, and the path is kept only for display.

The same reasoning applies one level down, to generated objects. CONVENTIONS ch. 4 derives IDs from `rule_name_hash`. The rule name is an authored string, so adding a rule does not renumber old objects, but **renaming or splitting a rule re-keys every object it ever produced, and every save delta pointing at them**. The fix costs nothing now. The hashed string becomes an immutable rule id that no one edits, with a separate display name, plus a rename map in each domain's manifest so that a deliberate split can say which new rule inherits which old IDs.

The Machinery shows how to make dependencies cheap. Its data model treated a reference as a **distinct property type**, so dependencies were "trivially enumerable" ([The Story behind The Truth](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-story-behind-the-truth-designing-a-data-model)). If `eng_types` gets a `ref` field kind (uid plus target type) in M0c, the dependency graph, save-reference validation and a reverse-dependency query all fall out generically. The reverse query ("which archetypes and cells use this material preset?") is the tool the art director needs before approving a change. It is also the basis of the blast-radius check described below.

### Implicit inputs and forgotten version bumps are where every engine leaks

Every engine keys derived data on source hash, builder version, settings, platform and discovered dependencies. The recurring failure is inputs that never make it into the key. Unity documents importers that read global state such as colour space, graphics API and compression settings, and it requires dynamic dependencies to be declared through `AssetImportContext` ([Unity 6: Refreshing](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/assets-and-media/asset-database/refreshing)). It also warns that cached import results "may be non-deterministic" and that a hit can come from a different editor version or machine ([Unity Accelerator](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/accelerator-caching.html)). Version bumps are manual everywhere: a GUID string in Unreal's `GetVersionString()` ([Epic API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Developer/DerivedDataCache/FDerivedDataPluginInterface/GetVersionString?application_version=5.5)), an `int` in Unity, a builder fingerprint in O3DE ([O3DE](https://docs.o3de.org/docs/user-guide/assets/pipeline/source-assets/)). In practice they fail. One UE 5.5 team reports that bumping the texture DDC version did not invalidate cached textures ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/lightmap-artifacts-caused-by-float-precision-difference-between-platforms-in-ue-5-5/2651652)). That report is anecdotal, but it is the typical failure.

Our Merkle tree is inherently transitive. That is a real advantage over Unreal's incremental cook, which in 5.6.1 still missed transitive World Partition dependencies ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/incremental-cook-of-world-partition-world-misses-ofpa-file-dependencies/2834447)). The advantage holds only if no generator reads anything outside its key. Two rules make that enforceable. **First, a generator receives exactly one explicit context struct** (recipe, seed, style, scale, world shape, upstream output hashes) and may not call `eng_cvar`, the clock or the file system. Tier A already forbids most of this, and a link-level lint can forbid the rest. **Second, CI turns "forgot to bump" into a build error.** The golden manifest stores, for every `(generator, recipe, seed)`, both the output hash and the domain version it was produced under. A changed hash with an unchanged version fails, unless the commit carries an explicit "cosmetic, no bump" acceptance logged in `review/`. The same idea serves O3DE's builder fingerprint: every cache entry records its producer (engine commit, generator source hash, platform, compiler), which makes a wrong hit diagnosable rather than mysterious.

### The cache needs crash safety, eviction and a bake command

Bevy's processor keeps a write-ahead log and never exposes a product without its matching meta ([Bevy 0.12](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-12/)). Zen has server-side GC, and the legacy UE local cache is cleaned after 8 days ([Epic: Using DDC](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-derived-data-cache-in-unreal-engine)). Our docs say nothing about either. Every domain bump orphans a generation of cached blobs, and a crash mid-write can leave a truncated blob behind a valid key. Both have simple fixes. Writes go to a temporary file followed by an atomic rename, with the manifest written last. `wzctl cache gc` evicts by last access and by superseded domain version.

Unreal also has explicit lifecycle commands: `-run=DerivedDataCache -fill` pre-fills a cache, and `-DDC=CreatePak` builds a distributable read-only `.ddp`. Epic advises distributing a pak rather than copying a DDC over the internet. Zen adds cooked-data snapshots that can be imported elsewhere ([Epic: Zen](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/zen-storage-server-for-unreal-engine)). M7 currently says "cooker and packs only to the extent needed". That should become two concrete commands. `wzctl bake --region` fills the cache for a region and writes a pack in the D8 pre-baked cell format. `wzctl pack` assembles the cooked archetypes for shipping. One lesson from Unreal is worth a measurement: an incremental cook that skipped 45k of 57k packages still took 24 of 28 minutes because of fixed per-run overhead ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/zen-incremental-cook-has-almost-as-long-a-runtime-as-a-clean-cook/2667839)). The no-op bake time should be measured and budgeted.

Sharing a cache between the MacBook and the Windows PC (D75) is tempting, and Unreal's tiered lookup with backfill (local, then shared, then generate) is the pattern to copy. Unity's warning sets the precondition: a shared cache is only as trustworthy as the determinism of its producers. Our tier A outputs qualify once the M0b/M3a cross-OS golden hashes are green. Anything that is not tier A, such as BC/ASTC encoder output (pending measurement 5) or GPU-baked impostors, must carry a platform tag in its key. Steelmanning the alternative: if generation proves fast enough, a shared cache is unnecessary, and regeneration on each machine is the simpler design. The recommendation is to design the key so sharing is possible and to decide after measurement 13.

## Hand edits are the open problem, so the delta model needs six explicit rules

The industry has not solved edit persistence. The defaults are to bake and freeze, to keep hand content in layers the generator does not own, or to exclude regions with volumes. Houdini Engine outputs belong to the asset actor and can be "overwritten or destroyed on recook until you Bake" ([SideFX: PDG](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/unreal/pdg.html)). Unreal's documented tools are editor dirty modes (Normal, Preview, Load as Preview) and Cleanup/Generate ([Epic API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PCG/EPCGEditorDirtyMode)). A secondary source reports that UE 5.8 (June 2026) added PCG "manual artist edits" stored as a "persistent delta recorded against the generated output". The same source concedes that "procedural instances have no stable identity by default", that a reseed leaves orphans, and that undo and streaming interactions are still being refined ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ue5-8-pcg-manual-artist-edits)). No Epic page confirming the feature was found, so treat it as unverified. Studios that ship at scale treat procedural output as a first pass. Snowdrop's scatter for *Avatar* is "the first pass", after which artists add, remove and move objects, and gameplay areas can be handcrafted without scattering ([Massive](https://www.massive.se/?p=18290)). Wildlands added a "settlement instancer" so towns could be tweaked "without a full re-cook" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-world-building-in-ghost-recon-wildlands/)). The clearest analogue to our model is Frostbite's terrain framework. It is a "non-destructive GPU-based layer compositing system" with **hundreds of interdependent overlays per Battlefield 2042 level**, and it cut a change that used to take weeks to "a couple moments" ([EA Frostbite](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/procedural-terrain-in-ea-sports-pga-tour)).

Games that changed generators under live saves show what failure looks like. No Man's Sky NEXT left bases buried. After Origins, Hello Games had to restore affected planets to their pre-update state in patch 3.01 ([PSFanatic](https://www.psfanatic.com/no-mans-sky-origins-patch-3-01-fixes-buried-floating-bases/)). In 2024 players again observed that "when algorithms change, so too does geography" ([VideoGamer](https://www.videogamer.com/?p=1083764)). Minecraft 1.18 froze old chunks and blended terrain height and biomes at the borders, with known seams and missing features under old chunks ([Minecraft 1.18 pre-release](https://www.minecraft.net/de-de/article/minecraft-1-18-pre-release-1)). Bedrock even kept producing old-version chunks until a world went through an update screen ([MCPE-149436](https://bugs-legacy.mojang.com/browse/MCPE-149436)), which shows that the generator version is stored per world.

D40 and the M7 item "domain classes and migration report" set the direction. Six rules turn that direction into a design. **Rule one: anchors come from input space, never from output order.** An anchor such as "the third window of facade X" re-keys whenever anything upstream changes. Anchors must be cell coordinates, authored spline uids or semantic slots in the rule's own grid, which CONVENTIONS already prefers. A lint should reject anchor keys derived from array indices of upstream output. **Rule two: edits are ordered, non-destructive layers, and they include region-level primitives**, following the Frostbite model. Per-instance deltas are not enough. We also need "keep out" and "hand-authored zone" regions that tell generators to skip an area. These play the role of Unreal's difference-with-volume/spline/Polygon2D inputs and ProtonScatter's negative shapes ([GitHub: scatter](https://github.com/HungryProton/scatter)). They are authored inputs that enter the L2/L3 keys, not deltas. **Rule three: an orphan policy.** A delta whose target vanished is never silently dropped. It stays visible in a studio review queue, where the art director chooses to reattach it (nearest same-archetype within ε), convert it to a free-standing authored object, or delete it.

**Rule four: drift detection.** Every delta also stores its absolute world transform and the terrain height under it. After regeneration, a lint flags any delta whose anchor moved by more than ε. That turns the No Man's Sky buried-base bug into a CI failure. **Rule five: per-cell version stamps and an explicit frozen state.** The world manifest records the domain version each cell was generated under. Cells of a frozen domain stay on their version until they are explicitly migrated. Cells of a free domain regenerate, and a declared border-blending policy covers seams between versions, following Minecraft. "Frozen" (output stored, generator ignored) is a first-class cell or asset state distinct from "edited", because baking is the industry's universal fallback. The cost is that frozen domains need their old generator code to stay buildable. Houdini's `::2.0` coexistence is the precedent ([SideFX](https://sidefx.com/docs/houdini/assets/versioning_systems.html)). The cheapest form is to keep old versions only for frozen domains, as copied source under a versioned folder, because AI-written generators otherwise get rewritten freely. **Rule six: define undo and preview across regeneration.** An edit made against pristine version N must have defined semantics once pristine becomes N+1: it is reapplied, or it becomes an orphan. Studio edits stay transient (Unreal's "Preview" mode) until committed, so dragging a slider never writes deltas.

Two tests make these rules hold. The first is a **local-stability test**. Guerrilla's Horizon placement system listed "deterministic" and "locally stable" as design goals, alongside 500+ asset types and roughly 250 µs of GPU load. Those figures come from a search summary of the slides ([Guerrilla slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). Local stability is what let artists "move mountains" without redressing the world ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)). Our version perturbs one cell's inputs and asserts that output hashes outside the cell plus a margin are unchanged. The second is a **version-bump diff report**. It regenerates the affected cells, shows before and after per cell, counts orphaned and drifted deltas, and requires art-director approval before the new version becomes the default. No studio source describes such a tool, so this is design work we have to do ourselves, not something we can copy.

## Runtime generation stays on the CPU, with bounded solves and an output contract

Runtime generation is proven at AAA scale. Horizon placed all of its vegetation, plus sounds, effects and wildlife, at runtime around the player ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)). Unreal's runtime mode generates around "generation sources" with per-grid radii, a cleanup-radius multiplier for hysteresis, and scheduling weighted by distance and view direction ([Epic: generation modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)). Our streaming tickets weigh distance and speed. Adding **per-level generate/cleanup radii with hysteresis and a view-direction weight** in M7 is cheap and copies a mechanism that is already documented. Unreal's warning about coarse data duplicated into every fine cell argues for one more written rule: every object is owned by exactly one cell, at the level that generated it, and data flows only from coarse to fine.

Unreal's GPU PCG and Horizon's GPU placement point to a deliberate split that ARCHITECTURE.md does not yet state. Tier A generators stay CPU-only, because they own IDs, collision and deltas. A **cosmetic GPU tier** may scatter grass, pebbles and similar clutter at runtime. It is seeded from the tier A cell hash, its objects have no stable IDs, no collision and no deltas, and it is excluded from golden hashes, which is exactly the trade Unreal's GPU spawner makes. This should be stated as a rule before M8 introduces vegetation at scale.

Solve size must be bounded. Wildlands town cooks grew exponentially with settlement area, up to 20 minutes for the largest towns, even on a render farm ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-world-building-in-ghost-recon-wildlands/)). In Townscaper every player edit re-evaluates the connected structure, so latency grows with build size ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). D39 makes player edits generator inputs. Each generator should therefore declare a work budget and a maximum dependency neighbourhood (for example, a player edit re-solves at most the owning L1 cell and its 8 neighbours). That turns measurement 25 into an enforced bound rather than a hope.

The output contract should also prevent the Cities: Skylines II failure. A RenderDoc community analysis found props without LODs (a log pile over 100K vertices seen only in shadows), a parking booth merged into its building so it could not be culled, and shadows taking about 40 ms of an 87.8 ms frame ([Paavo's blog](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)). Colossal Order acknowledged LOD as an issue ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skyline-2-dev-says-yes-our-characters-have-teeth-no-the-characters-teeth-are-not-affecting-performance)). The planned "monotonic LOD chain" lint covers part of this. The contract should also require an impostor beyond a declared distance, an explicit shadow-caster flag, and **separately cullable units**, so that merged props stay individually cullable. CI rejects any archetype that lacks them. A code-generated pipeline can make this failure structurally impossible, which a DCC-based pipeline cannot.

Three production rule models fit generators that are code. Ghost Recon Wildlands builds roads with a terrain-following pathfinder whose costs penalise slope and tight turns and discount existing roads. Designers supply waypoints, a road type and avoid-areas ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-world-building-in-ghost-recon-wildlands/)). That is a natural art-directable complement to the road generator in M3b and the player-drawn roads in M6. Snowdrop's scatter uses **parent-child seeding by radius, per layer**: a tree seeds plants, and a river seeds pebbles that seed flowers ([Massive](https://www.massive.se/?p=18290)). It is compact and deterministic if evaluated in fixed order per cell. Epic's City Sample turned an offline Houdini city into a point cloud plus ZoneGraph lanes, along which Mass spawns crowds and traffic. Users later could not reproduce the traffic without that Houdini pipeline ([Epic: City Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/city-sample-project-unreal-engine-demonstration)). The lesson is that **the road generator itself should emit the lane graph** that `eng_sim` mass entities consume, so traffic never depends on a separate authoring step.

## Iteration should take seconds, and generator commits need a blast-radius check

Iteration latency is the 2024–2026 differentiator, more than any new algorithm. Snowdrop graph changes appear in the running game "within seconds" ([Massive](https://www.massive.se/?p=18290)). Frostbite moved a terrain change from an export/re-import round trip to a live edit. Embark's Building Creator, a set of single-purpose Houdini "Feature Nodes" over a shared blockout, takes **4–6 minutes from blockout to fractured asset** ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/how-embark-studios-built-procedural-environments-for-the-finals-using-houdini)). Wildlands results came back from a render farm "within minutes". A code-only engine with no DCC round trip can beat all of these, and that is our main advantage over Houdini pipelines. Unreal shows the other side: artists switch off a volume's "Regenerate" toggle during landscape edits because live regeneration is disruptive ([UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-stop-pause-pcg-generation-when-editing-landscape/1185254/10)). The studio needs a pause-regeneration switch and region-limited regeneration from M3a. It also needs a "preview quality" parameter for slider dragging, which is excluded from golden hashes and from cache entries meant to persist.

The second risk is specific to AI-written generators. BlenderGym (CVPR 2025) found that top vision-language models make **irrelevant code changes** and miss subtle visual differences on edits that are easy for humans ([arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01786)). Procedura's agent applies "one fix at a time" behind compile and connectivity gates ([papers.cool](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2608.26238)). A generator edit meant for asset A can therefore silently change B, C and D. The roadmap's before/after gallery shows what changed. It does not show what changed *outside the intended scope*. Every generator commit should declare its scope (families, domains). CI then lists all golden-hash changes outside that scope and blocks the merge until they are accepted or reverted. The reverse-dependency query from the `ref` field kind supplies the expected set.

Two smaller additions close the loop. Infinigen Indoors separates *what* (declarative constraints in a DSL) from *how* (a solver) ([arXiv 2406.11824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11824)). That supports having one style/constraint spec that both generators and lints read, so style is never encoded twice and the two never drift apart. LL3M cut execution errors with retrieval over version-specific Blender API docs ([arXiv 2508.08228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08228)). Our equivalent is a generator-API reference generated from the `eng_types` schemas and handed to the agent, so the agent never works from a stale copy.

## Proposed changes to ARCHITECTURE.md and ROADMAP.md

ARCHITECTURE.md should gain one new section, "Asset identity, cache and edits", that states the rules below in normative form. It should also get two edits to existing text. The `eng_asset` row adds "uid registry, reverse dependencies, cache GC, bake/pack". "What is deliberately not done" gains "a node-graph editor (recipe trace and parameter panels replace it)" and a clarification that recipe and data reload is not engine hot reload. The determinism-tiers rule gains the cosmetic GPU tier.

Two new measurements belong in the ROADMAP table. **Measurement 29 (M7)** is the delta survival rate after a domain bump on a heavily edited cell: the share of deltas that reattach exactly, drift or become orphans. It decides the default orphan policy. **Measurement 30 (M7)** is no-op and one-recipe-change bake time for a 1 km region, which decides whether shipping needs packs at all. The rule-id change amends CONVENTIONS ch. 4 and should be logged in DECISIONS.md. The owner must decide three things before M7: the default orphan policy, whether frozen domains keep copies of old generator source, and whether the two development machines share a cache.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes where the risk sits. Wallhammer's ambitious parts (bit-exact generators, regeneration instead of storage, code instead of graphs) are its safest parts, because they rest on rules that can be checked. The fragile parts are the conventional ones every shipped engine had to retrofit: identity, key completeness, cache lifecycle and edit survival. Retrofitting them after saves and delta files exist is how No Man's Sky ended up restoring planets. Most of the fixes are one field or one CI rule each, if they land in M0c and M3a.

The one problem no source has solved, carrying hand edits across a generator version change, is also where a code-first, deterministic engine has a structural edge. Regenerating and diffing the world under two generator versions is exact for us and approximate for everyone else. If the version-bump diff report and measurement 29 are built in M7, Wallhammer would have a tool that, on the public evidence gathered here, no studio has described.
