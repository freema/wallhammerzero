# Regenerate the world from a seed, store only the differences

No Man's Sky stores nothing of its universe except the player's traces: terrain edits, bases and discoveries. Everything else is recomputed from the top down on every visit. The chain starts with a 64-bit positional seed. It continues with a data preset for the terrain, whose parameters the seed interpolates between Min and Max values. It ends with a stateless density function that fills voxels in an octree of 32 m regions ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y); [MBINCompiler](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler)). Streaming at runtime never blocks a frame. Coarse LOD is filled in first, then the detail in front of the camera, and areas that arrive late are blended in by dithering. The most time is not spent on noise but on **physics meshes and navmeshes** (same source). The weak point of NMS is persistence. Terrain edits live in one global bounded buffer and old records silently disappear from it, so the terrain "grows back" ([gamepretty](https://gamepretty.com/no-mans-sky-how-to-clear-all-terrain-edits-using-the-save-editor/)). After years of fixes, Hello Games handles generator changes by **freezing the old terrain and letting the new generator run only in a new space of seeds** ([Worlds Part II](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-ii-update/)). Light No Fire is meant to be one shared Earth-sized planet on the same engine. Technically, though, we know nothing about it as of November 2026 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2719590/Light_No_Fire/)). What NMS did not publish is filled in by other games. Minecraft adds an explicit chunk state machine with neighbour radii and tickets, Luanti a unified "emerge" API with bounded queues, Unreal PCG hysteresis and a cascade of grids, and Horizon Zero Dawn regeneration of dense scatter without storing it. For Wallhammer Zero this gives the following proposal. The **world shape port** will have two implementations: a flat grid and a cube-sphere with six quadtrees of height maps, to which local voxel volumes can optionally be added. Authoritative positions will be in doubles, cell data in floats relative to the cell frame. A cell passes through **six states** driven by **tickets**, including a predictive ticket for a car at 200 km/h. Stable IDs will be derived from the object's anchor, not from its order. Generator versions will be kept separately for each domain, and deltas will be stored per cell with no global cap. The flat implementation belongs in M4, the sphere in a new milestone M9. The API and the coordinates must account for the sphere from M0, though. Otherwise the assumption "Y is up" spreads through the whole code.

## Hello Games stores only player traces and recomputes the rest of the universe

### The seed flows top down and every intermediate stage can be stored

In his GDC 2017 talk, Innes McKendrick described generation as strictly cascading: "we always feed data to one generator, get more data, and that feeds the next generator". The cascade starts with the **positional seed** of the solar system. It determines the system parameters (distance of the planets from the sun, sky, atmosphere). The system parameters together with the planet's positional seed give the planet parameters (rocky, mountainous, with oceans), and these feed the terrain generator, whose output is voxels ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). The next sentence is the most important one for architecture. The data between generators is "mostly XML that we can write out and save". The team could therefore enter the chain at any level, load future data and save old planets for performance regression tests (same source). In the game binary these intermediate stages show up as structs. `GcSeed` is a `u64 Seed` with a flag, `GcPlanetGenerationInputData` carries the seed, biome, star class and planet index, and `GcPlanetGenerationIntermediateData` holds the selected terrain preset index, the terrain control weights and the creature role tables. Leaf objects have their own seeds too, for example the placement rules and creature roles, so the seed keeps splitting by type and layer ([GcSeed](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler/blob/development/libMBIN/Source/NMS/BaseTypes/GcSeed.cs); [GcPlanetGenerationInputData](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler/blob/development/libMBIN/Source/NMS/GameComponents/GcPlanetGenerationInputData.cs)).

The address of a place in the universe is a hierarchical integer. The community reconstructed a **48-bit galactic address** from it: 4 bits for the planet, 12 bits for the system index, and 8 + 12 + 12 bits for the region coordinates in a 4096 × 256 × 4096 grid per galaxy ([nms-core](https://docs.rs/nms-core/latest/nms_core/address/struct.GalacticAddress.html); [NMS Wiki](https://nomanssky.miraheze.org/wiki/Galactic_Coordinates)). The seed originally had 32 bits. Hello Games moved to 64 bits to show scalability ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_No_Man%27s_Sky)). The exact hash function that turns an address into a seed has not been published. But it is exactly the `generator(seed, cell)` pattern from our second report, only applied at galaxy scale: a discrete cell ID, a seed from it, and continuous coordinates only inside the active system. On disk this shows up as follows. According to Sean Murray, the 2 GB install was "mostly audio", and everything visible in the screenshots was generated from about **300 MB** of data ([Murray, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9RyEiEzMiU)).

### A planet is a point in a box of Min/Max parameters

Planet terrain is not code but a **data preset**. The file `VOXELGENERATORSETTINGS.MBIN` contains an array of items. Each item has two copies of the `TkVoxelGeneratorData` struct, Min and Max, and the planet picks a preset index and uses the seed to interpolate every numeric field between them ([Step Mods](https://stepmodifications.org/wiki/NoMansSky:Tutorials/Terrain_Generation)). The struct contains 8 layers of uber noise, 9 grid layers (pillars, floating islands and resource deposits), 7 shape features (rivers, craters, arches, blobs), 1 cave layer and scalars such as sea level, beach height and terrain smoothing under buildings ([TkVoxelGeneratorData](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler/blob/development/libMBIN/Source/NMS/Toolkit/TkVoxelGeneratorData.cs)). Each biome then adds, through `GcTerrainControls`, the weights of the individual layers of the chosen preset ([GcTerrainControls](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler/blob/development/libMBIN/Source/NMS/GameComponents/GcTerrainControls.cs)). A planet type is thus a box in parameter space and the seed picks a point in it. The development over time confirms this: in 2021 the array contained **10 presets and 7 noise layers**, in 2026 already **31 presets and 8 layers** (a low-frequency `Continent` layer was added, which splits continents and oceans) ([Step Mods](https://stepmodifications.org/wiki/NoMansSky:Tutorials/Terrain_Generation); [NoMansTerrain](https://github.com/gistya/NoMansTerrain)). Hello Games widened the variety mainly by adding data and changed the code less. According to a report from the time, the terrain generator had only about **1,400 lines** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_No_Man%27s_Sky)).

For our engine this is a pattern that fits both reference scenarios from D15. The type of a district in a GTA-like city ("downtown", "suburb", "harbour") and the biome of a Light No Fire style planet are both a preset with Min/Max ranges in a text recipe in `content/world/`. The seed of a cell or region picks a point inside the range. The art director tunes the box, not the code, and this matches the split "Claude writes the program, the owner edits parameters and seed" from the previous report. The `MaximumLOD` field of each NMS layer also shows a cheap trick and its price: fine layers are not evaluated at all on coarse LODs, which saves time but is a known source of "popping" ([NoMansTerrain](https://github.com/gistya/NoMansTerrain)).

### Uber noise replaces neighbours with a derivative

Murray described his terrain function as "Uber Noise", a "unified method of noise generation". It is fBm in which each octave's behaviour is driven by further noise: sharpness between billow and ridge, erosion by slope, damping by altitude, domain warping and emphasis on individual octaves. The function returns an **analytical derivative**, so that its output can feed further layers ([Murray, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9RyEiEzMiU)). He arrived at this form after three to four years of failures. A Minecraft clone gave only "piles and pyramids" after polygonization. Dense Perlin was repetitive and had constant slope. Noise trained on real height data looked faithful, but "the real world is terribly boring" (same source). He stated the main constraint directly: a voxel **must never read neighbouring voxels**, because that chains in a cascade and the problem becomes unsolvable. The substitute for context is the derivative, which lets the generator know it is on a slope, on flat ground, or that the terrain is rising. Erosion is therefore not modelled as a process but as an **end state** ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). The `TkNoiseUberData` fields in the binary match Murray's list one to one (`SlopeErosion`, `AltitudeErosion`, `PerturbFeatures`, `SharpToRoundFeatures`, `AmplifyFeatures`) ([TkNoiseUberData](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler/blob/development/libMBIN/Source/NMS/Toolkit/TkNoiseUberData.cs)). The closest publicly described maths is the derivative-driven "swiss" and "jordan" turbulence of Giliam de Carpentier. It uses derivatives to damp detail in valleys and domain warping to steer the gullies ([de Carpentier](https://www.decarpentier.nl/scape-procedural-extensions)). The claim that he worked directly with Hello Games on uber noise is only community lore and could not be verified.

The transfer to `gen_core` is straightforward. Every noise primitive returns a value and a gradient. The control parameters are themselves low-frequency noise. Following Murray's advice, the first octave has low emphasis, because it is the one that creates repetitive rows of mountains ([Murray, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9RyEiEzMiU)). The "no neighbours" rule is the same rule that our L3–L0 hierarchy already requires because cells can be generated in any order.

### Ten years of updates taught Hello Games to version the generator

The history of NMS is a natural experiment on what happens when you change a world generator that players have already edited. It went through three phases. In **Foundation (November 2016)** Hello Games "rebuilt the universe" and had to move players to a safe place ([Murray, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9RyEiEzMiU)). **Atlas Rises (2017)** regenerated the galaxy again and "relocated player bases to match the generation changes" ([Atlas Rises](https://www.nomanssky.com/atlas-rises-update/)). **NEXT (2018)** introduced a new terrain generation system ([NEXT notes](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/07/24/here-are-the-no-mans-sky-next-1-5-patch-notes-out-now/)). Since **Origins (2020)** a different rule applies. New planets appeared in existing systems, "the terrain of existing planets was not affected and bases were not reset", and flora, colours and lighting were regenerated so that "no existing planet will change its basic type or hazard". Creature generation, by contrast, was reset, and creatures discovered earlier were marked "extinct" in the catalogue ([Origins](https://www.nomanssky.com/origins-update/)). **Worlds Part II (2025)** took this strategy to its end. The new terrain algorithm with deeper oceans was used **only in a new class of purple stars**, which are "extra stars that expand the universe without regenerating existing planets" ([Worlds Part II](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-ii-update/)). A summary on Shacknews claims the opposite, but the official patch notes take precedence.

The lesson can be stated as a rule that NMS never wrote down but has followed in practice for ten years. Generated domains fall into two classes. **Frozen** domains are those that player work is anchored to: terrain shape, basic planet type and hazards. They do not change after release, and a new generator gets only a new space of seeds. **Free** domains are cosmetics and population: colours, flora, clouds, creatures. They may be updated and the player puts up with it. The difference between Origins and Worlds II shows that even Hello Games did not hold the boundary perfectly. Worlds I announced a "complete universe refresh" with new biomes ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/07/22/a-complete-universe-refresh-awaits-no-mans-sky-players-in-worlds-part-i/)), yet at the same time assured that "the base terrain shapes were not reset and bases will not move" ([Worlds Part I](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-i-update/)). I propose to adopt this pattern for Wallhammer Zero explicitly (see the proposal below). It should not be an emergency reaction after the first broken save.

### Light No Fire: an Earth-sized planet without a single technical detail

Officially we know only this. Light No Fire is a game of adventure, building, survival and exploration "on an Earth-sized fantasy planet". Players build "persistent buildings and communities" in it, ride wild animals, fly on dragons and sail across oceans and rivers. The release date is "to be announced" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2719590/Light_No_Fire/); [lightnofire.com](https://www.lightnofire.com/)). The game runs on the same internal engine as NMS ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_No_Fire)). According to Hello Games, the crewed ship technology from NMS Voyagers (2025) is "shared with our next game… a shared Earth-sized planet with real oceans that require large ships and crews" ([Voyagers](https://www.nomanssky.com/2025/08/voyagers/)). In January 2025 Murray wrote that he had been "working on a new terrain system for some time", with huge mountains and oceans several kilometres deep ([Worlds Part II announcement](https://www.nomanssky.com/2025/01/no-mans-sky-worlds-part-ii)). In August 2026 he said of LNF that people "don't realise how ambitious and crazy it is", and that he is deliberately not showing it ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/fans-were-originally-supposed-to-have-seen-much-more-of-light-no-fire-by-now-but-sean-murray-is-just-having-too-much-fun-working-on-it-i-dont-think-people-realize-just-how-ambitious-and-crazy-it-2000722957); [MassivelyOP](https://massivelyop.com/2026/08/11/sean-murray-says-hello-games-is-holding-back-light-no-fire-reveals-until-absolutely-necessary/)). No talk, paper or technical ad exists.

Two things can still be drawn from the numbers (my own reasoning, not a source). First, the 2017 NMS scheme, a 128 m voxel shell above a height offset of roughly 1 km, is not enough for mountains "taller than Everest" and kilometre-deep oceans. The "new terrain system" from Worlds II with the `Continent` layer is therefore the most likely public glimpse of LNF terrain. Second, a shared persistent planet means authoritative server storage of deltas at planetary scale, which NMS with its local edit lists does not solve. For us this means that **LNF is a bar for scope, not an implementation pattern**. We must take implementation patterns from NMS 2017, from open engines and from the planet literature.

## NMS runtime: a thin voxel shell, six LODs and blending instead of waiting

### Simulation on a sphere, data on a cube

Hello Games tried three planet geometries. The first prototype was an infinite plane that a shader wrapped into a sphere on departure, with the poles as far from the player as possible. The team rejected it because of convergence at the poles, "floating coordinates", places on the plane that cannot be mapped onto a sphere (and that broke shared locations for network features), and precision. It rejected cartographic projections as expensive or hard to invert. The shipped game therefore **simulates directly on the sphere** in continuous 3D space of the whole solar system, and "up" is the normalized vector from the planet centre ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). McKendrick did not hide the cost: the impact showed up "in almost every area of the code", including third-party libraries that assume a fixed up axis. Murray put it more briefly: "you're never in float precision… your Y is no longer up" ([Murray, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9RyEiEzMiU)).

Voxels are stored on a cube. The face is determined by the largest absolute component of the direction, the conversion is "really cheap", and generation happens in sphere space, so the cube distortion changes only voxel density, not shape. The team rejected simulating directly on the cube because of "literal edge cases in the world". Distortion grows with distance from the surface, which is why the voxel shell is only **about 128 m** thick. Tall mountains and deep oceans come from noise inside the transform, which changes the sphere radius **by 600 m to 1 km** ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). At the nearest LOD a voxel is **1 m³** and a region is **32 × 32 × 32 m**. Because of the border and overlap, a window of about **36³ voxels** is polygonized. A voxel takes about **6 bytes**: 2 B density, 2 B two materials and 2 B their blend. There are **6 LODs**, each twice as large as the previous one, and one octree for the whole system that switches between planets (same source). By my own calculation this gives about 280 KB per region at the nearest LOD and a coverage of 1,024 m per region at the coarsest LOD. That is why there is a special sphere "two voxels tall" with six polygonized faces, which every planet in the system has and which is visible from anywhere (same source). NMS does not solve seams between LODs by stitching. Each region is polygonized a bit past its edge, and the overlap covers the holes. The author of godot_voxel summarized from the talk that a lower isolevel is used for this, and reported that in his own renderer it led to z-fighting ([godot_voxel #24](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/issues/24)). The mesher started as marching cubes, then moved to dual contouring for sharp corners ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). In 2024 it was replaced by **dual marching cubes**, which according to the patch notes reduced the vertex count, sped up generation and saved a "significant amount of memory" ([Worlds Part I](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-i-update/)).

### A 32 m region goes through six phases and physics costs the most

Each region goes through the chain **generate → polygonize → spherically bend → physics mesh → navmesh → populate**. There are two main threads, update and graphics, synchronized once per frame. Everything else is jobs, and the main thread is touched only where necessary: when acquiring a vertex buffer, inserting physics, and adding creatures and models. On PS4 some jobs, explicitly object placement, ran as compute shaders ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). The key remark on cost for our planning: physics and navigation meshes "take up a significantly larger part of the time than terrain generation or polygonization themselves" (same source). The job order is purely visual. Low LOD is filled in quickly first, then the detail, with things in front of the camera going first and work proceeding outward from the camera. Fast flight is not handled by blocking but by **blending the region in with time-driven dithering** as soon as it is ready. Trees placed on coarse terrain move to the new surface when the fine LOD arrives, and impostors blend with models. McKendrick himself called it "a bit cheap, but much better than popping" (same source).

NMS places game content that must be visible from space differently from vegetation. Buildings stand on a **shifted grid** (one shifted point per grid cell), so for any point on the sphere the nearest building can be computed without generating terrain. Paths for creatures are generated in advance but instanced only up close. Thanks to determinism, creatures stand in the same places after you return (same source). In the placement scheme this corresponds to two primitives. The first is noise with a threshold and density masks, where density is specified separately for flat ground and for slopes. The second is a shifted grid for everything that must be findable globally ([GcObjectSpawnData via MBINCompiler](https://github.com/monkeyman192/MBINCompiler)). A GTA-like city needs the same (petrol stations, quests, map markers), and so does an LNF-style planet (villages, fortresses).

Newer data comes only from patch notes and from developers on social networks. In 2024 the rendering of trees, rocks and grass moved to a "more modern GPU-based system" ([Worlds Part I](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-i-update/)). Engine programmer Martin Griffiths showed on PS5 a visit to three planets in about 50 seconds with no loading screens ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/3-planets-in-under-a-minute-a-closer-look-at-the-power-of-no-man-s-sky-s-engine)). Worlds II reports up to 4× faster loading and "better caching of data that reduces hitches on transitions" ([Worlds Part II](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-ii-update/)). It does not say whether generated data is cached or only asset I/O. In 2017 voxels lived in memory uncompressed so that they could be edited and queried quickly, and the team admitted that discarding and compression would be possible, they just had not done it yet ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). Memory is still the tightest limit for Hello Games today. In 2026 Griffiths spoke of "impossible memory constraints" on the Switch and Steam Deck ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/no-mans-sky-dev-impossible-memory-constraints-switch-2-steam-deck-updates-take-longer/)).

### A bounded edit buffer makes terrain "grow back"

The community reconstructed NMS persistence from save files. `TerrainEditData` contains six arrays: `GalacticAddresses`, `BufferSizes`, `BufferAges`, `BufferAnchors`, `BufferProtected` and `Edits`. Edits are thus keyed by galactic address and have an age, an anchor and a protection flag. The quoted cap is **30,000 edits per save**, and once it is full, edits are reverted by age and protection ([gamepretty](https://gamepretty.com/no-mans-sky-how-to-clear-all-terrain-edits-using-the-save-editor/)). The exact number is unverified and differs by version. Repeated fixes in the patch notes show where the model fails. Right after the release of NEXT (2018), a bug was fixed where "edited terrain areas were repeatedly and needlessly regenerated" ([NEXT notes](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/07/24/here-are-the-no-mans-sky-next-1-5-patch-notes-out-now/)). In version 3.73 (2021), protected edits inside a base were sometimes overwritten when many edits from other players' bases were downloaded ([mp1st](https://mp1st.com/news/no-mans-sky-update-3-73-patch-notes-revisits-expeditions-this-nov-24)). Foreign edits thus fall into the same bounded buffer and can push out your own. The save is a sequence of LZ4 blocks with JSON with masked keys ([nms-save](https://docs.rs/nms-save/0.2.0/src/nms_save/lib.rs.html)).

A likely model can be inferred from the field names (this is inference, not documentation). A region is regenerated deterministically, all edits that touch it are replayed onto it in order, and then it is polygonized. All three known pains come from this. Edits are counted relatively, so a generator change shifts the terrain under them. Because of the global cap, edits then silently disappear. And because foreign and own edits sit in one buffer, they fight for space. Wallhammer Zero should adopt the "regenerate and replay deltas" model, but not the global cap or the silent deletion.

## Minecraft, Luanti and Unreal fill in what NMS did not publish

For its scheduler NMS published only the principle "coarse first, in front of the camera first". It did not describe an explicit state machine, budgets, cancelling of stale work or version persistence. These gaps are filled by titles whose internals are documented or decompiled.

### A chunk state machine with explicit neighbour radii

Minecraft generates a chunk as a state machine. It goes through the steps structure_starts → structure_references → biomes → terrain → features → initialize_light → light → spawn → full. Unfinished chunks are **proto-chunks**, which may stop at an early stage and continue when the player gets closer ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/World_generation)). Since version 1.21 the dependency of each step is an explicit data structure, `ChunkPyramid`, with radii. FEATURES needs CARVERS within radius 1 and may write blocks into neighbours (`blockStateWriteRadius(1)`). All steps from STRUCTURE_REFERENCES onward need STRUCTURE_STARTS within radius **8**. There is also a separate **loading pyramid**, in which a chunk read from disk needs neighbours only for light ([ChunkPyramid, MC 1.21.11](https://github.com/RecaniteButOnGit/Minecraft-1.21.11/blob/main/server/world/level/chunk/status/ChunkPyramid.java)). By my own calculation, a ring of 10–11 partially generated chunks therefore lies around every fully generated chunk. Writing features into neighbours has a price: C2ME states in its README that vanilla generation "differs significantly between runs with the same seed" ([C2ME](https://github.com/RelativityMC/C2ME-fabric)). The community rewrite of lighting, Starlight, in turn shows why stateless steps scale. According to its authors, the stateless lighting design allowed the chunk system to scale across 10 workers ([Starlight](https://github.com/PaperMC/Starlight)).

Our rule from the second report ("level k reads only levels ≥ k, or the same level within a fixed radius, never a neighbour's finer data") is stricter than Minecraft and therefore better. Nobody writes into a neighbour, so the result does not depend on order. From Minecraft we will still take two things: an **explicit table of step dependencies** and a **separate loading pyramid**. A cell from the cache or from a pack needs no ring of neighbours.

### Tickets: priority as a distance field

Minecraft does not decide on loading with a list of rings but with **tickets**. A ticket has a level, a radius and a lifetime. The level propagates from the source and grows by 1 with each chunk, and the lowest level always wins. Level ≤ 31 means full entity simulation, 32 block ticks, 33 border (accessible without simulation) and ≥ 34 inaccessible, but generation still runs there. Above 44 a chunk is released immediately ([Minecraft Wiki: Chunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk)). The ticket types show how non-player sources enter the field: portal (level 30, radius 3, 15 s), forced loading (persisted), ender pearl (2 s, renewed in flight, so it preloads the landing spot) and the "unknown" ticket, which lives for one tick and appears when data is needed before the chunk reaches the required step (same source). Unreal PCG reaches the same shape in different words during runtime generation. Components are generated around "generation sources" (the player, the World Partition streaming source, custom components, the editor viewport). Each grid size has its own radius, a larger grid a larger one, and cleanup happens only at the radius multiplied by the **Cleanup Radius Multiplier**, that is, with hysteresis. The default scheduler orders by distance and view direction, generates **16 components** in parallel, pools **100 partition actors**, and data flows only from larger grids to smaller ones ([UE PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)).

For our engine, the ticket is exactly the "reason why something is loaded" that the first report took from Horizon Zero Dawn as a strategy. The ticket is just more concrete and easier to compose. The player, the predicted position of a car, a teleport target, a script, the studio viewport (D18) and the debug server (D17) all add the same kind of object, and the cell takes the minimum of them.

### Emerge API, bounded queues and the work-unit size trap

Luanti (Minetest) has the cleanest public API for "give me a cell, no matter where it is". `core.emerge_area` queues blocks that are "asynchronously taken from memory, loaded from disk, or generated if they do not exist". The callback returns `EMERGE_FROM_MEMORY`, `FROM_DISK`, `GENERATED`, `CANCELLED` or `ERRORED`, and cancellation is an ordinary, non-error result ([Luanti lua_api.md](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/doc/lua_api.md)). The queues have caps: 1,200 blocks in total and 150 generated plus 150 disk-only per player. Generation thus does not starve disk reads, and vice versa ([Luanti settingtypes.txt](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/builtin/settingtypes.txt)). Luanti also shows a trap. The generation unit (mapchunk 80³) is larger than the storage unit (mapblock 16³), and the `chunksize` setting warns that reducing it "increases the density of caves and dungeons" (same source). The size of the work unit thus leaks into the output. A second warning says the default generators have "known bugs when used with more than one thread" (same source). For us this yields two lint requirements: generator output must not depend on the cell size or on the number of workers. The second already has milestone M1 ("same hash with 1 and 12 workers"), the first is missing from the plan.

Vintage Story has the same hierarchy as our L3–L0. A region of 16 × 16 columns carries 2D weather and maps for generation, and world-generation passes are labelled by whether they need neighbours ([VS wiki](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/index.php/Modding:WorldGen_Concept)). In 2014 Space Engineers described the canonical approach "don't store what you can regenerate". A procedural asteroid stores only the seed, position and changes, unchanged voxels are discarded after being inserted into physics, and on return the "exact same shape" is created. Distant asteroids ask for "coarse, faster generation", and the world is split into **physics clusters of at least 20 km with 64-bit object positions and 32-bit Havok physics** ([Marek Rosa, 2014](https://blog.marekrosa.org/2014/12/space-engineers-super-large-worlds_17/)). Horizon Zero Dawn regenerates all the nature around the player on the GPU: over 500 asset types and over 100,000 objects in an average of **about 250 µs of GPU time**. The input is streamed 2D WorldData maps of about 4 MB/km². The design goals were "deterministic, locally stable, art-directable" ([Guerrilla, GDC 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)). The largest source of hitches in modern APIs, however, is not CPU generation but the first use of a pipeline. Epic measured hitches of 5–10 ms and one of **117 ms**. The fix is to compile the PSO when content is requested and to postpone drawing until the pipeline is ready ([UE PSO Precaching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pso-precaching-for-unreal-engine)).

The 64 m zones and the layers of active, loaded and distant areas are documented by the community ([Jötunn](https://valheim-modding.github.io/Jotunn/tutorials/zones.html)), and the existence of the Upgrade World mod, which regenerates already generated zones, suggests that the game stores generated content ([Thunderstore](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/JereKuusela/Upgrade_World/v/1.3.1/)). A primary source from Iron Gate is missing.

### What to store: from "everything" to "only changes"

Shipped games fall into two camps. Minecraft, Luanti and probably Valheim **store the generated output** in the save. Visited areas are thereby frozen, and generator improvements reach them only through special tools. Minecraft also stores the generation state of each chunk and timestamps in the region file header ([Region file format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Region_file_format)). Space Engineers for asteroids, Horizon Zero Dawn for scatter and NMS for terrain **store only changes** on top of the regenerated output. This works only if generation is bitwise deterministic and stable across versions. Luanti adds a useful primitive for small fixes. Each block has a "last active" timestamp and each registered modification (LBM) has its introduction stamp, so a new post-processing fix runs once on old blocks the next time they are loaded. Maps from version 5.11 and older often lack the stamp, which shows the cost of not introducing versioning from day one ([Luanti lua_api.md](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/doc/lua_api.md)).

## A planet is six quadtrees on a cube, positions in doubles

### Cube-to-sphere mapping and cell IDs after S2

The standard and proven design is six cube faces, each a quadtree of fixed-resolution square patches projected onto the sphere. Simply normalizing a cube point (gnomonic projection) gives cells that, by my own calculation, are up to **3√3 ≈ 5.2× larger** in area at the face centre than in the corner. That is why the face coordinates are warped. Google S2 uses a quadratic transform per axis so that "cells have roughly the same size after projection". Its cell is a **64-bit S2CellId** composed of 3 bits for the face, 2 bits per level up to 30, and a terminating bit. At level 30 a cell is about 1 cm on Earth. The API can find neighbours across an edge (4), across a vertex (usually 4, only 3 at the 8 corners of the cube) and all neighbours of a given level ([S2](https://s2geometry.io/devguide/s2cell_hierarchy.html)). Outerra uses a variant of a "quadrilateralized spherical cube". Its main component of distortion is shear, and since it is affine, the frustum planes can be transformed into the skewed space of the tile and cached at deep levels ([Outerra](https://outerra.blogspot.com/search/label/planet%20rendering)). For C17 I recommend (own reasoning) a separable "equiangular" warp `u = tan(π/4 · s)`. The inverse is trivial, `s = 4/π · atan(u)`, unlike Nowell's mapping with no iteration, and the cell size difference drops to roughly 1.4–2×. The functions `det_tan` and `det_atan` must come from our deterministic maths, not from `<math.h>`.

Seams between faces are a problem of topology, not geometry. If the edge vertices of neighbouring patches are computed from the same 3D point on the sphere and noise is sampled in 3D (direction × radius), they match bitwise, and what remains is to compute correctly the adjacency and the rotation of (u, v) across the face edge. For LOD there are two families of methods. Thatcher Ulrich's **chunked LOD** meshes chunks independently and hides cracks with "skirts", strips of triangles below the edge ([Ulrich, SIGGRAPH 2002](https://masters.donntu.ru/2011/fknt/liseenko/library/article1.htm)). Filip Strugar's **CDLOD** selects LOD with a single function of the 3D distance of the observer and handles transitions by morphing without stitching ([CDLOD](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD)). For procedural height planets CDLOD fits better, because it needs no precomputed meshes: one shared grid is shifted for each node according to the height data. Without bindless, heights must lie in a texture array or an atlas with an offset for each draw. This maps directly onto our RHI. The closest existing template for our port is Proland from INRIA. In it, one planar quadtree and one pipeline of producers run either flat or through a `Deformation`, whose implementations are identity, `SphericalDeformation(R)` and `CylindricalDeformation`. The deformation provides `localToDeformed`, its differential, `getVisibility` and the uniform `tangentFrameToWorld`. The configuration then just sets `deform="sphere"` and a size of 6,360,000 m ([Proland Deformation](https://proland.inrialpes.fr/doc/proland-4.0/core/html/classproland_1_1Deformation.html); [SphericalDeformation](https://proland.inrialpes.fr/doc/proland-4.0/core/html/classproland_1_1SphericalDeformation-members.html); [examples](https://proland.inrialpes.fr/doc/proland-4.0/terrain/html/page-examples.html)).

### Height map as the base, voxels only locally

NMS chose voxels everywhere because it wanted caves and overhangs on every planet, and it paid for that with a 3D octree, isosurfaces and memory ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). A height-based cube-sphere is an order of magnitude simpler. The data is 2D, the tree is 2D and seams are solved by skirts or morphing. If voxels are needed, the established seamless solution for marching-cubes LOD is Eric Lengyel's Transvoxel: transition cells between full and half resolution, 512 cases in 73 classes, patent-free, but only for a 2:1 ratio ([transvoxel.org](https://transvoxel.org/)). Dual contouring or surface nets with seam overlap in the NMS style are easier to implement. A large radius runs into precision, though. A godot_voxel user tried an SDF sphere with a radius of 24,000,000 units, and the answer was that Godot needs a double-precision build ([godot_voxel #639](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel/issues/639)). Space Engineers 2, in a 2025 diary, describes overhangs and voxel destruction on planets but not the LOD structure ([Marek Rosa, 2025](https://blog.marekrosa.org/2025/08/mareks-dev-diary-august-21-2025/)).

For Wallhammer Zero I therefore propose a two-step hybrid (my own recommendation). The base of every planet in all LODs is a **height-based cube-sphere** fed by a deterministic function `height(dir3)`, on top of which the same city building and scatter run as on a flat map. Only an optional later step adds **local voxel or SDF volumes** (caves, overhangs, craters, excavations), which are streamed as 3D chunks only near the player and blended into the height map. For a city this is fine, because levelled terrain lies under the buildings anyway. For an LNF-style planet it gives mountains and oceans at any height, because the height map has no "128 m shell", and caves only where the generator asks for them.

### Float32 ends at 100 km, Earth needs 64 bits

By my own calculation from IEEE 754, the float step is about 0.5 mm at 4 km, 1 mm at 10 km, 7.8 mm at 100 km and **0.5 m at 6,371 km**. Vertices in floats relative to the planet centre are therefore unusable for an Earth-sized planet from 100 km on. A float is safe only inside a frame whose origin lies within a few kilometres of the geometry. There are two industry patterns. UE5 Large World Coordinates moved to double in all core types with a default WORLD_MAX of 88 million km, but Niagara stays in float and splits the world into cells as "cell index + float offset". Chaos physics is in doubles with an explicit conversion to float vectors ([UE5 LWC](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/large-world-coordinates-in-unreal-engine-5)). KSP keeps Unity physics in single precision with a floating origin. The active ship sits at zero and Krakensbane shifts the origin when a threshold is crossed. Before version 0.17 the "Deep Space Kraken" appeared, phantom forces at high speeds ([KSP FloatingOrigin](https://kspmoddinglibs.github.io/KSPDocsSite/class_floating_origin.html); [Krakensbane](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/API:Krakensbane)). Cesium for Unreal works internally in ECEF, and the georeference places the engine origin at a geographic position. Users of UE5 in doubles report, however, that automatic origin rebasing "causes more problems than it solves" ([Cesium origin shift](https://cesium.com/learn/cesium-unreal/ref-doc/CesiumOriginShiftComponent_8h_source.html)).

The depth buffer is a separate question. In a simulation, Nathan Reed found that **float32 depth with reversed-Z** gives a zero share of indistinguishable comparisons regardless of the far plane, and recommended it for every perspective projection ([Reed](https://www.reedbeta.com/blog/depth-precision-visualized/)). Outerra reached the same conclusion in 2012: reversed FP32 is slightly more precise than 24-bit logarithmic depth over about 9 orders of magnitude. Logarithmic depth written in the pixel shader, however, disables early-z and costs about **8–10 % FPS** ([Outerra](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2012/11/maximizing-depth-buffer-range-and.html)). For SDL_GPU on Metal, Vulkan and D3D12, the right choice is therefore D32_FLOAT with reversed-Z and an infinite far plane. Logarithmic depth is enough as a fallback path for extreme views from orbit.

### Gravity, curvature and horizon in numbers

On a sphere "up" is simply `normalize(pos − centre)`. Every controller, ground test, jump and camera, however, must get this axis for the given position instead of the constant +Y. Catlike Coding shows this on a complete controller. A static function `GetGravity(position, out upAxis)` is called at the start of every fixed step, ground and steepness tests are dot products with `upAxis`, movement axes are projected onto the gravity plane, and the camera holds an alignment quaternion that is rotated every frame by the minimal rotation from the old axis to the new one ([Catlike Coding](https://catlikecoding.com/unity/tutorials/movement/custom-gravity/)). Cesium warns that the further from the georeference origin, the less accurately the engine's −Z axis matches the true "down" ([Cesium](https://cesium.com/learn/unreal/unreal-placing-objects-v1-x/)).

Curvature determines where a flat city may stand. The drop of the plane tangent at a point at distance d is approximately d²/2R (own calculation). On an Earth-sized planet that is **5 mm at 256 m**, 7.9 cm at 1 km and 7.9 m at 10 km. On a planet with a radius of 100 km it is 0.33 m at 256 m and 5 m at 1 km. A flat district in one tangent frame is therefore safe on Earth up to a radius of about 1 km. On a small planet only a cell fits into one frame, and decimetre differences must be absorbed by foundations or by levelling the terrain under the plot, which a GTA-like generator does anyway. The horizon is `√(2Rh)`: on Earth about 5 km from eye height, 36 km from 100 m, 357 km from 10 km and about 2,300 km from 400 km (own calculation). An Earth-sized planet thus behaves like a flat world at ground level. A small planet, in contrast, shrinks the streamed set by itself. Cesium horizon culling tests each tile's "occludee point" in a space where the ellipsoid is a unit sphere. In the example it removes more than half of the tiles, and 15 % more than the earlier bounding sphere test. Terrain below the ellipsoid level (the sea floor), however, needs a conservative point ([Cesium horizon culling](https://cesium.com/blog/2013/04/25/horizon-culling/)). For approaches from orbit, a Star Citizen trick helps. Planet Tech v5 estimates the colour of distant terrain from data including not-yet-loaded trees and grass, so that no popping is visible on arrival ([starcitizen.tools](https://starcitizen.tools/Planet_Tech_v5)). It is the same idea as the "macro colour from the coarse level" that our deterministic hierarchy gives us for free.

### Bending a flat world at the end is a dead end

Sakura Crossing, one of our reference repos, shows the opposite approach in practice. The whole world is built synchronously at startup as a plane, and at the end `bakeToPlanet` bends it onto an **equirectangular sphere with a radius of 160 m**. Long edges are halved to 4 m, gameplay and collisions keep running in flat coordinates, and lights are pinned to the tangent frame under the player. At an eye height of 1.62 m the horizon is about **23 m**, a railway circling the planet sends about 900,000 triangles every frame without culling, and the equirect converges at the poles ([sakura-crossing](https://github.com/Kenton-GMI/sakura-crossing); our analysis in `docs/research/notes/reference-repos/sakura-crossing.md`). As a toy it works. As an engine it fails in the same places that made Hello Games abandon its first prototype of an "infinite plane wrapped onto a sphere" ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). From sakura-crossing we will still take two small things that turned out useful in the analysis. Bending should be done **per cell within the cell**, never for the whole world. And archetypes need a `BEND` / `RIGID` / `DYNAMIC` flag: static geometry is bent per vertex, while animated rigs and pivots are relocated whole.

## Proposal for Wallhammer Zero: the world shape port and the cell state machine

Everything in this part is a proposal derived from the cited sources. Numbers that are not cited are default values to be verified by measurement, not findings. According to D11 this is analysis, not code.

### World shape port: one API, two implementations

The port combines the patterns of Proland (planar logic plus a swappable deformation), S2 (a 64-bit hierarchical ID with neighbours across seams), Cesium (local tangent frames and anchoring) and UE5 (double on the CPU, float on the GPU). It is a real seam with two implementations, and therefore, according to the first report, justifiably a port. Unlike the five adapters, it does not wrap a foreign technology. It is a pure data table of functions in the `world` module, which the `gen_world` generators read as well.

```c
/* include/eng/world/world_shape.h — world shape port (proposal, not an implementation) */
#include <stdint.h>
#include <stdbool.h>

typedef struct wh_dvec3 { double x, y, z; } wh_dvec3;
typedef struct wh_vec3  { float  x, y, z; } wh_vec3;

/* 64-bit cell ID: [face:3][level:5][x:28][y:28]
   face 0–5 = cube faces, 7 = flat world (x, y with an offset of 2^27).
   level 0 = root (cube face / root tile), higher = finer. */
typedef uint64_t wh_cell_id;

typedef enum wh_shape_kind { WH_SHAPE_FLAT = 0, WH_SHAPE_CUBE_SPHERE = 1 } wh_shape_kind;

typedef struct wh_shape_desc {
    wh_shape_kind kind;
    double  root_edge_m;      /* flat: edge of the root tile, e.g. 65,536 m */
    double  radius_m;         /* sphere: radius of the reference surface (sea level) */
    double  gravity_mps2;     /* gravity magnitude at the reference surface */
    uint8_t stream_level;     /* level of the L1 streaming cell (flat: 8 -> 256 m) */
} wh_shape_desc;

typedef struct wh_frame {     /* local tangent frame of a cell */
    wh_dvec3 origin;          /* double, world coordinates */
    wh_vec3  east, up, north;
} wh_frame;

typedef struct wh_bounds { wh_dvec3 center; double radius; wh_dvec3 occludee; } wh_bounds;

typedef struct wh_world_shape_vt {
    /* topology: purely integer */
    wh_cell_id (*cell_at)(const void *self, wh_dvec3 pos, uint8_t level);
    wh_cell_id (*parent)(wh_cell_id c);
    void       (*children)(wh_cell_id c, wh_cell_id out[4]);
    uint32_t   (*neighbors)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c, wh_cell_id out[8]); /* 8; 7 at a cube corner */

    /* geometry: deterministic (detmath), callable from generators */
    void     (*cell_frame)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c, wh_frame *out);
    wh_dvec3 (*surface_point)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c, double u, double v, double h);
    wh_dvec3 (*noise_domain)(const void *self, wh_dvec3 pos);   /* seamless noise input */
    double   (*altitude)(const void *self, wh_dvec3 pos);       /* above the reference surface */
    wh_vec3  (*up_at)(const void *self, wh_dvec3 pos);
    wh_vec3  (*gravity_at)(const void *self, wh_dvec3 pos);

    /* streaming and LOD */
    double   (*cell_edge_m)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c);    /* non-uniform on a sphere */
    double   (*cell_distance_m)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c, wh_dvec3 pos);
    void     (*cell_bounds)(const void *self, wh_cell_id c, float h_min, float h_max, wh_bounds *out);
    bool     (*below_horizon)(const void *self, const wh_bounds *b, wh_dvec3 eye);
} wh_world_shape_vt;

typedef struct wh_world_shape {
    const wh_world_shape_vt *vt;
    const void   *self;
    wh_shape_desc desc;
    uint64_t      shape_hash;   /* goes into all cache keys and IDs */
} wh_world_shape;
```

The two implementations differ only where they must. The **flat world** has one "face" (7) and an infinite grid through index offset. With a root tile of 65,536 m, an L1 cell with a 256 m edge comes out at level 8, a 1,024 m district at level 6 and a 4,096 m region at level 4, so the L3–L0 hierarchy from the second report is just a naming of quadtree levels. A 28-bit range at level 8 gives roughly ±34,000 km (own calculation). `up_at` returns a constant, `below_horizon` is always false and `noise_domain` returns (x, z, 0). The **cube-sphere** has six faces with the tan warp, `up_at = normalize(pos − centre)`, neighbours across edges with a rotation of (u, v) and the special case of 7 neighbours at the corners, `noise_domain = direction × R` (seamless 3D noise), and `cell_bounds` accounts for the bulge of the curvature and the min/max height, otherwise horizon culling and the frustum would remove visible mountains. On Earth a face has an arc of πR/2 ≈ 10,007 km, so level 15 gives cells of about 305 m and level 16 about 153 m (own calculation). Anyone who wants exactly 256 m chooses a radius of about 5,340 km. This yields a rule that touches D16: **cell size is not an engine constant but a query to the port**. Generators read `cell_edge_m` and take object dimensions from the scale table, never from the cell size.

Content is bent on the sphere according to the archetype flag. Terrain vertices are produced via `surface_point` in doubles and stored in the blob as floats relative to `wh_frame.origin`. The city is generated in the flat tangent frame of a cell, where the curvature error amounts to millimetres on Earth. On a small planet the `BEND` archetype is split into edges shorter than the chosen limit and the vertices are projected, whereas `RIGID` is relocated whole according to the anchor's frame. This takes the good part from sakura-crossing without a global bend. The port also has one immediate benefit for the flat world. A lint in CI forbids a hard-coded "up" (`(0, 1, 0)` and its variants outside `world_shape_flat.c`). NMS showed that a later move to a variable axis leaks into "almost every area of the code" ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)), so the contract must be enforced from M4, even though only the flat implementation exists until M9.

### Coordinates: double on the CPU, float only relative

The first report recommended `int32 cell_x, cell_y + float` local offset. For a flat map the size of GTA V this is enough, but it does not work on a cube-sphere. The cells there are not axis-aligned and each has a different frame, so "cell + offset" would have to be recomputed between frames on every cell crossing. I therefore propose the UE5 and Space Engineers model. **The authoritative position on the CPU is `wh_dvec3` in metres**: on a flat map in the world frame, on a sphere in a frame centred on the planet. At 6,371 km a double has a step of about 1 nm (own calculation), and addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and square root are deterministic in IEEE, provided the generators follow the `-ffp-contract=off` rules from the second report. **Stored cell data** (vertices, instances) is float relative to the origin of the cell frame, as Outerra does for patches ([Outerra forum](https://forum.outerra.com/index.php?topic=81.0)). The **GPU** gets, for every draw, a matrix `(cell origin − camera)` computed in doubles and only then converted to float. A large number therefore never reaches the GPU, and bindless is not needed for this. **Depth** is D32_FLOAT with reversed-Z and an infinite far plane, with logarithmic depth only as a fallback path. **Physics** runs in float in islands with their own origin, like the Space Engineers clusters with 64-bit positions and 32-bit physics ([Marek Rosa, 2014](https://blog.marekrosa.org/2014/12/space-engineers-super-large-worlds_17/)). The island origin shifts when the player moves away by more than a few kilometres (the float step at 2–4 km is about 0.24–0.5 mm). The shift must preserve velocities, because that is exactly what Krakensbane solves in KSP ([KSP wiki](https://wiki.kerbalspaceprogram.com/wiki/API:Krakensbane)). Whether joltc can do a double-precision build, or whether we stay with islands, is a question for the measurement list. Gravity is applied as a custom force on the body according to `gravity_at` (the Catlike pattern), not as a global world constant.

### Six cell states and two dependency pyramids

An L1 cell has a linear data state and, orthogonal to it, flags for expensive products. NMS showed that physics and navmesh cost more than generation ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)), so detailed collision and navmesh are built only where a ticket requires them, and not as a mandatory step of the chain.

```c
typedef enum wh_cell_status {
    WH_CELL_NONE = 0,
    WH_CELL_MACRO,      /* holds references to the data of the L3 region and L2 district */
    WH_CELL_LAYOUT,     /* phase A: heights, road axes, plots, footprints, POI anchors, coarse collision */
    WH_CELL_CONTENT,    /* phase B: instance lists with stable IDs, applied deltas */
    WH_CELL_MESHED,     /* CPU meshes of terrain and unique buildings */
    WH_CELL_RESIDENT,   /* uploaded to the GPU, drawn */
    WH_CELL_ACTIVE      /* physics inserted, AI and scripts tick */
} wh_cell_status;

enum { WH_CELL_F_COLLISION = 1u << 0, WH_CELL_F_NAVMESH = 1u << 1 };
```

| State | Content | Generation dependency | Load from cache / pack | Where it runs | Cache |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MACRO | references to L3 (macro heights after erosion, region type, highways) and L2 (district type, tensor field, main roads) | parent L2 and L3 at radius 0 | none | job | yes: small and expensive |
| LAYOUT | the cell's height field, road axes, plots, footprints, POI anchors from the shifted grid, coarse collision (analytic ground and building prisms) | own MACRO | none | job | yes |
| CONTENT | instances with stable IDs, anchors of large objects, applied deltas | LAYOUT of neighbours at radius 1 (exclusion masks across the edge) | none | job | pristine yes, with deltas only for edited cells |
| MESHED | terrain and building meshes; detailed collision and navmesh only with the flag | own CONTENT and LAYOUT of neighbours at radius 1 (seams) | none | job | yes for expensive meshes |
| RESIDENT | GPU buffers, instances in a storage buffer | MESHED; all PSOs precompiled at boot | — | copy pass on the main thread within a byte budget | no |
| ACTIVE | bodies in physics, AI, scripts | RESIDENT and `F_COLLISION` | — | main thread within an ms budget | no |

The accumulated pyramid is small. An ACTIVE or MESHED cell needs only a ring of one cell in the LAYOUT state, unlike the roughly 10–11 chunks in Minecraft. This is because roads and districts arise at L2 and nobody writes into a neighbour. The **loading pyramid** is empty: a cell from a pack or from the cache does not need neighbours, just like in Minecraft ([ChunkPyramid](https://github.com/RecaniteButOnGit/Minecraft-1.21.11/blob/main/server/world/level/chunk/status/ChunkPyramid.java)). Dense scatter (grass, small details) is missing between the states on purpose. By the rule from the second report it is purely visual, may run on the GPU and is regenerated every time, as in HZD ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)).

The interface towards the rest of the engine is an emerge in the Luanti style. `cell_request(cell, target_status, flags)` returns a future result with the origin `FROM_MEMORY`, `FROM_CACHE`, `FROM_PACK`, `GENERATED`, `CANCELLED` or `ERROR` ([Luanti](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/doc/lua_api.md)). For the `asset` module this is the root ID, which the `asset_provider` port from the second report resolves. Cancellation is handled by the **cell epoch**. Every change of the target state downward raises the epoch and the job checks it at phase boundaries, so stale work ends as `CANCELLED`, which is a normal result. A state drop goes the opposite way from growth. First the bodies are removed from ACTIVE, then the GPU buffers are released from RESIDENT, and then the decoded CPU data. Compressed blobs stay in the cache the longest, as the notes on HZD and Luanti also advise ([Luanti settingtypes.txt](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/builtin/settingtypes.txt)).

### Tickets, priorities and a car at 200 km/h

A cell's level is the minimum over tickets: `level = ticket.base + cell_distance_m(cell, ticket.center) / cell_edge_m(cell)`. Both the distance and the size are provided by the port, so the same code works on a plane and on a sphere. The target state is read from the level by a table. For 256 m cells I propose these default values (to be verified): level 0–1 → ACTIVE with detailed collision, 2–3 → RESIDENT with coarse collision, 4–5 → MESHED, 6–9 → LAYOUT (HLOD proxy of the district and queries like "nearest petrol station"), from 10 only references to L2 and L3. Hysteresis works as in Unreal PCG. A cell drops one state down only at a level 1–2 higher and after a grace period; Luanti has 29 s as the default release ([UE PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine); [Luanti](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/builtin/settingtypes.txt)).

| Ticket | Centre | Base level | Lifetime | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `player` | player position | 0 | permanent | the only one that gives ACTIVE in the full radius |
| `predict` | `p + v·t_look`, for a car along the road graph from LAYOUT | 1 | renewed every step, expires after 1 s | never ACTIVE; `t_look = max(2 s, 2 × p95 cell latency)` |
| `camera` | camera, when far from the player (drone, photo mode) | 2 | permanent | visual states only |
| `travel` | target of a teleport or fast travel | 0 | 15 s | after the Minecraft portal ticket |
| `actor` | important NPC or mission vehicle away from the player | 1, radius 0 | per script | LAYOUT and coarse collision only |
| `forced` | script, mission | configuration | persisted | equivalent of `/forceload` |
| `tool` | debug server, MCP, studio viewport (D17, D18) | configuration | until revoked | headless "give me cell X in state Y" |

Job order is driven by a lexicographic key. The first criterion is missing collision under an actor, the second a missing drawable parent (the "coarse first" rule from both NMS and UE). Then the deciding factors are the deficit between current and target state, the ticket level, the angle to the view or velocity vector and finally distance. There are two queues, as in Luanti: loading from the cache or a pack, and generation, each with its own limit on work in progress. As a default I propose twice the number of workers for generation (Unreal uses 16 parallel components). The main thread integrates results within a budget of about 1–2 ms and with a limit on upload bytes per frame. The default of 16.667 ms in Unreal PCG is a whole frame, and therefore too generous for us ([UE PCG](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)). All pipelines the generator may use are compiled at boot, because the generator's material palette is finite and known. Lazy pipeline creation would bring hitches of the order of 117 ms ([UE PSO](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pso-precaching-for-unreal-engine)).

A fast vehicle sets the budget. 200 km/h is 55.6 m/s, so a car crosses a 256 m cell in 4.6 s (own calculation). If the cold latency of a cell L (generation, mesh, upload) comes out at 1–2 s, the RESIDENT state must run ahead of the car by at least `v·L + cell edge`, that is, by 370–480 m, which is roughly two cells in the direction of travel. A predictive ticket at level 1 ensures this. Prediction along the road graph from LAYOUT is more accurate in a city than a straight line, because the car turns with the street. A frame never waits for generation. If the player outruns the budget, the parent LOD or HLOD is drawn and newly finished cells are blended in by dithering as in NMS. Collision, however, must always exist, and so it has two layers. **Coarse** is available already in the LAYOUT state: analytic ground from the deterministic height function and prisms from building footprints. **Detailed** comes from MESHED. A car thus does not fall through even a cell that is still being generated. Above a configured altitude or speed the streamer switches to a "LOD only" mode, as GTA San Andreas has it above 50 units ([gta-reversed](https://github.com/gta-reversed/gta-reversed)): the ACTIVE ring shrinks and the LAYOUT and HLOD ring grows. On a sphere the horizon is added. Cells below the horizon get only MACRO, and the terrain quadtree is selected by the CDLOD distance, which naturally coarsens with altitude ([CDLOD](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD)). On teleport the scene reveal is conditioned only on a minimal set: ACTIVE at radius 1, RESIDENT at radius 2, applied deltas and finished pipelines. The rest catches up after control returns.

### Stable IDs from the anchor, not from the order

The second report proposed `hash(level, coordinates, subsystem, local index)`. Luanti, however, shows that even the output must not depend on the size of the work unit ([Luanti settingtypes.txt](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/builtin/settingtypes.txt)), and the same holds for IDs. I therefore propose to derive IDs from the **object's anchor**:

```c
/* id = H64(world_seed, shape_hash, domain_name_hash, rule_name_hash, anchor_key) */
/* anchor_key: integer key of the anchor in the rule's grid, independent of the cell size.
   Scatter and POI: index of the dither / shifted grid cell in world coordinates.
   Plots and buildings: quantized centroid (0.5 m) within the L2 district + graph face index.
   Player objects: H64(player_namespace, monotonic counter in the save). */
```

The rule name is a hash of an authored string, not an index in an array, so adding a new rule does not renumber existing objects (the same logic as the string IDs of seeds in the second report). The owner cell is derived from the anchor only afterwards through `cell_at`, so changing the size of the streaming cell does not change the ID. Diffs are keyed by the pair `(owner_cell, id64)`. A collision of the 64-bit hash then matters only inside a cell. With 10⁵ objects in a cell the probability of a collision is about 3 × 10⁻¹⁰ (own calculation), and the generator lint detects duplicates within a cell anyway and resolves them with a deterministic salt.

### Cache keys and generator version

The Merkle key from the second report is extended with the world shape and the domain version and split into two layers. The **pristine** key is `H(output_kind, domain_version, gen_code_hash, preset_hash, world_seed, shape_hash, cell_id, lod, hashes_of_outputs_of_input_levels, lib_versions)`. It contains no deltas, so a player's edit does not invalidate the cache and the pristine cache can be shared between saves of the same world. The **edited** key is the pristine key plus the hash of the cell's deltas and exists only for edited cells. Cost decides what to cache. Cache what is expensive in time and small in bytes (macro terrain after erosion, road graph, LAYOUT, building meshes). Regenerate what is large and cheap (dense scatter as in HZD). As a default rule I propose to cache when generation time exceeds about five times the disk read time. The format of the cache and the deltas can adopt the layout of Minecraft region files: N × N cells in one file, 4 KiB sectors, an offset table and timestamps in the header, and overflow into a separate file ([Region file format](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Region_file_format)). The intermediate stages of the generator (MACRO, LAYOUT, CONTENT) can be dumped as JSON in the dev build, just as NMS stores XML between generators ([McKendrick, GDC 2017](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y)). This serves the golden tests and the debug server from D17.

Versioning follows the lesson from NMS. A world has a **manifest**: the seed, the shape description and its hash, and for each domain a version number and a code hash. Domains are split into two classes.

| Domain | Class | Reason | On a new engine version |
|---|---|---|---|
| world shape (kind, radius, root) | frozen forever | everything else stands on it | new world only |
| macro terrain L3, roads L2/L3, plots | frozen for the world | buildings and deltas anchor to it | the old version stays callable (`gen_terrain_v1`); the new one only for new worlds or a new space of seeds |
| building mass, interiors | frozen with migration | deltas refer to building IDs | migration with an orphan report |
| scatter, vegetation | free | deltas are only "removed" | orphaned REMOVE is dropped |
| materials, colours, palettes | free | purely visual | no migration |
| fauna, pedestrians, traffic | free | does not live in the save | no migration |

A "new space of seeds" is our equivalent of the purple stars. A new district or biome type is switched on only for regions above a certain index or for newly created worlds, and leaves old areas unchanged ([Worlds Part II](https://www.nomanssky.com/worlds-part-ii-update/)). Small fixes get the LBM mechanism from Luanti. Each cell carries `last_processed_epoch`, fixes are registered with the epoch of their introduction, and on an older cell they run once, the next time it is loaded ([Luanti](https://github.com/luanti-org/luanti/blob/master/doc/lua_api.md)). Forced regeneration of a frozen domain is an explicit operation, not a side effect. The tool `wzcli world migrate --dry-run` in headless mode lists which deltas would be orphaned.

### Delta persistence without silent deletion

Deltas are stored per cell in region files, not in one global buffer. A record has a type, a sequence number, a domain and its version. The types are `REMOVE(id)`, `OVERRIDE_XFORM(id, xform)`, `OVERRIDE_PROPS(id, kv)`, `ADD(instance with a player ID)`, `TERRAIN_OP(shape, parameters)` for digging, filling and levelling, and `TERRAIN_SNAPSHOT(area, absolute heights or densities)`. The difference between the last two is essential. An operation survives a generator change semantically ("dig a sphere at point X" makes sense on new terrain too), whereas an absolute snapshot makes sense only over the same base (own reasoning on the relative edit model in NMS). For protected areas (bases, player cities) I therefore propose to store a **terrain snapshot under the footprint** with a margin for blending. The build then stays exactly as the player left it, even if a frozen domain someday did have to be regenerated. That is exactly what led to "relocating bases" in Atlas Rises ([Atlas Rises](https://www.nomanssky.com/atlas-rises-update/)). There is no global cap. When the number of operations in a cell exceeds a threshold, they are merged into a snapshot (compaction). The disk budget is reported in the UI and nothing is deleted silently. For future multiplayer and for a shared LNF-style planet, deltas are the natural unit of replication, because NMS exchanges between players only edits and lists of base parts ([mp1st](https://mp1st.com/news/no-mans-sky-update-3-73-patch-notes-revisits-expeditions-this-nov-24)). Server storage of deltas is, however, outside the scope of this analysis.

## What changes in the previous proposal and in the milestones

The streaming proposal from the first report (refcounted graph, strategies, 128–256 m cells, parent LOD until the children arrive) and the generator as an `asset_provider` from the second report still hold. This research refines them in nine points.

| Earlier proposal | Refinement | Source of the change |
|---|---|---|
| strategies add references to root IDs | strategies produce **tickets**; "what is loaded" is still resolved by the refcounted graph | Minecraft tickets, UE PCG generation sources |
| two target states Loaded / Activated | **six states** plus collision and navmesh flags; generation and loading pyramids | ChunkPyramid; cost of physics in NMS |
| position `int32 cell + float` | **double on the CPU**, float relative to the cell or camera frame, physics in islands | cube-sphere; UE5 LWC; Space Engineers |
| root cell ID `(x, y, lod)` | `wh_cell_id` 64-bit (face, level, x, y) from the shape port | S2 |
| ID `hash(level, coordinates, subsystem, index)` | ID from the **anchor** and the rule name, independent of cell size | the `chunksize` trap in Luanti |
| Merkle key | + `shape_hash` and domain version; separate pristine and edited layers | NMS persistence |
| per-cell diffs | + world manifest, frozen/free domain classes, fix epochs, terrain snapshots under buildings | NMS history 2016–2025; LBM in Luanti |
| predictive streaming source `position + v × 1–2 s` | ticket along the road graph, `t_look` from measured p95 latency, two-layer collision, "LOD only" mode | the 200 km/h budget; GTA SA |
| cell phases A/B | = states LAYOUT and CONTENT; district HLOD from LAYOUT | no change of principle |

The milestones from the second report (M0–M8) remain valid. The content of some of them changes and two new ones are added, both after the first city, as D15 requires.

The list of measurements from the third report, which should be done at the start of implementation, must be extended by five items. First, the cold latency of a 256 m cell from request to RESIDENT (p50 and p95) on the M4 Pro, from which `t_look` is determined. Second, the cost of detailed collision and navmesh per cell against the cost of generation, because NMS claims that physics dominates. Third, the draw cost of CDLOD tiles on Metal without bindless. Fourth, the cost of `det_tan`/`det_atan` per vertex. Fifth, the replay time of the deltas of a heavily edited cell. Until then, the ticket level numbers, the queue limits and the 1–2 ms integration are only default values.

## Conclusion

The main lesson from NMS is not about planet shape, noise or LOD. It is that **the generator version is part of the world's identity**. Hello Games needed four major updates and one relocation of player bases before arriving at the rule "freeze what carries player work and let the new generator run only in a new space of seeds". An engine that wants to regenerate the world from a seed must have a domain manifest, callable old versions and deltas that survive a change of the base, and it must have them before the first save exists. The second lesson is less visible. What decides is the cost of physics and navigation and the reliability of collision, not the speed of noise. That is why two-layer collision pays off, and a fast car driving over a cell that is not finished yet does not fall through. The third lesson is that the assumption "Y is up" is an architectural decision even if nobody makes it consciously. A world shape port with a lint from M4 costs little, whereas introducing it afterwards cost Hello Games a change in "almost every area of the code".

There are three uncertainties. About Light No Fire we know nothing technically, so in our proposal it is only a bar for scope. Most NMS numbers come from 2017 and from reverse engineering, and later rewrites (dual marching cubes, GPU passes, "better caching") remain undescribed. Above all, though, nobody has yet measured whether a 256 m cell on the M4 Pro generates fast enough for 200 km/h. The whole ticket budget rests on the first of the new measurements, and until it is done, the levels in the tables are only a well-reasoned estimate.
