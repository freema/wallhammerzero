# Scale, proportions and metrics for procedurally generated 3D open worlds, and how to keep many independent generators at one consistent scale

Compiled 2026-11-05 for Wallhammer Zero (C17, all content generated by code, bar GTA III / WoW, free third-person and car-chase camera). It implements decision D16: 1 unit = 1 m, one central scale table, ratio lints, a reference mannequin in every preview, and stylisation set in one place.

**Method and limits.** The session's shared web-search budget ran out after 5 searches, so most findings come from direct fetches of known primary or secondary pages. These sources returned nothing: valvesoftware.com (403), nacto.org (403), gtaboom (DNS failure), gamesradar (truncated), and Unity docs (redirected, not followed). Facts from those sites come only from search-result summaries and are marked as such. Every finding is tagged:
- **[STD]** a standard or code, or a reference work that cites one.
- **[GAME]** measured or documented data from a shipped game or engine.
- **[OPINION]** a practitioner's opinion or a forum claim.
- **[COMP]** my own arithmetic from cited numbers.
- **[K]** general knowledge, not verified in this session. [K] values appear only in Gaps and in the proposal, never as cited facts.

Context from our own docs: a texel density of 128 px/m for the environment and 256 px/m for cars and characters. At vertical FOV 60° and 1080 lines, 1 m at distance *d* covers ≈ 935/*d* px. A 1.8 m figure is ≈ 170 px tall at 10 m. The world hierarchy is L3 region 4–8 km, L2 district ~1 km, L1 cell 128–256 m, L0 scatter. The `genlint` tool and the review contact sheets are already planned (wallhammerzero/docs/research/procedural-graphics.md).

## Q1. Real-world reference dimensions (body, architecture, street, vehicles)

### Takeaway
The real-world anchors fit in one table:
- A person of about 1.75 m with eye height about 1.65 m.
- A door of about 0.9 × 2.0–2.1 m.
- A storey of about 3.0–3.1 m for residential and about 3.9 m for commercial buildings.
- An urban lane of about 3.0–3.5 m. NACTO recommends 10 ft (3.05 m), German urban roads go down to 2.5 m, and motorways use 3.75 m.
- A kerb of 100–200 mm.
- A parking bay of about 2.4–2.7 × 4.8–5.8 m.
- A block of about 80 × 274 m in Manhattan and 100 × 200 m in Chicago and Melbourne.

Several common values (detailed human height statistics, Neufert door and window sizes, railings, sidewalks, lamp posts, specific car models) could not be verified this session. They are listed in Gaps as [K] values to check before freezing the table.

### Cited Findings
**Human body**
- Average US adult (ergonomic reference used by level designers): body about 20 in wide × 69 in tall (0.51 × 1.75 m), eye height 66 in (1.68 m). [STD/secondary] — [The Level Design Book, "Metrics"](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Young Dutch men averaged 183 cm in 2012, among the tallest populations (Schönbeck et al., *Pediatric Research* 2012). This sets a realistic upper anchor for a "tall" population. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Human height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_height)

**Architecture**
- US real-life interior metrics: interior wall 6 in thick and 96 in tall (0.15 × 2.44 m ceiling), minimum hall width 48 in (1.22 m), door 36 × 80 in (0.91 × 2.03 m), step 7 in rise × 11 in run (178 × 279 mm, slope ≈ 32°). Stairs should have landings every 12–16 steps, and modern stairs run at 30–35°. [STD/secondary] — [The Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Storey height, floor to floor: about 3.1 m in residential towers and about 3.9 m on leased commercial floors (Saroglou, Theodosiu & Meir 2017, *Energy and Buildings* 149). A generic "≈3.0 m (10 ft)" figure in the article has no citation. Lobbies and utility floors are taller, with no figure given. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Storey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey)
- Blondel's stair rule: 2 × riser + tread = step length. Standard stairs are almost always under 45°. Building codes may restrict helical stairs with risers over 9.5 in (240 mm). [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Stairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stairs)

**Streets**
- NACTO (Urban Street Design Guide):
  - 10 ft (3.05 m) travel lanes are appropriate in urban areas.
  - One 11 ft (3.35 m) lane per direction is allowed on designated truck or transit routes.
  - Lanes wider than 11 ft "should not be used" because they encourage speeding.
  - 9–9.5 ft (2.75–2.9 m) through lanes work in select cases next to a turn lane.
  
  Source quality: these come from search-result summaries, because the page returned 403. [STD] — [NACTO lane width](https://nacto.org/publication/urban-street-design-guide/street-design-elements/lane-width/)
- Lane widths generally:
  - Typically 2.7–4.6 m.
  - US Interstate standard 12 ft (3.7 m).
  - European minimums are generally 2.5–3.25 m.
  - German Autobahn: 3.75 m with two lanes per direction. With three lanes, the rightmost is 3.75 m and the others 3.5 m.
  - German Bundesstraße: at least 3.5 m per lane, plus 0.25 m on the outer sides and shoulders of at least 1.5 m.
  - German urban access roads: as narrow as 2.5 m.
  
  Urban lanes narrower than 2.8 m or wider than about 3.1–3.2 m increase crash risk. Capacity peaks at 3.0–3.1 m. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane)
- Vehicle width limits: EU maximum 2.55 m for most trucks and 2.6 m for refrigerated ones, with at least 0.25 m extra clearance per side. US maximum truck width has been 102 in (2.59 m) since 1976. On curves, rear trailer axles can track 1–3 m inside the front path. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane)
- Kerb reveal (visible height) is typically 4–8 in (100–200 mm). Total kerb height including the buried part is often 16 in (406 mm). Faces over 6 in (152 mm) can stop car doors from opening. The GB "high containment" kerb is 14 in (36 cm). Kerb cuts should have zero reveal. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Curb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curb)
- Parking bays by country [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Parking space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_space):
  - US: at least 8.5–9 ft (2.6–2.7 m) wide. The often-quoted DOT perpendicular depth of 19 ft (5.8 m) is tagged "citation needed". Parallel stalls are 20–24 ft (6.1–7.3 m).
  - UK: recommended 2.4 × 4.8 m, proposed 2.6 × 5.0 m.
  - France (NF P 91-100): 2.2–2.3 m minimum width.
  - Australia (AS2890): 2.4 × 5.4 m.

**Blocks**
- Manhattan standard block: about 264 × 900 ft (80 × 274 m). Chicago: 330 × 660 ft (100 × 200 m), with 16 east–west or 8 north–south blocks to the mile. Melbourne: also 100 × 200 m. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: City block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_block)

**Vehicles**
- China GB/T 3730.1 sedan segments by length: A00 under 4.0 m, A0 3.7–4.4 m, A 4.2–4.8 m, B 4.5–5.0 m, C/D at least 5.0 m. Hatchbacks and SUVs are classed by wheelbase: A00 under 2.5 m, A 2.5–2.8 m, B 2.7–3.0 m. The IIHS groups cars by "shadow" (length × width): about 6.5–7.4 m² for mini and small cars and over 10.2 m² for the largest. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Car classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_classification)

**Street furniture**
- Historical: 17th-century Paris street lanterns hung at 20 ft (6.1 m), 18 m apart. Modern wide-angle LED retrofits in Flanders doubled pole spacing from 45 m to 90 m. [STD/secondary] — [Wikipedia: Street light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_light)

### Inferences
- [COMP] Real ratios to carry into the scale table, from the US ergonomic set above:
  - door height / body height = 80/69 ≈ 1.16
  - door width / body width = 36/20 = 1.8
  - ceiling / body = 96/69 ≈ 1.39
  - hall / body width = 2.4
  - storey (3.1 m) / body (1.75 m) ≈ 1.77
  - urban lane (3.05 m) / truck width (2.55 m) ≈ 1.2
  - urban lane / car width (~1.8 m [K]) ≈ 1.7
- [COMP] At 50 km/h, a 100 m Chicago block takes 7.2 s to drive. The 274 m long side of a Manhattan block takes 20 s. These timings decide how often a chase meets an intersection.
- Kerb height is a strong scale cue near the camera. At 15 cm it should be a fixed constant, not a free parameter. It is also gameplay-relevant: car suspension, step-up height and the ped/vehicle boundary.

### Gaps

## Q2. How shipped games deliberately deviate from real scale, and why

### Takeaway
Shipped games break real scale in two separate ways:
1. **Openings and spaces around the player get larger.** Doors, halls and ceilings are scaled by roughly 1.2–1.5× relative to the character. Source, for example, uses door height ≈ 1.5 × player height against 1.16 in reality. This gives the camera, the collision capsule and the animation room to work.
2. **World distances shrink.** Skyrim's overworld is about 14–16 sq mi, roughly half of Manhattan, and GTA maps compress whole cities.

The characters themselves keep roughly human height. The previous project's problem (giant people in a toy city) matches the reverse mistake: character scale and architectural scale drifted apart, with no shared reference.

### Cited Findings
**Engine metrics**
- Common engine conventions: Unity works in metres and Unreal in centimetres. Quake, Half-Life and Source work in "inches?", used "lightly", in a way that does "not really make sense". [GAME] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Engine and community default metrics compared with US real life [GAME, from the Level Design Book table]:

  | | Player W × H | Eye height | Door W × H | Wall / ceiling | Min. hall | Step rise × run |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Unity | 1.0 × 1.8 m (or 2.0) | 1.5–1.7 m | 1.25 × 2.5 m | 3.0 m | 2.0 m | 0.1 × 0.15 m |
  | Unreal | 60 × 176 cm | 152 cm | 110 × 220 cm | 300 cm | 150 cm | 15 × 25 cm |
  | Quake / HL / Source | 32 × 72 in | 64 in | 56 × 112 in | 128 in | 64 in | 8 × 12 in |
  | US real life | 20 × 69 in | 66 in | 36 × 80 in | 96 in | 48 in | 7 × 11 in |

  Notes from the same page:
  - Collision boxes are "usually much larger than the character model", for movement stability.
  - In-game eye height is often below real eye height. FPS cameras often sit at the neck or chest.
  - Minimum hallway width is at least 2 × player width, "and will still feel narrow".
  - Doors should be narrower than halls, to leave room for the frame.
  
  — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Valve Developer Community values for Source (HL2/CS:S):
  - Player collision 32 × 32 × 72 units, crouched 36, eye level 64.
  - "Normal" door 48 × 108 units, "normal" corridor 64 wide × 128 tall.
  - Left 4 Dead: player 72 tall, 33 wide.
  - The wiki is reported to use about 0.75 in per unit for architecture, as opposed to 1 in per unit for characters. Forum users dispute whether that matches the real world.
  
  Source quality: search-result summary only, page returned 403. The Level Design Book gives a different Source door (56 × 112), so the two sources conflict. [GAME] — [VDC Dimensions](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/Dimensions_(Half-Life_2_and_Counter-Strike:_Source)); conflicting door size in [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)

**Bethesda modular kits (Skyrim)**
- Joel Burgess (Bethesda), "Skyrim's Modular Approach to Level Design", GDC 2013 Level Design in a Day; article dated 2013-05-01 [GAME]. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
  - A character is "about 128 units, or six feet tall". The engine is Z-up.
  - Kit footprints such as 512³ rooms tile with 256³ halls. A 384³ room "will eventually create gaps and/or overlaps", so sub-kit footprints must be multiples of each other. Grid snap is typically half the footprint. Pieces must stay inside the footprint.
  - A uniform door-frame size is set early. Doors can then be reused and transitions work, and AI and animation get a fixed standard.
  - The narrowest traversable space is at least two character widths.
  - The AI incline limit is about 60°, but kits use 30–45° slopes because steeper ones look bad in animation.
  - Floor thickness is fixed up front to avoid z-fighting.
  - Seven dwarven kits covered 400+ cells. Skyrim's scope was a 16 sq mi overworld, 5 cities, 300+ dungeons and 140+ points of interest.
- Skyrim world scale per the Level Design Book: about 14 sq mi, "about half the size of Manhattan". The tallest mountain is 766.5 m. The player runs 11.7 mph (18.8 km/h) without tiring. "Big levels are actually much smaller than real-world equivalents." Fast player speeds feel normal in-world, and the world must be "internally consistent". [GAME + OPINION] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Stylised worlds still need internally consistent metrics. Quake 1 steps are often 16 in tall, and the rest of the world is built "chunky" to match. Games should "build at the player's scale", which may not be human scale. [OPINION] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)

**Why spaces get larger, and city scale**
- Third-person cameras: a developer quoted by GamesRadar says Gears of War's doors need to be massive so the third-person camera can pass through. A Guild Wars 2 player calls tiny archways and doors "horrible camera-wise", and players there complain that doors are too small for mounted characters. EverQuest scaled doors per race: small in gnome areas, huge in ogre areas. [OPINION, from search-result summaries; the GamesRadar page could not be read] — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/why-are-the-doors-so-big-in-video-games-some-developers-explain); [GW2 forum](https://en-forum.guildwars2.com/topic/99980-large-world-tiny-people-map-design-flaw); [Pantheon forum](https://seforums.pantheonmmo.com/content/forums/topic/6925/areas-scaled-to-race-size)
- "Everything is relative to the player and how the player sees the world." Character collision bounds drive doorway height and width. Base metrics "will work 9 times out of 10". (Kirill Tokarev, 2016-12-26, summarising World of Level Design.) [OPINION] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/guide-to-player-scale-and-worldarchitecture-in-ue4)
- GTA V map area is quoted at about 75.8 km² (29.3 sq mi) including countryside and ocean, against 500+ sq mi for Los Angeles. Writers describe it as "compressed distances and rearranged locations". Other blogs give about 50 sq mi, and none states a method. [GAME, low-quality sources] — [GTA V map vs LA (expertbeacon)](https://expertbeacon.com/how-accurate-is-gta-map-to-la/); [thegtaplace](https://m.thegtaplace.com/gtav/setting/)
- No official Blizzard statement on WoW's architectural scale was found. Search results were forum talk only. [—] — [Blizzard forum](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/can-someone-explain-wows-size-logic/784606)

### Inferences
- [COMP] **Door-height / player-height ratio** by source:

  | Source | Door / player height |
  |---|---|
  | Real US | 80/69 = 1.16 |
  | Unreal community | 220/176 = 1.25 |
  | Unity community | 2.5/1.8 = 1.39 |
  | Source (VDC) | 108/72 = 1.50 |
  | Source (LDB) | 112/72 = 1.56 |

  **Door-width / player-width** (collision): real 1.8; Unreal 1.83; Unity 1.25 (but its collision box is wide, 1.0 m); Source 1.5–1.75.

  Shipped and engine conventions therefore enlarge door height by about 1.08–1.34× relative to reality, after normalising to player height. Their ceilings are also higher: Unity 3.0 m against 2.44 m real, a ratio of 1.67 to the player against 1.39.
- [COMP] Skyrim: 128 units = 6 ft gives 1 unit ≈ 1.43 cm, so its 512-unit kit footprint ≈ 7.3 m and the 256-unit hall ≈ 3.7 m. A 3.7 m hall is about 2× a real 1.8 m corridor. Spaces are inflated for a third-person, melee-friendly camera, while the character stays human-sized.
- [OPINION/inference] Characters stay about 1.7–1.9 m. What gets scaled up is the clearance around them (openings, halls, ceilings, lanes), and what gets scaled down is the distance between points of interest (map size, block count). Our previous failure (people too big for the city) amounts to scaling the character without the openings. If doors were 2.0 m and characters about 2.2 m, the door/character ratio would be about 0.9, well below the 1.16–1.56 range that every convention above respects.
- For a GTA / WoW-style target, sensible stylisation multipliers relative to real life are: openings (door height/width, ceilings, halls) 1.15–1.35×; storey height about 1.1–1.2×; urban lane about 1.1–1.25× (driving at arcade speeds with a chase camera); world distances compressed to about 0.3–0.6× by fewer and shorter blocks, not by smaller objects. These ranges are my synthesis, not measured GTA or WoW data.

- No primary source was found on WoW's architectural scale factor or character height in world units. The "1.3–1.5× architecture" figure in the brief remains unverified.
- Not researched because the search budget ran out: Insomniac's Spider-Man NYC scale and Zelda: Breath of the Wild scale. Ed Byrne's *Game Level Design* (2005, chapter on metrics) is a book and was not accessed.

## Q3. Camera and perceived scale

### Takeaway
Perceived scale depends on scale cues (door, window, person, car) and on the camera:
- **FOV** sets apparent speed and distortion.
- **Camera height relative to the eye-level horizon** sets whether the viewer reads the scene as human, giant or toy.
- **Boom distance** sets how much clearance the architecture needs.

Our own numbers (935/*d* px per metre at 60° vFOV and 1080p) let generators check readability.

### Cited Findings
- FOV:
  - Quake's default FOV was 90°, and competitive players used 120°.
  - Modern action games offer 60–90° sliders with narrower defaults.
  - Higher FOV raises apparent peripheral speed and distorts the image. Lower FOV feels more intimate.
  - Lens analogy: 20 mm (84°) is extreme and close, 70–100 mm (29–20°) naturalistic, 200 mm (10°) flat and distant.
  - Third-person action games often widen FOV while sprinting.
  
  [OPINION/practice] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Speed perception is relative to body size: 4 mph is about one body length per second for a human. Sound, HUD and NPC behaviour change perceived speed. Super Hang-On's "turbo" raised the speedometer cap from 280 to 324 km/h without changing actual movement. [OPINION/GAME] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Collision boxes are larger than visual models, and FPS eye height sits below real eye height. Both shift the relation between camera and architecture. [GAME] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics)
- Our own design doc: at 60° vFOV and 1080 lines, 1 m at distance *d* ≈ 935/*d* px. That gives about 134 px/m at 7 m (car camera) and 94 px/m at 10 m. A 1.8 m figure is about 170 px tall at 10 m, with a face of about 23 px. [COMP, internal] — wallhammerzero/docs/research/procedural-graphics.md

### Inferences
- [COMP] Screen height of an object of height H at distance d = H × (res_y / 2) / (d × tan(vFOV/2)). The **ratio** between two objects at the same distance does not depend on FOV. FOV and distance therefore change absolute readability, not relative scale. Relative scale errors, such as a character against a door, cannot be fixed by the camera. Only the metrics table fixes them, which is why lints must check ratios.
- [Geometry, inference] **Horizon test.** In a perspective view, the horizon line passes through every object at the camera's height. With the camera at a standing eye height (about 1.6 m), the horizon should cut every standing adult at eye level, whatever the distance:
  - If characters' heads sit well above the horizon while a door's top sits near it, the characters read as giants.
  - If the camera is high (a third-person boom at 2.5–4 m) and the horizon cuts first-floor windows, the whole scene reads as seen by a tall observer.
  - With a very high camera plus a narrow FOV, the scene reads as a miniature ("toy town").
  
  This gives a cheap automated check: render the scale rig with the gameplay camera and verify where the horizon falls on the mannequin and the door. The miniature effect also relates to depth-of-field cues, but no source was collected for this.
- [Inference] A third-person camera boom of length L behind and above the character needs free space of about L plus the camera's collision radius. Otherwise the camera collides and pushes in, and the scene "shrinks". Interior ceilings must exceed the camera height in the follow pose, and halls must leave room for the camera's lateral offset (over-the-shoulder cameras). This is why Gears-style doors and Skyrim-style halls (about 3.7 m) are larger than reality. The chase camera behind a car at about 7 m needs road clearance (lane plus parked-car zone plus kerb), or it clips buildings in corners.
- [Inference] The strongest scale cues are, in order: (1) people next to doors, (2) window rows, which count storeys, (3) cars against lanes and kerbs, (4) repeated street furniture (lamp posts, bollards, benches). Generators should never vary these freely. All of them should come from the table. Large-object variety (building massing, tree size) is less risky.

### Gaps
- No published numbers were found for typical third-person boom lengths and heights (e.g. WoW default camera distance, GTA III on-foot or car camera offsets). Measure them in the games or treat them as a game parameter.
- No source was found on the "toy town" or tilt-shift perception threshold. It is described from general knowledge [K].

## Q4. Engineering practices that enforce scale consistency in a procedural pipeline

### Takeaway
Shipped pipelines enforce scale through:
- one unit convention;
- a small set of **agreed metrics fixed early** (door frame, footprint grid, floor thickness, minimum widths);
- **grids whose sizes are multiples of each other**;
- a **metrics test map** (the "zoo") with gridded placeholder textures.

A pure-code pipeline can do better: the table can be *data that every generator reads*, derived values can be *computed* rather than copied, and ratios can be *linted automatically* on every seed.

### Cited Findings
- Bethesda's process [GAME/practice] — [Game Developer (Burgess 2013)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design):
  - The footprint is "the foundation of the entire kit", agreed by the designer and the artist during the "proof" phase, which takes 1–3 weeks with almost no mesh detail.
  - A uniform door frame is decided early, as a standard for AI and animation.
  - Pivot placement is decided during graybox, because changing it later means fixing many instances by hand.
  - "Patch-up" pieces, such as a half-length hall, hide root footprint errors and bloat kits.
  - Kit errors multiply across hundreds of instances.
- Practice points from the Level Design Book [OPINION/practice] — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics):
  - Modular kits "bake measurements into tiles: measure once".
  - Changing kit grid or granularity may mean rebuilding levels.
  - Test metrics early in blockout, with gridded placeholder textures and a playtested **metrics zoo** map.
  - Grids: Source and older engines used powers of two, while Unity users prefer 1s and 10s.
  - "Metrics are not magic." Feel is decided in playtesting.
- Level design and art coordinate on gameplay metrics such as jump height, cover height and minimum width. (Search summary of the GDC 2013 slides.) [GAME] — [SlideShare: Modular Level Design for Skyrim](https://www.slideshare.net/JoelBurgess/gdc2013-kit-buildingfinal)
- Our existing plan already has `genlint`, review contact sheets of N seeds, golden hashes, and texel-density constants of 128/256 px/m. [internal] — wallhammerzero/docs/research/procedural-graphics.md; DECISIONS.md D16

### Inferences
**Engine-level practices to adopt.** These are my proposals, building on the cited practices.

1. **One unit, one up axis, one table.** Set 1 unit = 1 m (D16). All metrics live in one versioned data file, e.g. `content/scale/metrics.toml`, compiled into a `ScaleBible` struct. Generators take `const ScaleBible*` as input. The table's hash enters every asset's cache key, so a metrics change invalidates exactly the affected assets, which the Merkle cache already supports. No generator may hard-code a length literal. A CI grep lint can flag float literals above 0.05 in `gen_*` sources outside an allowlist, for example normalised 0–1 shape parameters.
2. **Derived, not duplicated.** Only about 6–8 values are primary: hero height H, population height distribution, stylisation multipliers, module grid, car reference class, lane base. Everything else is a function of them, evaluated in one place (`scale_derive()`). Examples: door height from the tallest NPC plus headroom times the opening multiplier; storey from door plus head plus slab; lane from the widest common vehicle plus clearance; sidewalk from two passing capsules plus a furniture zone. This mirrors Bethesda's "door frame first" but makes it executable.
3. **Footprint grids that are multiples of each other** (Burgess). Use a base module m (e.g. 0.5 m). The kit grid is 2m or 4m, the storey is quantised to m, the façade bay to 2m or 3m, and the parcel frontage to a multiple of the bay. Quantising snaps generators onto shared seams and prevents "384 vs 512" mismatches between building, sidewalk and road generators.
4. **A reference "scale rig" in every preview.** Every contact sheet and review render includes:
   - a 1 m checker ground grid;
   - the hero mannequin (H), plus a tall NPC and a short NPC (p95 and p5 heights);
   - the standard door;
   - the reference sedan;
   - a horizon line at the camera's eye height.
   
   It is rendered from both the gameplay third-person camera and the chase camera at their gameplay distances, not just an orbit thumbnail. This is our version of the "metrics zoo".
5. **Lints at three levels:**
   - *per asset*: bounding box within a class range;
   - *cross-class ratio*: e.g. door / hero, lane / car width;
   - *world-statistical*: generate N cells per district type and check histograms of storey height, street width, block length, door/character clearance and texel density. Failures block the cook, like golden hashes do.
6. **Collision first.** Lints use the movement capsule and the camera probe sphere, not only the visual mesh. Collision boxes are larger than models (Level Design Book), and openings must pass the capsule and the camera.

### Gaps
- Not researched: CityEngine/CGA unit handling (CGA rules operate in the scene's metric coordinate system; attributes such as floor height and lane width are rule parameters [K]) and Houdini or UE5 PCG practices for metrics (e.g. data assets of parameters shared by PCG graphs [K]). The search budget ran out, and no source was fetched.
- No published example was found of an automated cross-asset scale lint in a shipped procedural pipeline. The lint proposal is original engineering, not precedent.

## Q5. Planet scale: planet radius vs character scale

### Takeaway
Do not shrink characters on a planet. The radius is a per-game parameter, and curvature must be handled per streaming cell. Light No Fire targets an Earth-sized planet, where curvature is negligible at street level: about 8 cm drop over 1 km. Small "game planets" (R ≈ 100 km) bend metres per kilometre, so city generators need local tangent frames per 128–256 m cell.

### Cited Findings
- Light No Fire (Hello Games) is announced as an open world the size of the actual Earth. [GAME] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/hello-games-grand-follow-up-to-no-mans-sky-light-no-fire-is-an-open-world-the-size-of-the-actual-earth)
- Proland's example planet radius is 6,360,000 m. The same quadtree pipeline runs with `deform="sphere"`. [GAME/tech] — [Proland examples](https://proland.inrialpes.fr/doc/proland-4.0/terrain/html/page-examples.html) (via our note planety_tvar_sveta.md)
- Curvature drop d²/(2R), computed in our earlier note [COMP] — research_notes/no-mans-sky-generation-and-streaming/planets_world_shape.md:

  | Radius | Distance → drop |
  |---|---|
  | Earth (6,371 km) | 256 m → 5.1 mm; 1 km → 7.9 cm; 10 km → 7.9 m |
  | 600 km | 1 km → 0.83 m |
  | 100 km | 256 m → 0.33 m; 1 km → 5 m |

  Horizon at 2 m eye height: about 5 km on Earth, about 630 m at R = 100 km. A flat district in one tangent frame is safe while d²/2R stays under 5–10 cm: about 1 km on Earth, about 100 m on a 100 km planet.

### Inferences
- The "compression" planet games appear to use is in radius and feature density (biomes, settlements per km²), not in character or building scale. A small radius makes curvature visible from altitude and keeps travel times short. Characters, doors and vehicles keep the same ScaleBible. The radius is a world-shape parameter (D15 port), never a scale multiplier.
- [COMP] At R = 100 km, a 256 m cell sags 0.33 m, so a single flat street grid across a district would visibly float. Building foundations and road decks must be fitted per cell, and a lint should check the maximum foundation gap. At R ≥ 1000 km, a 256 m cell sags under 3.3 cm, within the kerb-height tolerance.
- Mountains and vistas: Skyrim's tallest peak is 766.5 m (Level Design Book). Perceived grandeur comes from relative height and fog, not real heights in kilometres. Terrain amplitude can be a separate stylisation knob without touching the human-scale table.

### Gaps
- No verified figure was found for No Man's Sky planet radii, or for any Hello Games statement on how distances are compressed (commonly cited [K]: NMS planets of a few hundred km circumference to low thousands of km). Not verified.

## Q6. Proposal: a concrete scale bible for the engine, fixed vs per-game parameters, and lints/tests

### Takeaway
Use a two-layer table:
- **Engine-fixed:** units, axes, the derivation graph, lint framework, texel densities and camera/readability math.
- **Per game:** about 10 primary numbers. These are hero height and population distribution; opening, storey and lane stylisation multipliers; the module grid; block and frontage ranges; vehicle classes; and planet radius or flat world.

All other dimensions are derived. A starting "GTA III / WoW-like" profile is below. Every multiplier is a design proposal grounded in the ratios of Q1 and Q2, not a measured value from those games.

### Cited Findings
- The ratios this proposal is built on are the Q1 real ratios (door / body 1.16, ceiling / body 1.39, urban lane 3.05 m) and the Q2 engine conventions (door / player 1.25–1.56; halls at least 2× player width, Skyrim about 3.7 m). — [Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics); [Burgess 2013](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design); [NACTO](https://nacto.org/publication/urban-street-design-guide/street-design-elements/lane-width/); [Wikipedia: Storey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey)

### Inferences (the proposal)

**A. Engine-fixed (not per game)**
- 1 unit = 1 m (D16). Choose one up axis now: Y-up or Z-up. Bethesda used Z-up; the choice is free, but it must be single and documented. Gravity default 9.81 m/s² overridable; on a planet, "up" is normalize(pos − centre).
- The texel density constants (128 px/m environment, 256 px/m characters and cars) and the readability formula px = H × (res_y/2) / (d × tan(vFOV/2)).
- The `ScaleBible` schema, the `scale_derive()` function and the lint framework (`genlint scale`). The table hash goes into every cache key.
- The scale rig used in all review renders.

**B. Per-game primary parameters, with the default "stylised city" profile**

| Key | Real anchor | Default (stylised GTA/WoW) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hero_height` | 1.75 m | **1.80 m** | Visual height. Capsule radius ≈ 0.30–0.35 m (proposal). |
| `pop_height_mean / sd` (M, F) | ≈1.75/0.07, 1.62/0.07 [K] | same, clamped to ±2.5 sd → max ≈ 1.95 m | Also scales stride and IK (already parametric). |
| `k_opening` | 1.0 | **1.20** (range 1.10–1.35) | Doors, gates, halls, interior ceilings. |
| `k_storey` | 1.0 | **1.10** (range 1.0–1.2) | Taller façades read "heroic" without making people look small. |
| `k_lane` | 1.0 | **1.15** (range 1.0–1.3) | Arcade driving and chase camera. |
| `k_distance` | 1.0 | **0.5** (range 0.3–0.7) | Applies only to counts and lengths of blocks, districts and roads, never to objects. |
| `module` | — | **0.5 m** | All kit dimensions are multiples of it. |
| `vehicle_ref` | sedan ≈ 4.6 × 1.8 × 1.45 m [K] | per class table, `k_vehicle` = **1.0–1.05** | Keep cars nearly real; the chunky style comes from the silhouette, not size. |
| `world_shape` | — | flat / sphere(R) | R is a world parameter (Q5), not scale. |

**C. Derived dimensions** (computed by `scale_derive()`, rounded up to `module` or 0.05 m)
- `door_h = max(2.05, pop_max_h + 0.15) × k_opening` → (1.95 + 0.15) × 1.2 ≈ **2.5 m** (Unity convention 2.5 m). The minimum k_opening of 1.10 gives 2.3 m.
- `door_w = max(0.9, 2.6 × capsule_r) × k_opening` → about **1.1 m** (Unreal 1.1 m). Double doors and shops are 2 × door_w.
- `storey_res = (door_h + 0.35 head + 0.3 slab) × k_storey`, floored at 3.0 × k_storey → about **3.4 m**. `storey_ground_retail = storey_res × 1.35` → about **4.6 m**. `storey_commercial` = 3.9 × k_storey ≈ **4.3 m**.
- `ceiling_clear = storey − slab`. This must also exceed the camera height in the interior follow pose plus 0.3 m. That is a lint rule, not a derivation.
- `window_sill = 0.9 m × k_opening^0.5` and `window_head = door_h`, so heads align across the façade (Neufert convention [K]).
- `step_rise ≈ 0.17 m` and `step_run ≈ 0.28 m` (Blondel 2R + G ≈ 0.62 m), **not** scaled by k_opening. Steps, kerbs, railings and seats are body-contact dimensions and stay real; scaling them makes characters look small.
- `railing_h = 1.0–1.1 m` (unscaled). `kerb_h = 0.15 m` (unscaled, Q1).
- `lane_w = max(3.05, truck_w + 2 × 0.25) × k_lane` → max(3.05, 3.05) × 1.15 ≈ **3.5 m**. `parking_lane = 2.5 × k_lane` ≈ 2.9 m. `sidewalk_min = 2 × (2 × capsule_r + 0.3) + furniture_zone (0.8)` ≈ 2.8 m; downtown 4–6 m.
- `street_width(type)` = Σ lanes + parking + 2 × sidewalk. With the defaults: a residential two-lane street with parking on both sides is about 13.6 m kerb to kerb ≈ 19 m wall to wall. A downtown four-lane arterial is ≈ 26–30 m wall to wall.
- `block_len(type)` = real range × k_distance, clamped to multiples of the façade bay. Manhattan-like long side 274 × 0.5 ≈ 140 m with the short side about 80 m. A European perimeter block is about 60–100 m. A US-suburban block is longer, but with fewer houses.
- Prop and vehicle classes are given as tables of real ranges × k_vehicle or unscaled. Lamp posts follow street type (about 6 m residential, 9–10 m arterial [K]), scaled by k_storey so they stay below or near the 2nd–3rd floor windows.

**D. Lints and tests** (`genlint scale`, run at cook time on N seeds per generator and per district type; failures block approval)
1. **Bounding box per class.** For example: character height ∈ [p1, p99] of the population; car length ∈ its class range; door opening ∈ [door_h ± 2 %]; building storey count × storey height = building height ± module.
2. **Cross-class ratios**, all computed from the actual generated outputs, not from the table:
   - door_h / tallest NPC ≥ 1.10, and door_h / hero ∈ [1.2, 1.5];
   - door_w / (2 × capsule_r) ≥ 1.5;
   - storey / door_h ∈ [1.3, 1.9];
   - lane / widest vehicle allowed on the street ∈ [1.25, 1.6];
   - sidewalk ≥ 2 passing capsules plus the furniture zone;
   - car roof height / hero ∈ [0.75, 0.95];
   - bus height / storey_res ≈ 0.9–1.0;
   - kerb, step and railing within ±10 % of the real values.
3. **Clearance by simulation.** Sweep the movement capsule through every generated door and corridor. Sweep the camera probe sphere along the third-person follow path through interiors and alleys, and along the car-chase path through generated street corners. Fail if the camera must push in more than X % of its boom length.
4. **Statistical world lint.** Generate 50 cells per district type and check histograms of street width, block length, storey height and building height against the profile. Check the distance between intersections in seconds at the target driving speed (e.g. 4–10 s).
5. **Texel density lint.** Already planned: 128 / 256 px/m ± tolerance, measured from UV area against world area.
6. **Horizon / scale-rig render test.** Use a headless render of the scale rig and a sample street. Check numerically that the projected heights of mannequin, door and car keep the derived ratios. Check that the horizon at the gameplay eye height crosses the mannequin within its head band. Store the result as a golden image or hash plus numeric assertions.
7. **Grid lint.** Every kit piece and parcel frontage must be a multiple of `module` (Burgess's multiples rule). Flag non-multiples as errors, not warnings, because they compound across instances.
8. **No-literal lint.** CI grep in `gen_*` for length literals outside `ScaleBible`.

**E. Process**
- Treat the table like Bethesda's footprint. Freeze the primary values in a "proof" milestone (M2/M3), with a metrics-zoo scene of the generated scale rig plus one street. The owner, as art director, approves it there, before mass generators exist.
- Later changes go through DECISIONS.md and invalidate the cache through the table hash.

### Gaps
- All multipliers (k_opening 1.2, k_storey 1.1, k_lane 1.15, k_distance 0.5) are proposals. They need validation on the scale-rig scene against GTA III and WoW reference screenshots supplied by the owner. No measured GTA or WoW proportions were obtained.
- Camera boom length, height and FOV defaults for on-foot and chase cameras still need a source or measurement. They are needed for lints 3 and 6.
- [K] values in the table (population height statistics, sedan size, lamp-post heights, Neufert window and furniture data) should be verified against NCD-RisC, ČSN/DIN and Neufert before the table is frozen.
