## 1. UV-free texturing: triplanar, biplanar, world/object-space shading, normal mapping; when UVs are still needed and how to generate them

### Takeaway
For a code-only engine, the default surface path should be **world- or object-space projection (triplanar / biplanar) of procedurally generated tileable textures, plus purely analytic per-pixel noise for detail**. This removes UV unwrapping from almost everything. Normals need a correct "triplanar normal mapping" blend (Golus: whiteout or RNM per plane, with axis-sign handling). UVs are still needed for baked per-object unique data: lightmaps, baked AO/curvature, and decals with unique content. **xatlas** produces those automatically.

### Cited Findings
- **Biplanar mapping (Inigo Quilez)** does texturing with **2 texture fetches instead of triplanar's 3**. It picks the two projections best aligned with the normal and drops the third, "at the cost of some extra arithmetic". IQ notes that "much of the visual complexity comes from the fact that we have to provide texture gradient by hand", because the plane choice is per pixel, so implicit derivatives break and you must use `textureGrad` / HLSL `SampleGrad` with `ddx/ddy` of the world position — [iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar](https://iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar) [search]
- Biplanar artifacts concentrate near the singular directions where the normal is ≈ (±1,±1,±1)/√3, where the dropped third plane would have had equal weight. Elsewhere it looks clean — [scrapbox 0b5vr: Biplanar Mapping](https://scrapbox.io/0b5vr/Biplanar%20Mapping) [search]
- IQ's weight remap: weights are taken from |n| of the two chosen axes, then `w = clamp((w - 0.5773)/(1 - 0.5773), 0, 1)` (0.5773 = 1/√3, which pushes the dropped axis's weight to 0 at the singularity), then raised to a sharpness power `k/8`. A Shadertoy reference is linked from the article — [iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar](https://iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar) [prior]
- Anecdotal counterpoint: a Godot developer who ported biplanar reported trouble with normal-map swizzling and found "little actual performance benefit (at least in my testing)". This is one anecdote, not a benchmark — [r/VoxelGameDev thread via search](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/VoxelGameDev/comments/jzjv1u/biplanar_mapping_a_potentially_cheaper) [search]
- Further cost-reduction variants exist: **uniplanar mapping with dithered blending** samples one plane per pixel and hides the seam with dither (meant to sit under TAA) — [godotshaders: Uniplanar mapping with dithered blending](https://godotshaders.com/shader/uniplanar-mapping-with-dithered-blending/) [search]
- **Ben Golus, "Normal Mapping for a Triplanar Shader" (2017).** Naively blending the three tangent-space normal maps as if they shared one tangent frame gives wrong lighting: normals point the wrong way on some faces and are mirrored on negative-facing sides. He compares, per projection plane: (a) a simple "swizzle" that treats the tangent normal xy as world offsets on the two plane axes; (b) **UDN blend**; (c) **Whiteout blend**; (d) **Reoriented Normal Mapping (RNM)**. Each plane's tangent normal is blended with the surface (world) normal swizzled into that plane's space, then swizzled back to world space and weighted. Whiteout is the cheap, good-looking default; RNM is the most correct but costs more. Negative-axis mirroring is fixed by multiplying the tangent-normal x by `sign(normal.axis)` (and flipping z accordingly). Blend weights: `w = pow(abs(n), sharpness); w /= (w.x + w.y + w.z)` — [Golus, Medium](https://bgolus.medium.com/normal-mapping-for-a-triplanar-shader-10bf39dca05a) [prior; 403 in this session]
- **Triplanar cost model:** 3 projections × N texture maps (albedo, normal, ORM/height) = 3N samples per layer. A material with 3 maps blended across 4 terrain layers is 36 samples per pixel. Biplanar cuts this by a third, and the fetch-count savings is IQ's stated motivation — [iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar](https://iquilezles.org/articles/biplanar) [search]
- **Automatic UV generation:** **xatlas** is a small C++11 library with no external dependencies. It produces unique texture coordinates for lightmap baking or texture painting, is a fork of thekla_atlas (used by *The Witness*), and does segmentation into charts, parameterization, and packing. Workflow: create atlas → add meshes → generate. Output meshes get extra vertices at seams, but the index count stays the same. It can pack multiple meshes into one atlas — [xatlas fork README (ducphamhong/xatlas)](https://github.com/ducphamhong/xatlas); [Adobe Lagrange xatlas binding docs](https://opensource.adobe.com/lagrange-docs/python/autoapi/lagrange/xatlas/index.html); [xatlas-three npm](https://npmjs.com/package/xatlas-three) [search]. Upstream is github.com/jpcy/xatlas (MIT) [prior; upstream page not confirmed in this session].
- A pure-Rust reimplementation following xatlas's approach exists (uvunwrap), which shows the algorithm is reimplementable — [docs.rs uvunwrap](https://docs.rs/crate/uvunwrap/0.2.0/source/README.md) [search]

### Inferences
- **Recommended default per object class:**
  - *Terrain* (heightfield): planar XZ UV for the top plane, plus triplanar or biplanar only where the slope is above a threshold (branch on |n.y|). Most terrain pixels then pay for 1 projection, not 3.
  - *Procedural props/buildings* (generated meshes): the generator already knows the semantics (wall, roof, trim), so **emit UVs directly from the generator** (box/cylindrical projection per generated face, scaled in world units). This is free, exact and seam-controlled. Use triplanar only for "blobby" geometry (SDF-meshed rocks, caves, Marching Cubes / Surface Nets output).
  - *SDF/voxel-meshed geometry:* biplanar with `SampleGrad`, or pure analytic 3D noise evaluated in object space (no texture at all).
- **Use object space, not world space**, for anything that moves, so the texture doesn't "swim". Store the object-space position, or a per-instance offset/scale, as an interpolant.
- **When UVs are still needed:** (1) lightmaps or any baked per-surface unique data (AO, curvature, thickness, edge-wear masks baked from geometry); (2) unique decals or atlas pages; (3) virtual-texture page addressing for non-terrain meshes; (4) the tangent-space normal maps you sample with standard MikkTSpace tangents (triplanar avoids this). xatlas at cook time on the CPU (C++11, wrap with a C ABI) covers case (1)–(3). Cache its result by mesh hash like any other cooked asset.
- **HLSL specifics:** keep `ddx(worldPos)` / `ddy(worldPos)` and call `SampleGrad` for biplanar or for any plane selection that changes per pixel, otherwise mip selection breaks at seams. Plain triplanar with the full 3 fetches can use implicit derivatives because every plane is always sampled.

### Gaps
- No measured GPU numbers (ms) for triplanar vs biplanar vs UV mapping on Apple M-series or Vulkan/D3D12 hardware were found. The only performance claim found is an anecdotal "little benefit".
- Golus's article and IQ's biplanar article couldn't be fetched in full this session (403/404). The formulas above should be re-checked against the originals before implementation.

## 2. Procedural texture generation techniques: noise families, fBm, derivatives, domain warping, texture bombing, stochastic tiling/blending, Wang tiles

### Takeaway
A small kernel is enough to cover most natural materials: **hash → value/gradient noise (with analytic derivatives) → fBm → domain warping → Worley/Voronoi (F1, F2−F1, edges) → remap/colorize**. Two techniques remove repetition from tileable textures: **texture bombing / IQ's "texture repetition" tricks**, and **Heitz–Neyret histogram-preserving tiling-and-blending** (3 fetches + a LUT), with Mikkelsen's hex-tiling as a cheaper relative. Only the histogram-preserving method keeps contrast without ghosting, and it suits only stochastic textures (rock, moss, sand, bark), not structured ones (bricks, tiles).

### Cited Findings
- **Heitz & Neyret, HPG 2018, "High-Performance By-Example Noise using a Histogram-Preserving Blending Operator."** It takes a small example of a stochastic texture and synthesizes unlimited output with the same appearance. It partitions output space on a **triangle grid**, assigns a random input patch to each vertex, and **blends three patches** inside each triangle. The key contribution is a blending operator that preserves the histogram: no ghosting, softened edges, reduced contrast or new colors. For Gaussian inputs, histogram preservation reduces to preserving mean and variance. Non-Gaussian inputs are first **"Gaussianized"** with a precomputed histogram transform, blended, then de-Gaussianized with the inverse transform. Both transforms are precomputed, so the whole operation runs per pixel in a fragment shader. It works on random-phase and many non-random-phase stochastic inputs ("moss, granite, sand, bark"). It is claimed to be ">20× faster" than state-of-the-art procedural noise at comparable quality — [Heitz research page for the paper](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/) [fetched]
- Variance-preserving blend detail: `G = Σ wᵢ(Gᵢ − μ) / sqrt(Σ wᵢ²) + μ` (dividing by sqrt of sum of squared weights instead of the sum of weights restores the variance lost by averaging). The inverse transform T⁻¹ is a 1D LUT texture per channel (after decorrelating the color channels with PCA). The Deliot–Heitz chapter also prefilters T⁻¹ per mip level so mipmapping stays correct — [Heitz & Neyret paper](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/) / [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/procedural-stochastic-texturing-in-unity) [prior for formula details]
- **Unity Labs implementation:** "Procedural Stochastic Textures by Tiling and Blending", Thomas Deliot & Eric Heitz, *GPU Zen 2*. It shipped as a research-prototype Shader Graph node and a legacy Standard-shader variant on GitHub, and is explicitly not under formal development. Limitation stated by Unity: suitable only for **tileable textures that look like random noise from a distance (rust, moss, bark)**, not textures with precise geometric shapes — [Unity blog: Procedural Stochastic Texturing in Unity](https://unity.com/blog/engine-platform/procedural-stochastic-texturing-in-unity); [UnityLabs/procedural-stochastic-texturing README](https://github.com/UnityLabs/procedural-stochastic-texturing/blob/master/README.md) [search]
- Jason Booth (MicroSplat author) describes stochastic texturing as sampling the texture several times from different regions and blending with a luminosity-preserving operator. He notes that the **height-blend variant** (choosing samples by height instead of a histogram blend) has become common in Unity/Unreal terrain tools — [Booth, "Stochastic Texturing", Medium](https://medium.com/@jasonbooth_86226/stochastic-texturing-3c2e58d76a14) [search]
- **Hex-tiling (Mikkelsen, JCGT 2022, "Practical Real-Time Hex-Tiling")** is a production-oriented relative. It uses a hex grid, random offset and rotation per hex, and a contrast-preserving blend sharpened by luminance, with no Gaussianization LUT. It is also 3 fetches, and it supports normal maps by rotating the tangent-space normal with the per-tile rotation — [JCGT](https://jcgt.org/published/0011/03/05/) [prior]
- **IQ "Texture repetition"** shows three approaches: (1) per-tile random offset/flip with 4-tap blending across tile borders; (2) a "virtual pattern" using Voronoi cells, each with a random offset (≈ up to 9 fetches); (3) a cheap 2-fetch version that picks two of N offsets indexed by a low-frequency noise value and blends them. Derivatives are again taken from the original UV and passed with `textureGrad` — [iquilezles.org/articles/texturerepetition](https://iquilezles.org/articles/texturerepetition/) [prior; 404 in this session]
- **Texture bombing:** splat randomly placed, rotated and scaled sprites per grid cell (check the 3×3 neighborhood) to break up repetition or to place features such as pebbles, cracks and spots — [GPU Gems ch. 20 "Texture Bombing" (R. Steven Glanville)](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-iii-materials/chapter-20-texture-bombing) [prior]; theory in Lefebvre & Neyret, "Pattern Based Procedural Textures", I3D 2003 [prior]
- **Wang tiles:** Cohen, Shade, Hiller, Deussen, "Wang Tiles for Image and Texture Generation", SIGGRAPH 2003. A small set of tiles with color-coded edges (e.g. 8 tiles for 2 colors per edge direction) tiles the plane non-periodically. A stochastic tile index can be derived per cell from a hash, with no stored map — [Cohen et al. 2003](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/882262.882265) [prior]
- **Noise families, IQ articles (canonical references):** value noise and gradient noise with analytic derivatives — [iquilezles.org/articles/morenoise](https://iquilezles.org/articles/morenoise/), [gradientnoise](https://iquilezles.org/articles/gradientnoise/); fBm and the meaning of the Hurst exponent / gain=0.5 — [iquilezles.org/articles/fbm](https://iquilezles.org/articles/fbm/); domain warping `f(p + h·fbm(p + h·fbm(p)))` — [iquilezles.org/articles/warp](https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/); Voronoi edges with correct distance to borders — [iquilezles.org/articles/voronoilines](https://iquilezles.org/articles/voronoilines/); smooth Voronoi — [iquilezles.org/articles/smoothvoronoi](https://iquilezles.org/articles/smoothvoronoi/) [all prior]. Key practical idea from "morenoise": analytic noise derivatives give normals for free (no finite-difference re-evaluation), and feeding the accumulated derivative magnitude into the octave weight (`a *= 1/(1+dot(d,d))`) produces eroded-looking terrain.
- Gradient noise vs value noise: gradient noise has no blocky low-frequency artifacts. Simplex has fewer directional artifacts and scales better in 3D/4D. In the original patent context, Perlin's simplex noise in ≥3D was covered by US patent 6,867,776 (expired January 2022), which is why OpenSimplex was created — [Wikipedia: Simplex noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplex_noise) [prior]

### Inferences
- **Minimal "material kernel" library in HLSL** (shared include, also compiled to a C reference for CPU-side cook): `hash` (PCG or integer hash, no `sin` hash, for cross-API determinism), `valueNoise2/3+deriv`, `gradNoise2/3+deriv`, `simplex3`, `worley2/3 (F1,F2,cellId)`, `voronoiEdgeDist`, `fbm`, `ridged`, `warp`, `remap/smoothstep/bias/gain`, `palette (IQ cosine palettes)`. That is ~500 lines and covers rock, stone, dirt, sand, bark, rust, cracks and cells.
- **Determinism across Metal/Vulkan/D3D12:** float noise evaluated per pixel can differ in the last bits between backends (fma contraction, transcendental precision). That is fine for runtime shading but **not** for cooked assets hashed by content. Bake cook-time textures on one canonical path (the CPU reference, or GPU compute with integer hashing), and hash the *inputs* (program + params + seed + generator version), not the outputs.
- **Repetition-breaking strategy by texture type:** stochastic textures (rock, grass, sand) → Heitz–Neyret or hex-tiling. Structured textures (bricks, planks, tiles) → generate procedurally per cell with a cell-ID hash (brick variation from Worley/grid IDs) instead of tiling-and-blending. Small details → bombing.
- Heitz–Neyret preprocessing (Gaussianization histogram, PCA, inverse LUT) is cheap CPU work that fits naturally into the cook step next to texture generation.

- No direct performance comparison of Heitz–Neyret vs hex-tiling vs IQ's 2-fetch method on one GPU was found.

## 3. Demoscene precedents: Farbrausch .kkrieger / werkkzeug, 4k/64k intros, operator graphs

### Takeaway
Farbrausch's tools show that **an operator DAG (generators → filters → combiners), evaluated in order at load time with high-precision intermediates, produces convincing textures from bytes of data**. The whole stack is open source (BSD/public domain) in `farbrausch/fr_public`. **OpenKTG (`ktg/`) is the cleanest reference** to port to C17 + compute shaders: 16-bit/channel, premultiplied alpha, sampling conventions matching D3D10+/GL.

### Cited Findings
- `fr_public` contains: **altona_wz4** (Altona framework + Werkkzeug4, "fully functional and a good base for your own engine or tool"), **altona2**, **genthree** (Candytron), **kkrunchy/kkrunchy_k7** (exe packers), **ktg** (OpenKTG, "a texture generation function set designed around 2007"), **lekktor**, **RG2** (RauschGenerator 2, for 64k intros), **v2** (synth used in intros, kkrieger, debris), **werkkzeug3** (used for many demos/intros; includes **w3texlib**, the texture library used for fr-033), and **werkkzeug3_kkrieger** (a kkrieger branch, "unlikely to build"). Everything is BSD-licensed or public domain, with terms per project — [github.com/farbrausch/fr_public](https://github.com/farbrausch/fr_public) [fetched]
- **OpenKTG README:** made in 2007 in response to a request for an open texture-generation standard. It is "simpler to describe and more orthogonal" than previous FR generators, and "the first of our texture generators to properly respect (premultiplied) alpha everywhere". "Written for clarity and simplicity, not speed." It keeps **full 16 bits per color channel** with proper rounding, and defines texture sampling, filtering and pixel-center rules that match OpenGL/GL ES and D3D10+ so that hardware ports are straightforward. It is explicitly suggested as a starting point for **pixel- or compute-shader texture generation** and as a better base than Werkkzeug3's generator. Files: `gentexture.cpp/hpp`, `types.hpp`, `demo.cpp` — [fr_public/ktg](https://github.com/farbrausch/fr_public/tree/master/ktg) [fetched]
- OpenKTG's operator set (from `gentexture.hpp`): `Noise` (octaved Perlin-style), `GlowRect`, `Cells` (cellular), `ColorMatrixTransform`, `CoordMatrixTransform`, `ColorRemap` / `CoordRemap` (look up one texture through another, for distortion and gradients), `Derive` (gradient and normals from height), `Blur`, `Ternary` (lerp/select by mask), `Paste`, `Bump` (lighting a height map), `LinearCombine`. The `demo.cpp` builds a full material from about a dozen calls — [fr_public/ktg/gentexture.hpp](https://github.com/farbrausch/fr_public/blob/master/ktg/gentexture.hpp) [prior; README fetched, header not read this session]
- *.kkrieger* (Farbrausch, Breakpoint 2004) is a 96 kB FPS whose textures, meshes and sounds are all generated at startup from operator graphs authored in werkkzeug. The commonly cited figure is that its assets would take hundreds of MB if stored conventionally — [Wikipedia: .kkrieger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.kkrieger) [prior]

### Inferences
- **Architecture to copy:** an asset = a small serialized DAG (`op_id, params[], inputs[]`) + seed. The cooker evaluates nodes in topological order. Each node output is cached by `hash(op_id, params, input_hashes, generator_version)`, which is a Merkle-style DAG hash. Editing one node only re-evaluates downstream nodes, which is exactly what the user's "programs + params + seed, cached by hash, hot reload" model needs.
- Use **16-bit (R16_UNORM / RGBA16)** or float intermediates in the graph and quantize or BC-compress only at the end. OpenKTG explicitly keeps 16 bits per channel; 8-bit intermediates band badly after several remap/blur stages.
- OpenKTG ops map 1:1 onto compute shaders. Generators are pure per-texel functions; `Blur` is a separable 2-pass shader; `Derive`/`Bump` are 3×3 stencils; `CoordRemap` is a dependent fetch. A port is mostly mechanical.
- 4k/64k intros differ from kkrieger: they usually evaluate materials **per pixel** in a raymarching fragment shader (SDF scenes with procedural shading), instead of generating textures. This is the "evaluate in shader" end of the spectrum, the same family as IQ's Shadertoy work.

### Gaps
- I did not read werkkzeug3 `w3texlib` sources or Fabian "ryg" Giesen's blog posts on the generators this session, so specific op counts and memory/time budgets for kkrieger's texture generation are unverified.
- No sourced information was found on how 64k intros (RG2) store texture graphs (byte sizes per texture).

## 4. Material graphs as code: height → normal, AO, curvature, edge wear, PBR parameter generation; baking masks from SDF or mesh

### Takeaway
Substance-Designer-style materials can be reproduced with a handful of derived maps: **height → normal (Sobel/central differences), height → AO (horizon or multi-scale blur difference), height → curvature (Laplacian), plus geometry-level masks (mesh curvature, SDF AO, distance-to-edge)**. These drive edge wear, dirt in crevices, and moss on up-facing surfaces. In a procedural pipeline the generator *knows the geometry*, so masks can be computed analytically (distances from SDFs, generator-emitted edge distances) instead of being baked from a high-poly mesh. Tiny Glade's window wear from a per-vertex signed distance attribute is a shipped example.

### Cited Findings
- **Tiny Glade** windows add irregular wear to the plaster around them. The technique is a **single float vertex attribute storing a signed distance to the window edges**, which the shader combines with noise. This is geometry-derived masking with no unwrap and no baked texture — [80.lv: Procedural windows in Pounce Light's Tiny Glade get an upgrade](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade) [search]
- A fan wiki claims Tiny Glade's "ruined" wall material changes geometry rather than swapping a texture. This is low reliability and not confirmed by developers — [Tiny Glade building tools (fan wiki)](https://cf-inc.shapes.inc/fandom/tiny-glade/building-tools) [search]
- **SDF ambient occlusion:** sample the SDF at k steps along the normal and compare the distance to the step length: `occ = Σ (hᵢ − sdf(p + n·hᵢ)) · falloffᵢ`. It costs 5 SDF evaluations and is the standard trick in IQ's raymarching work — [iquilezles.org/articles/nvscene2008 "Rendering Worlds with Two Triangles"](https://iquilezles.org/articles/nvscene2008/) [prior]; origin: Alex Evans, "Fast Approximations for Global Illumination on Dynamic Scenes", SIGGRAPH 2006 course [prior]
- **Teardown** (voxels) uses ray tracing for AO, soft shadows and specular occlusion, and **no** global illumination ("light does not bounce off surfaces"). This is per-frame computed occlusion rather than baked maps — [Software Engineering Daily interview with Dennis Gustafsson](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/2025/01/02/teardown-and-voxel-based-rendering-with-dennis-gustafsson/) [search]

### Inferences
(Standard derivations; none of these were sourced this session. They follow from the techniques above and standard image processing.)
- **Height → normal:** `n = normalize(float3(-(h(x+1)-h(x-1))·s, -(h(y+1)-h(y-1))·s, 2))` (central differences) or Sobel 3×3. Scale `s` = height range in world units ÷ texel size in world units, so normal strength is physically consistent across texture resolutions. Use wrap addressing for tileables.
- **Height → cavity/AO (texture-space):** `ao = saturate(1 − k·(blur_big(h) − h))`. Use multiple blur radii for multi-scale cavity, or horizon-based AO over 8 directions for quality. Run it at cook time as a compute pass.
- **Height → curvature:** Laplacian `∇²h` (positive = convex edges/peaks, negative = cavities). Edge wear = `smoothstep(t0, t1, curvature + noise·amp)`; dirt = `smoothstep(.., -curvature) * ao`.
- **Mesh curvature** (for generated meshes): per-vertex mean curvature via the cotangent Laplacian, or the cheaper "angle between adjacent face normals" averaged per vertex. Store it as a vertex attribute (like Tiny Glade's SDF attribute), so no UVs are needed.
- **SDF-native curvature:** curvature = Laplacian of the SDF (6 extra SDF taps), or `div(normal)`. AO = the SDF-AO trick above. For SDF-modeled props that get meshed, evaluate these at mesh vertices during cook and store them as vertex colors.
- **Generator-emitted masks are the cheapest and best:** if code builds a wall with a window, it can emit `distToOpening`, `distToCorner`, `heightAboveGround` and `isTopFacing` per vertex. Shaders then do Substance-like layering (`moss = upFacing * noise * (1-heightAboveGround/2m)`, `edgeWear = distToCorner < noise·w`) entirely per pixel.
- **PBR parameter generation:** generate albedo from height/masks through palettes (IQ cosine palette or 1D gradient LUT), keeping albedo within physically plausible ranges (roughly 30–240 sRGB for non-metals). Derive roughness from the same masks (worn edges smoother on metal, rougher on paint). Metalness is binary per layer. Pack as **albedo (BC7/BC1) + normal xy (BC5) + ORM/height (BC7 or BC4×n)**, so 3 textures per material layer.
- **Layering order (Substance-style "smart material" in code):** base (noise fBm on height) → structure (Voronoi cells/bricks with cell-ID variation) → derived (normal, AO, curvature) → masks (edge wear, cavity dirt, top-facing moss, height-band) → material layers blended by masks (height-blend for natural transitions) → PBR pack.

## 5. GPU compute vs CPU generation; caching to disk; texture arrays/atlases instead of bindless; virtual texturing (Far Cry 4 AVT)

### Takeaway
SDL_GPU's binding model is **16 samplers, 8 storage textures, 8 storage buffers and 4 uniform buffers per stage, with no bindless**. Organize materials as **`Texture2DArray`s, one array per (format, resolution) class, indexed by a per-material layer index from a storage buffer**. That covers hundreds of materials with 3–6 bindings. Generate textures with **compute shaders at cook time**, **BC-compress on the CPU, and cache to disk by input hash**. For terrain, a **software virtual texture / runtime-composited terrain cache** (Far Cry 4 AVT style) is implementable without sparse/tiled resources: an indirection texture plus a physical page atlas, rendered into by compute or render passes.

### Cited Findings
- SDL_GPU internal limits (SDL `src/gpu/SDL_sysgpu.h`): `MAX_TEXTURE_SAMPLERS_PER_STAGE 16`, `MAX_STORAGE_TEXTURES_PER_STAGE 8`, `MAX_STORAGE_BUFFERS_PER_STAGE 8`, `MAX_UNIFORM_BUFFERS_PER_STAGE 4`, `MAX_COMPUTE_WRITE_TEXTURES 8`, `MAX_COMPUTE_WRITE_BUFFERS 8`, `MAX_VERTEX_BUFFERS 16`, `MAX_VERTEX_ATTRIBUTES 16`, `MAX_COLOR_TARGET_BINDINGS 8`, `MAX_FRAMES_IN_FLIGHT 3` — [SDL_sysgpu.h on GitHub (main)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libsdl-org/SDL/main/src/gpu/SDL_sysgpu.h) [fetched]
- SDL_GPU shader binding conventions: in HLSL (DXBC/DXIL), vertex shaders put `t[n]` (sampled textures, storage textures, storage buffers) and `s[n]` (samplers) in **space0** and uniforms `b[n]` in space1. Fragment shaders use **space2** for t/s and **space3** for uniforms. SPIR-V uses sets 0/1 (vertex) and 2/3 (fragment). Registers start at 0 and **must be consecutive with no gaps**. Ordering is: samplers/sampled textures, then storage textures, then storage buffers. In MSL, sampled textures come first, followed by storage textures; vertex buffers start at `[[buffer(14)]]`. The page doesn't state numeric limits — [SDL wiki: SDL_CreateGPUShader](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/SDL_CreateGPUShader) [fetched]
- **Far Cry 4 Adaptive Virtual Texturing (Ka Chen, Ubisoft Montreal, GDC 2015):** addresses texel-resolution loss of MegaTexture / procedural virtual texturing in large open worlds. It was applied to a **10 km × 10 km** world at **10 texels/cm**, which the session listing calls **"20 times higher than traditional virtual texturing"**. It covers rendering terrain and large numbers of decals on CPU and GPU — [GDC Vault session 1021761](https://gdcvault.com/play/1021761/Adaptive-Virtual-Texture-Rendering-in); [GDC Vault 1021760](https://gdcvault.com/play/1021760/Adaptive-Virtual-Texture-Rendering-in); [Game Watch (JP) coverage](https://game.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/691757.html) [search]. The CEDEC 2015 Far Cry 4 talk (Stephen McAuley) says AVT noticeably increased terrain detail — [CEDEC 2015 session](https://cedec.cesa.or.jp/2015/session/ENG/14698.html) [search]
- AVT mechanism: the virtual texture is **procedurally composited** (terrain material layers + decals rendered into physical pages on demand, not streamed from disk). "Adaptive" means each terrain sector's virtual image size scales with distance to the camera (near sectors get a huge virtual resolution, far sectors a small one), and sectors are re-allocated in the indirection texture as the camera moves. Physical pages are kept in a cache atlas — [GDC Vault 1021761](https://gdcvault.com/play/1021761/Adaptive-Virtual-Texture-Rendering-in) [prior for mechanism details]
- **Teardown's palette approach as a bindless substitute:** each voxel stores **one byte** indexing a **256-entry palette** of color + material properties. All palettes are packed into **one texture 256 wide × (number of palettes) tall** kept on the GPU. Rendering is one draw per object with that object's 3D texture bound. Unused palette slots are filled at load time with precomputed color variations (many yellows because of the spray can) — [Voxagon blog: spraycan](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/12/03/spraycan.html); [Juan Diego Montoya: Teardown breakdown](https://juandiegomontoya.github.io/teardown_breakdown.html); [acko.net: Teardown frame teardown](https://acko.net/blog/teardown-frame-teardown/) [search]

### Inferences
- **Texture arrays as bindless replacement:** SDL_GPU supports `SDL_GPU_TEXTURETYPE_2D_ARRAY` [prior]. Plan for ~4 arrays bound per fragment stage: `albedoArray (BC7)`, `normalArray (BC5)`, `ormhArray (BC7)`, plus optional `detailArray`. All layers in one array share size and format, so standardize materials at e.g. 1024² (or 512² for terrain detail layers that get triplanar plus detail noise). Per-material constants (layer indices, tint, roughness scale, triplanar scale, noise params) go in a **storage buffer indexed by material ID** (per-instance or per-draw uniform). This uses 4–6 of the 16 sampler slots and 1 of the 8 storage-buffer slots, so there is ample headroom. Layer-count limits come from the API; Vulkan guarantees ≥256 layers (`maxImageArrayLayers`), and D3D12/Metal allow 2048 [prior]. Check what MoltenVK and Metal report on the M4 at runtime.
- **Adding a material = writing a new layer**, so hot reload is cheap: regenerate one layer with `SDL_UploadToGPUTexture` into that slice. Bump the array size by reallocate-and-copy only when it fills.
- **Atlases vs arrays:** avoid atlases for tiling materials (bleeding at mips, no hardware wrap). Use atlases only for non-tiling unique data (xatlas lightmaps, VT physical pages, decal sheets).
- **Where to generate:**
  - *Cook time (offline, cached on disk by input hash):* every tileable material texture set (graph → 16-bit → derived maps → BC compression), Heitz-Neyret LUTs, xatlas UVs + baked AO/curvature lightmaps, mesh vertex-attribute masks. Run graph ops as **compute shaders** (fast iteration, the same HLSL as runtime noise) or on the CPU reference (deterministic). Do BC encoding on the CPU with a library (e.g. bc7enc_rdo / ISPC texcomp, both permissively licensed [prior]), because SDL_GPU has no GPU BC encoder. Persist as DDS/KTX2-like blobs keyed by hash.
  - *Load time:* cheap variations (palette and color remaps into new layers, Teardown-style), assembling texture arrays from cached blobs, or regenerating at reduced resolution when the cache is missing (dev builds only).
  - *Per frame, cached (runtime virtual texture):* terrain splat compositing (multiple layers + height-blend + decals + roads) rendered into VT pages only when a page becomes visible or more detailed. The cost is amortized and the per-pixel shading cost becomes a single fetch.
  - *Per pixel, every frame:* micro-detail noise (1–2 octaves of gradient noise with derivatives for normal perturbation), macro variation (low-frequency world-space noise tinting albedo to kill tiling at distance), anti-tiling (stochastic blend), mask-driven layering on props from vertex attributes, and SDF-material evaluation for SDF-rendered things.
- **Software VT on SDL_GPU (no sparse resources needed):** (1) feedback pass: a low-res render target writes `(pageX, pageY, mip)`, which is read back asynchronously with `SDL_DownloadFromGPUTexture` 1–3 frames late; (2) CPU LRU page cache picks pages to (re)generate; (3) a compute or render pass composites terrain layers into a **physical page atlas** (e.g. 8k² RGBA8 ×2–3 textures: albedo, normal, ORM, with 128+2·4-texel borders for filtering); (4) an **indirection texture** (RGBA8/16 per virtual page → physical page) is updated; (5) the terrain shader does 1 indirection fetch + 1 fetch per physical texture. Total bindings: about 4 samplers. Everything needed (render-to-texture, compute storage writes, readback) exists in SDL_GPU. Hardware sparse/tiled textures (`VK_EXT_sparse`, D3D12 tiled resources, Metal sparse textures) are **not exposed** by SDL_GPU, so a software page table is required.
- **Compressing VT pages at runtime** (BC on the GPU via compute writing to a `R32G32B32A32_UINT` storage texture, then copying into a BC texture) needs a texture-to-texture copy between compatible formats. SDL_GPU may not allow copying from an uncompressed storage texture into a BC texture: **flag as unverified**. Fall back to uncompressed RGBA8 physical pages (more memory, simpler).
- **Hot reload:** because cached outputs are keyed by input hash, editing a graph parameter invalidates only the changed node and its descendants. Hashing at the node level (as in section 3) means slider tweaks re-run only a few compute dispatches, giving interactive (sub-100 ms) iteration on 1k² textures on an M4 Pro [inference; not measured].

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether SDL_GPU's `SDL_CopyGPUTextureToTexture` permits uncompressed→BC "reinterpret" copies, which runtime GPU BC compression would need.
- I did not verify which BC formats SDL_GPU exposes on Metal for Apple Silicon (expected: BC1–7 supported on Apple-silicon Macs). Query at runtime with `SDL_GPUTextureSupportsFormat`.
- The Far Cry 4 AVT slides (GDC Vault) were not readable this session. The mechanism details marked [prior] (sector sizing, indirection layout, page size) should be checked against the slides.
- No sourced generation timings were found for compute-shader texture graphs (e.g. ms per 1k² fBm octave) on Apple M-series.

## 6. Shipped games with fully or mostly procedural surfaces

### Takeaway
Shipped precedents use different mixes. **No Man's Sky** generates textures and color palettes procedurally inside a voxel → polygon → texture → population pipeline. **Teardown** has essentially no textures: 1-byte voxel material IDs into 256-entry palettes plus ray-traced AO and shadows, with detail coming from geometry. **Tiny Glade** gets wear and variation from geometry-derived attributes (SDF-to-feature vertex attributes) plus ray-traced GI. **Dreams** and **Claybook** render SDF-based worlds where the "texture" is the geometry plus per-point/per-pixel shading. The common lesson is that **stylization plus good lighting (AO/GI) carries procedural surfaces further than photoreal texture detail**.

### Cited Findings
- **No Man's Sky:** Innes McKendrick (Hello Games), "Continuous World Generation in No Man's Sky", GDC 2017, walks through the pipeline from voxel generation through polygonization and **texturing** to population and simulation — [GDC Vault 1024265](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024265/Continuous-World-Generation-in-No); [Game Developer coverage](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/video-how-continuous-world-generation-works-in-i-no-man-s-sky-i-) [search]. A third-party code analysis says a similar procedural approach generates **textures and color palettes for creatures and planets** — [Game Developer: What the code of No Man's Sky says about procedural generation](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/what-the-code-of-i-no-man-s-sky-i-says-about-procedural-generation) [search; secondary]. Related talk: "Learning to love procedural art while making No Man's Sky" — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/video-learning-to-love-procedural-art-while-making-i-no-man-s-sky-i-) [search]
- **Teardown:** an 8-bit palette per volume (≤255 materials) and 1 byte per voxel. Palettes are packed in a 256-wide texture, with load-time generation of color variants in unused slots. Ray-traced AO, soft shadows and specular occlusion; no GI — [Voxagon blog](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/12/03/spraycan.html); [SE Daily interview](https://softwareengineeringdaily.com/2025/01/02/teardown-and-voxel-based-rendering-with-dennis-gustafsson/); [acko.net frame teardown](https://acko.net/blog/teardown-frame-teardown/) [search]
- **Tiny Glade (Pounce Light: Anastasia Opara, Tomasz Stachowiak):** the first prototype was in Bevy, and its constraints led them to write their own rendering. The game began as a side project while Opara was at Embark Studios. It features ray-traced GI and reflections and "a lot of procedural technology". Window wear comes from a per-vertex signed-distance attribute — [80.lv author page / interview listing](https://80.lv/author/pounce-light); [80.lv windows article](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade); [80.lv roofs article](https://80.lv/articles/a-look-at-procedural-tower-roofs-in-tiny-glade) [search]
- **Dreams (Media Molecule):** Alex Evans gave "Learning from Failure" at Umbra Ignite 2015, a version of his SIGGRAPH 2015 talk. It surveys "unconventional and mostly abandoned renderers for Dreams on PS4" (a ~240 MB PDF of slides is linked) — [Media Molecule blog](https://www.mediamolecule.com/blog/article/alex_at_umbra_ignite_2015_learning_from_failure_video) [search]. Content (prior knowledge of the talk): user sculpts are lists of SDF edits (CSG primitives with smooth blends) evaluated on the GPU into sparse bricks. The shipped renderer draws dense **point clouds/splats** placed on the surface, with "painterly" flecks giving the look, and no texture maps or UVs — [same source](https://www.mediamolecule.com/blog/article/alex_at_umbra_ignite_2015_learning_from_failure_video) [prior]
- **Claybook (Second Order):** Sebastian Aaltonen, "GPU-based clay simulation and ray-tracing tech in Claybook", GDC 2018 Advanced Graphics Techniques tutorial. The stack is a GPU clay/fluid simulator, a fully deformable world and characters, **SDF modelling**, and **ray-traced visuals at 60 fps on current-gen consoles** — [GDC Vault 1025316](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025316/Advanced-Graphics-Techniques-Tutorial-GPU); [GPUOpen GDC 2018 presentations](https://gpuopen.com/gdc-2018-presentations/) [search]. An unverified forum claim of 4.88 Gigarays/s on Vega 64 was found and is not used here.
- **Townscaper (Oskar Stålberg)** and **Elite Dangerous (Stellar Forge)**: not researched in depth this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- For a small team targeting "good-looking without artists", the Tiny Glade / Teardown pattern beats the photoreal-texture pattern: **flat or palette albedo + procedural macro/micro noise + strong geometry detail + good AO/GI**. Ray-traced GI is not available in SDL_GPU (no ray-tracing API), but SDF/voxel-based AO, cone tracing, or screen-space GI + SDF AO are reachable with compute.
- NMS-style planet surfaces map onto: (cook) a small library of generated tileable layer sets in texture arrays; (runtime) per-biome palette remaps, triplanar/biplanar on voxel-meshed terrain, and slope/height/noise-driven layer selection per pixel.
- Teardown's "material = palette entry" model is directly applicable if the world is voxel-based. A palette texture (256 × N) plus per-object 3D textures needs no bindless, but one 3D texture per draw means many draws.
