# How shipped games and engines implement environment destruction (state 2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-06. Context: Wallhammer Zero (C17, generated-from-recipe world, tier A bit-deterministic generators, edits stored as deltas over regeneration, Jolt physics at tier B1; see wallhammerzero/docs/DECISIONS.md D28, D39, D40, D74, D79).

Source quality legend: **[P]** primary (developer docs, slides, developer's own blog, official studio/publisher article); **[I]** interview or news article quoting named developers; **[S]** secondary, journalist paraphrase or wiki; **[W]** weak (aggregator, forum, fan page). The research tooling could not render Epic's current UE5 doc pages, which load in JavaScript behind Cloudflare. The Chaos facts below come from the UE 4.27 docs and the Fields guide, which did render, plus API references.

## 1. Red Faction: Guerrilla / Geo-Mod 2.0 (Volition, 2009)

### Takeaway
RFG broke man-made structures only, not terrain. Buildings were pre-split into hidden, material-sized "shards" and held up by a real-time stress calculation, and the system was memory-bound on Xbox 360/PS3. I found no primary technical slides: the two 2009 GDC destruction talks exist but are not freely retrievable. Numbers on debris limits and ms budgets are therefore a gap.

### Cited Findings
- The engine was planned as "RED" (Realtime Environment Destruction) and renamed Geo-Mod 2.0. It allows free-form destruction of buildings and structures, but unlike the original Geo-Mod (2001), Guerrilla does **not** allow terrain destruction. [S] — [Wikipedia: Red Faction: Guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Faction:_Guerrilla)
- The original 2001 Geo-Mod was CSG subtraction: "We take a low-[polygon] shape that mimics the explosion, and that shape gets subtracted from the world" (Alan Lawrance, lead designer). They limited it by limiting ammo: "We just limited the amount of rockets or grenades that you could obtain." Early builds produced "weird little tiny triangles that would go all the way through the world" (John Slagel, lead programmer). [I] — [The Ringer, 2024](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals)
- RFG had a real-time stress system: a building buckles and falls when enough supports are removed. Per gameplay programmer Matt Gawalek, the artists' first buildings "fell down in the engine." Buildings were split into predefined chunks, which needed extra textures and memory. "We were literally stealing memory from other systems just to do basic functionality"; "Our textures were lower quality than comparable titles of that era, just to save some memory." The game supported 16 online players. [I] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals)
- Authoring (Chad Greene, Volition, on Armageddon, which reused the Guerrilla engine): "we have invisible shards that are in the buildings that don't get activated until destruction has happened". Shard size is per material: "concrete will have a different size from fiberglass". A failure case: "we had it once where shard size was so dense you could write your name into the wall," which produced hundreds of thousands of polygons. Persistence: "World anchoring basically has to do with what stays around and what doesn't after destruction"; QA found wrongly anchored geometry. Armageddon's "Nano Forge" "repairs anything man-made that can be destroyed," which extends repair tech from Guerrilla multiplayer. [I] — [Game Developer: The Destructible World: Building Red Faction: Armageddon](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-destructible-world-building-i-red-faction-armageddon-i-)
- Eric Arnold (destruction, stress and physics programmer) said the goal was to make the entire world destroyable, and that the engine reacts to exactly where, and how, you hit a building. [I] — [CBS News Q&A](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/red-faction-guerrilla-q-a/); [Daily Illini profile](https://dailyillini.com/uncategorized/2013/04/11/eric-arnold-the-man-behind-the-destruction/)
- The team says they started a tech team more than five years before launch to build the physics-based destruction engine. [I] — [Worthplaying interview, Apr 2009](https://worthplaying.com/article/2009/4/24/interviews/61103-red-faction-guerrilla-all-developer-interview/)
- GDC Vault lists a Volition session "Multiplayer Level Design in Red Faction Guerrilla" (Luke Schneider). It covers how destruction changes level flow, playtest fixes and technical constraints, and it refers to two GDC lectures by the team in 2009. [P, abstract only] — [GDC Vault 1012330](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012330/Multiplayer-Level-Design-in-Red)
- A developer comment says the "whole stress/fracture system is rather complex" and that GDC presentations exist. [W] — [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/1452450)

### Inferences
- The RFG pattern is pre-authored shard sets per material, a connectivity graph with a stress check, and anchoring to the ground. It maps directly onto kit-part buildings: each kit part already is a "chunk", and the grammar knows which parts rest on which.
- The memory pressure came from storing pre-fractured geometry and textures for every building. A generator that can produce fracture pieces on demand (deterministically, from seed plus part ID) avoids storing them at all.

### Gaps
- The 2009 GDC talks (stress model details, iteration counts, debris caps, ms budgets) could not be retrieved; GDC Vault is members-only. I found no reliable source on whether RFG used Havok for rigid bodies. One CVG quote, seen only second-hand on a forum, says they wrote their own physics engine.
- I found no source on how RFG saved destroyed buildings in single-player saves.

## 2. Battlefield / Frostbite (Destruction 1.0–4.0, Levolution, BF 2042, Battlefield 6)

### Takeaway
Frostbite destruction is **authored and part-based**. Buildings are made of destructible parts with health and "health transitions" that swap geometry, add detail meshes, decal masks, particles and debris. It is server-authoritative, with heavy bandwidth prioritization. Battlefield 6 (2025) kept this model, reworked health transitions and the networking of destruction events, and made ruins and debris piles persist in a match. It is not a general runtime fracture solver.

### Cited Findings
- **Frostbite 1 (BF Bad Company, 1943, BC2):** destroying part of a house removes a piece of geometry, adds detail meshes around the hole, adds a destruction mask, and adds particle effects and mesh debris. All three titles used a destruction-masking system that needed hand-made UV maps per destructible part. [P] — [Kihl, "Destruction Masking in Frostbite 2 using Volume Distance Fields", SIGGRAPH 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Kihl-Destruction%20in%20Frostbite(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)
- **Frostbite 2 masking:** artists place spheres (auto-generated, one per destructible part, then adjusted). The engine computes a signed volume distance field per masked geometry at about **2 m per texel**, typically **8×8×8** textures packed in atlases (Xbox 360 3D tile size 32×32×4), with no mips. The shader samples the field and early-outs outside the mask. On PS3, branching was slow ("fixed cost of 6 cycles... group of up to 1600 pixels"), so they tried deferred decals and SPU triangle culling. The culling splits the mesh into two index buffers and is recomputed only when the distance field changes. Triangle culling was fastest on PS3 but platform-specific. [P] — [Kihl, SIGGRAPH 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Kihl-Destruction%20in%20Frostbite(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)
- History: Bad Company let buildings be blown out but not brought down, and interior walls stayed up. BC2 ran on Frostbite 1.5, "Destruction 2.0", and collapsed buildings whose structural integrity was destroyed. Girders, reinforced concrete and elevated terrain stayed intact. BC2 had 32 players. BF3 (Frostbite 2, "Destruction 3.0") added lethal falling debris, and BF4 added "Levolution". Networking: "our bandwidth requirements were substantially higher than other shooters"; "Everything within 25 meters is super important, send that data"; design rule "If the player shoots at it, something should happen" (Alan Kertz, ex-DICE). [I/S] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals)
- **Battlefield 6 (2025):** Frostbite reworked the **health transition system** so game teams can customize what happens in transitions (Johan Leijon, Technical Lead, DICE). It overhauled **how destruction events are networked**, with bandwidth optimization and "prioritization and culling of part destruction and debris spawning" for 64-player matches. It made "part-based destruction and debris spawning systems more extensible" and added damage to players beneath collapsing buildings. On persistence: "We wanted the result of destruction to stay, creating interesting ruins and level-designed debris piles" (Rickard Antroia, Principal Game Designer). VFX emitters "listened to debris activation and explosion shockwaves," sharing parameters with audio. They also reworked physics fidelity for debris, terrain deformation and vehicles. [P] — [EA: Breaking new ground: How Battlefield 6 reimagines destruction](https://www.ea.com/news/how-battlefield-6-redefined-destruction)
- BF6 map design has three states, from intact to "peak destruction". DICE has a "big red button" that triggers all destruction at once to test maps in each state. Doing so caused performance problems, because maps are not designed to have every instance destroyed at once (Shashank Uchil, Jeremy Chubb). [I] — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/battlefield/battlefield-6-devs-have-a-big-button-that-blows-up-everything-on-a-map-but-it-isnt-just-for-fun-dice-uses-it-to-make-sure-maps-are-fun-in-all-three-different-states-of-destruction/)
- The technical director credits dropping last-gen consoles: "Maybe the only magic trick is that we're not on the PS4 or Xbox One any more" (Christian Buhl). [I, headline only verified] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/battlefield-6-dev-says-its-destruction-tech-is-so-delicious-for-one-simple-reason-the-only-magic-trick-is-that-were-not-on-the-ps4-or-xbox-one-any-more/). The lead producer claims "This kind of destruction... is not achievable on a generic engine" (David Sirland). This is a marketing claim, not independently tested. [I] — [Battlefield Bulletin on X](https://x.com/BFBulletin/status/1952757806173491684)

### Inferences
- Battlefield's model (parts with health, discrete damage states, authored debris) is the cheapest to network and persist. State per building is a small set of part-health integers, which is ideal as a D40 delta. Wallhammer's kit-part buildings make this the natural baseline: "part ID → damage state" is a tiny, regeneration-stable delta if part IDs come from input-space anchors (D79).
- The "big red button" test is a useful CI idea: generate a cell, apply "destroy all", and measure frame cost and nav validity.

### Gaps
- I found no public DICE talk on the destruction networking protocol (event format, join-in-progress, rubble physics sync), nor any concrete BF6 debris counts, budgets or bandwidth numbers.
- How navmesh and bots react in BF2042/BF6 after a collapse: no source found.

## 3. The Finals (Embark Studios, UE5, 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Movement, physics and destruction run **server-side** on Embark's servers, and every client sees the same result. The building model has two layers: **"Strain"**, a structural-integrity layer, and general physics for debris. Season "Prime Time" (20 Oct 2026) added a third "gradual/elastic" layer. Embark has published no engineering talk on synchronization or determinism. All mechanism details below are second-hand.

### Cited Findings
- "All of the destruction is taking place on servers Embark Studios controls, not the native hardware someone is using to play The Finals, and it will look the same for all players in a match"; Embark declined to explain how before launch. [I] — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/i-almost-dont-believe-the-destructibility-in-this-fps-is-real); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/the-finals-preview-destruction-will-make-developers-panic/)
- Embark moved movement, physics and destruction to servers. The game runs on UE5, and its destruction and physics are described as off-the-shelf engine systems that UE5 "sort of solves at least a lot of the initial problems". Artists had to build structures that stay upright, and some elements were made indestructible. [S, journalist paraphrase] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals). Note: "off-the-shelf" is the journalist's wording. Embark has not confirmed that it uses stock Chaos.
- Limits: "there is a ground level that you can't destroy"; "We kind of had to draw the line at some point. Otherwise, you would be able to just dig your way through" (Joakim Stigsson). Design rule: "if it breaks in one area, it needs to break in another area as well"; "consistency is incredibly important" for tactical use (Gustav Tilleby). [I] — [MP1st interview](https://mp1st.com/news/the-finals-interview-embark-talks-balancing-destruction-and-more)
- Oct 2026 update (video by gameplay programmer Roman Smirnov and senior game designer Tim Overvliet): the existing system has two layers, structural integrity ("Strain") and debris physics. The new **gradual** layer creates weak, dynamic connections between parts after Strain breaks them, so buildings deform (tilted floors, sagging rooms) instead of dropping as one block. Smoke and gas clouds now respect destroyed geometry, and explosions can clear smoke. [I/S] — [KitGuru, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.kitguru.net/gaming/joao-silva/embark-studios-reveals-major-destruction-system-update-for-the-finals/); [ixbt.games](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/10/05/roman-smirnov-pokazal-ulucsennoe-razrusenie-okruzeniia-v-the-finals-suter-avtorov-arc-raiders-polucit-bolsoe-obnovlenie.html)
- Before the update, components were "either held absolutely firmly or fell away completely once they exceeded their load limit". All destruction runs as server-side physics, and no server load figures were given. [S] — [Playfront](https://playfront.de/en/the-finals-zerstoerungs-update-neue-physik-ebene-ab-20-oktober/)

### Inferences
- The Finals proves the "authoritative server simulates and clients receive results" pattern works for full building collapse at 12–16 players in small arenas. Bandwidth and server CPU are the price, and Embark keeps maps small.
- The layered model (connectivity/strain → elastic links → free debris) is the same structure as Blast and Chaos (bond graph, then strain threshold, then split into rigid bodies).

### Gaps
- No primary source on: the replication method (state sync vs. events vs. determinism), whether debris is replicated or cosmetic per client, debris counts, server tick or CPU budget, or how bots navigate. I found no GDC, Unreal Fest or Embark-blog talk on destruction; searches of GDC Vault returned nothing.

## 4. Teardown (Tuxedo Labs, Dennis Gustafsson)

### Takeaway
Everything is voxel volumes on a regular grid, ray-marched with no triangles. Destruction is discrete voxel removal, so it can be made **exactly deterministic in fixed-point integers**. Multiplayer (2026) replicates **destruction commands** ("cut hole of shape S at voxel x,y,z") over a reliable ordered stream. It never sends voxel data, while rigid bodies use ordinary unreliable state sync. Saves store the full world, run-length encoded, not a diff.

### Cited Findings
- Objects are "represented and modeled as voxel volumes on a regular grid"; "there aren't any triangles involved". Collision detection was rewritten for voxels, using SIMD and multithreading. [I] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/teardown-developer-breaks-down-multiplayer-and-voxel-destruction-tech)
- **Multiplayer architecture (primary):** "destruction done deterministically, while most other things use state synchronization." The destruction logic was rewritten in fixed-point integer math because voxel volumes are discrete. Breakage becomes a command stream, e.g. "cut hole in this shape at voxel coord x,y,z", plus commands for shape ownership and reconnecting joints, some of which still use floats. Command bandwidth is small and independent of object size. Transforms, velocities and players use unreliable state sync, prioritized per client (visible objects first) within "around one Mbit per client". Clients simulate locally and snap to the server. The host is a player, with no dedicated servers. An early 2021 attempt sent changed voxel data, and large objects choked the connection. Join-in-progress replays the command stream; full scene serialization would be 30–50 MB. The command buffer is size-limited, and join-in-progress is disabled when it fills. [P] — [Gustafsson, "The unlikely story of Teardown Multiplayer", 13 Mar 2026](https://blog.voxagon.se/2026/03/13/teardown-multiplayer.html)
- **Saves:** thousands of voxel volumes, from a few hundred to millions of voxels each, with roughly **half a billion voxels** on large levels. Each save stores the entire world (not a diff) "for robustness". RLE shrinks it to "15-20% of original size", about **80 MB** quicksaves on large levels. Shipped missions are zlib-compressed initial snapshots, about **20 MB** each. Physics voxel representation and all acceleration structures are rebuilt at load, not saved. Explicit per-object save/load callbacks. [P] — [Gustafsson, "Teardown quicksave", 2020](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/11/18/teardown-quicksave.html)
- **Structural integrity:** at launch there was none: "entire buildings will often be held up by one voxel". Gustafsson spent months optimizing the connectivity check (whether the bottom of a structure still touches its top) over millions of voxels. Teardown limits level size and makes bedrock and key objects indestructible. [S] — [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/teardown-review/); [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals)
- On 1 Sep 2026 Gustafsson showed a **structural-integrity prototype**, which Teardown developer Riv Olle Patrik Lundahl confirmed as a prototype for the game. The solver is "inspired by Erin's work" (Erin Catto, Box3D). How it works is not public. [S] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/this-real-time-voxel-building-demolition-is-so-satisfying/)

### Inferences
- **This is the closest precedent for Wallhammer.** Destruction as a stream of discrete, integer-defined commands is exactly a D40/D79 delta: an ordered edit layer applied over regenerated geometry. Teardown applies commands to stored voxels. Wallhammer would apply them to regenerated geometry, so the save is the command log, or a compacted form of it, rather than Teardown's 80 MB snapshot.
- Teardown's split matches D28/D74: tier A for the shape change (integer, cross-OS identical) and tier B for rigid-body motion of debris (state-synced or local).
- The join-in-progress buffer limit is the warning: an unbounded command log needs compaction, i.e. folding commands into a per-part state.

### Gaps
- Voxel size is not stated in the primary sources I reached. Commonly cited as 10 cm, but unverified here.
- I found no source on the cost of the connectivity or structural check, or on how floats in joint reconnection are kept consistent. A promised follow-up post on cross-platform floats is not out.

## 5. Rainbow Six Siege, Control, Crackdown 3, Noita, Minecraft

### Takeaway
Siege and Control show **procedural fracture of authored surfaces**: material-driven, applied at runtime to walls, floors and props. Crackdown 3 is the cautionary tale of offloading destruction to cloud servers: Havok in Azure, limited to one multiplayer mode. Noita and Minecraft are the cell-based extremes, where destruction is just a world edit and persistence comes for free through chunk saves.

### Cited Findings
- **Siege (RealBlast, Ubisoft Montreal):** Julien L'Heureux (physics programmer) works on "procedural and prefragmented destruction tools". Procedural destruction "plays a central role and is used to change the environment and influence the game's outcome" and is "vastly more complex than simple pre-fragmented destruction". Materials "need to react logically and consistently to different stimuli". RealBlast was used earlier in Assassin's Creed IV. [I/P] — [Ubisoft News interview, Mar 2016](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/4GHX2yepSaKkflLjLAlpwO/the-art-of-destruction-in-rainbow-six-siege-an-interview-with-julien-lheureux); GDC 2016 talk "The Art of Destruction in Rainbow Six: Siege" — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023307/contactUs). A forum claim that Siege replicates a fracture seed is speculation. [W] — [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/procedural-destruction-rainbow-six-siege-style/81885)
- **Control (Remedy, Northlight):** GDC Summer 2020 "Destructible Environments in Control: Lessons in Procedural Destruction" (Johannes Richter, principal VFX artist, Houdini workflow). [P, abstract] — [GDC Vault 1030643](https://gdcvault.com/play/1030643/Destructible-Environments-in-Control-Lessons). Second-hand summary: Northlight runs destruction logic and decides which events and particles react, NVIDIA PhysX simulates rigid bodies, compound objects (a door with a hole) stay one object via internal connections, and physics behavior keys off material names. [W] — [tech-en.netlify.app write-up](https://tech-en.netlify.app/articles/en526574)
- **Crackdown 3:** "What we're doing in Wrecking Zone is we're running Havok in Azure", about "12 Xbox Ones" of compute "to do this lockstep full destruction" (Joseph Staten). Full destruction existed only in the multiplayer Wrecking Zone mode, with fixed environments in the campaign. A claimed 2–4 Mbps bandwidth is unverified. [I/S] — [Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/inside-crackdown-3s-azure-cloud-powered-destruction); [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-chips/crackdown-3-uses-cloud-to-compute-massive-amounts-of-destruction); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/64701/crackdown-3-uses-cloud-tech-12-xbox-ones-power/newsletter/index.html)
- **Noita:** a pixel falling-sand world. The world is split into **64×64 chunks**, each with a dirty rect, and falling chunks become rigid bodies. [S] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation/); talk: [GDC 2019 "Exploring the Tech and Design of Noita" (Petri Purho)](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025695/Exploring-the-Tech-and-Design). Checkerboard multithreaded chunk updates are reported second-hand. [W] — [BeefSand README](https://github.com/BradFitz66/BeefSand)
- **Minecraft:** chunks are 16×16 blocks and 384 high in the Overworld (98,304 blocks), in 16-block sections. They are generated from the seed when first needed, but "entity movement can cause slight variations". Ticking is limited by load level and distance (chunk ticks within 128 blocks of a player in Java). [S/wiki] — [Minecraft Wiki: Chunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk)
- **BattleBit Remastered:** up to 254 players with destructible environments, using a forgiving low-poly style. The smallest wall fragments shrank from large chunks to single bricks. [S] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals)

### Inferences
- Minecraft's model (generate from seed; once modified, store the whole chunk) is the opposite of D40: it stores full state after the first touch. Wallhammer's design (command deltas over regeneration) is more storage-efficient but needs the anchor and orphan rules (D79) that Minecraft avoids.
- Crackdown 3 shows that shifting destruction to remote compute did not scale to the whole game. Keep destruction gameplay local and bounded (D79 "bounded work").

### Gaps
- No primary technical numbers for Siege (fragment counts, sync method), Control (debris budgets), Noita (thread counts, chunk-to-rigid-body conversion via marching squares is folklore, not verified) or Crackdown 3.

## 6. Engines and middleware: UE5 Chaos, Havok Destruction, NVIDIA Blast, and Jolt

### Takeaway
The industry-standard model, in Chaos and Blast alike, is **pre-fractured hierarchical chunks plus a bond/connection graph**. A damage model (strain thresholds per level or bond health) breaks bonds, connected components split into new rigid bodies, and sleep or removal culls debris. Blast is physics-agnostic, has a stateless C API and an iterative bond stress solver, and is BSD-style licensed. It is the most directly reusable design for a C17 engine. **Jolt has no fracture or destruction module.** It does provide the primitives: mutable compound shapes, breakable-constraint pattern, cross-platform determinism, and state save/restore.

### Cited Findings
**UE Chaos Destruction**
- Geometry Collections are built from static meshes. Fracture methods: Uniform, Clustered, Radial, Planar and Slice Voronoi, plus Brick, with sub-fracturing. Cluster levels: level 0 is the whole object, and "you will want a small number of clusters (or bigger pieces) on Level 1". Max Cluster Level caps breakage. Each level has a **Damage Threshold** (strain needed to break). Collision is analytic or level-set; level sets are accurate but memory "scales cubically". "you might not want thousands of pieces on the initial break." A **Cache System** records simulations to replay kinematically, for background or non-interactive destruction. [P] — [Epic: Chaos Destruction Overview (UE 4.27)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-destruction-overview?application_version=4.27)
- **Fields:** Anchor fields set bones static "until a Strain force is applied". They must be applied at construction because "the connection graph must also be established prior to simulation". External Strain "breaks the connections between the bones" when above the damage threshold. Internal strain decays thresholds over time. Sleep and Disable fields stop bodies below a velocity threshold, and disabled bodies "cannot be reawakened" and are removed. [P] — [Epic: Chaos Fields User Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-fields-user-guide-in-unreal-engine)
- `remove_on_max_sleep` removes particles and dissolves render geometry after the sleep threshold, and `scale_on_removal` shrinks them. The damage threshold is an array per cluster level. [P] — [Unreal Python API 5.4: GeometryCollection](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/GeometryCollection?application_version=5.4)
- **Replication:** GeometryCollectionComponent has a replication toggle and a "replication abandon cluster level", below which corrections stop being sent for chunks. [P] — [UE 4.27 API](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Runtime/GeometryCollectionEngine/GeometryCollection/UGeometryCollect-/index.html). In 5.3, by default the initial velocity at destruction is replicated and clients predict the final fragment positions. The cvar `p.bGeometryCollectionRepUseClusterVelocityMatch 0` restores exact position replication. [W, community] — [UE forum 5.3](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/5-3-chaos-destruction-replication/1326436)
- Community advice: avoid huge collections and use one per element, so the root proxy renders the intact mesh with normal LOD and instancing. [W] — [UE forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ue-5-5-chaos-destruction/2062915)

**NVIDIA Blast** (successor of APEX Destruction)
- Three layers: NvBlast (low-level), NvBlastTk (toolkit), and NvBlastExt extensions. Low-level: "C-style API consisting of stateless functions, with no global framework or context"; "Functions do not spawn tasks, allocate, or deallocate memory"; damage "completely defined by user-supplied 'shader' functions"; "portable memory layout... memcopy cloning and direct binary serialization". It has "no physics or collision representation" and "no graphics representation". ExtAuthoring splits meshes hierarchically. Serialization uses Cap'n Proto. [P] — [Blast docs index](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/index.rst)
- Concepts: chunks in hierarchies, a **support graph** of support chunks and bonds, actors, and families. "Exact coverage" means every leaf-to-root walk hits exactly one support chunk. Damage is staged: generate fracture commands, apply them, then split into new actors. [P] — [Blast definitions](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/api/definitions.rst); [low-level guide](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/api/api_ll_users_guide.rst)
- **Stress solver (ExtStress):** "iteratively spreading forces across bonds that connect nodes of the support graph", with separate limits for tension, compression and shear. Gravity needs at least one zero-mass (fixed) node, otherwise the structure behaves as if in free fall. Collision impulses can be fed in so objects break "in a weak place instead of the place of contact". "Computation time is linearly proportional to the maxSolverIterationsPerFrame setting". Graph reduction is experimental. It uses only core NvBlast, with no physics library needed. [P] — [Blast ExtStress docs](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/api/extensions/ext_stress.rst)
- Status: v5.1.0 (28 Sep 2026) builds without PhysX by default. It is still maintained, with a BSD-style license (blast-sdk-LICENSE.md). [P] — [Blast CHANGELOG](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/CHANGELOG.md); [repo](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast)

**Havok Destruction**
- Introduced around 2008 and now listed as discontinued. I found no official end-of-life notice. [S/W] — [Wikipedia: Havok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havok_(software)); licensed by Saber in 2009 — [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development/timeshift-dev-taking-on-havok-destruction)

**Jolt Physics**
- No fracture module. Asked for real-time fracture, Jorrit Rouwe replied: "I was planning on making a demo for this someday, but this is not going to happen anytime soon." [P] — [Jolt discussion #1722](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/discussions/1722)
- Relevant primitives: **StaticCompoundShape** "cannot be changed afterwards" (tree-accelerated), while **MutableCompoundShape** "can be constructed/changed at runtime and trades construction time for runtime performance". Removing a child invalidates sub-shape IDs. **Breakable constraints** work by checking the total lambda (impulse) after each step and calling `SetEnabled(false)` above a threshold. [P] — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)
- **Determinism:** the CMake option `CROSS_PLATFORM_DETERMINISTIC` makes Jolt "approximately 8% slower" but deterministic across compiler, OS, architecture and word size, given the same source and defines. Modifying APIs must be called in exactly the same order. Broadphase queries and listener callback order are **not** deterministic. `SaveState` records only what the step modifies. `RestoreState` breaks if bodies were added or removed in rolled-back frames. Stable body IDs can be kept with `CreateBodyWithID`. [P] — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html). A draft PR adds deterministic body IDs when restoring state. [P] — [Jolt PR #2073](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/pull/2073)
- Jolt advises that a partially synced scene must treat non-synced "destructibles" as kinematic relative to synced bodies, "or else they will affect the simulation and break determinism". [P] — [Jolt discussion #1254](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/discussions/1254)

### Inferences
- For Wallhammer: implement a Blast-style **bond graph and stress solver in tier A**, in fixed-point/detmath inside `eng_sim` or a generator-adjacent module. It decides deterministically *which* bonds break. Hand only the resulting free pieces to Jolt (tier B1) as debris. The gameplay-relevant result, which parts are gone, then stays bit-identical cross-OS and storable as a delta. Debris motion becomes cosmetic and need not be saved.
- Blast's low-level design (stateless, no allocation, POD memory layout) fits the project's C17, arena and handle rules. It can serve as a reference design, or be wrapped as an adapter, since its license appears permissive (verify).
- Intact buildings should be a few static compound bodies. Convert a part to dynamic only when its bond component detaches. A MutableCompoundShape suits a damaged-but-standing building, where children are removed as parts break.

### Gaps
- Current UE 5.5–5.8 Chaos doc pages (connection graph details, ISM/Nanite rendering of geometry collections, Dataflow fracture, damage model "use size and material") could not be retrieved: JS rendering plus Cloudflare. No Epic numbers for piece counts or ms budgets.
- I found no published benchmark of the Blast stress solver (ms per N bonds).

## 7. Cross-cutting: gameplay vs cosmetic, persistence, networking/determinism, navmesh/AI, LOD/rendering, budgets

### Takeaway
Every shipped system separates **authoritative structural state**, which is small, discrete and gameplay-relevant (part health, bond broken or not, voxel removed or not), from **debris**, which is large, physical and usually cosmetic, culled by sleep, removal or prioritization. Networked games either run the destruction server-side and replicate results (Battlefield, The Finals, Crackdown 3) or replicate deterministic commands (Teardown). No source documented how navmesh and AI handle destruction in these titles.

### Cited Findings
- **Gameplay vs cosmetic:** Teardown syncs destruction deterministically but debris rigid bodies only by best-effort state sync, and clients "snap" visibly. [P] — [Teardown Multiplayer](https://blog.voxagon.se/2026/03/13/teardown-multiplayer.html). Chaos stops replicating corrections below an "abandon" cluster level. [P] — [UE 4.27 API](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Runtime/GeometryCollectionEngine/GeometryCollection/UGeometryCollect-/index.html). BF6 prioritizes and culls "part destruction and debris spawning" on the network. [P] — [EA](https://www.ea.com/news/how-battlefield-6-redefined-destruction). Frostbite masking, detail meshes and particles are pure rendering on top of a part removal. [P] — [Kihl 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Kihl-Destruction%20in%20Frostbite(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf)
- **Persistence:** BF6 keeps ruins and debris piles for the match. [P] — [EA](https://www.ea.com/news/how-battlefield-6-redefined-destruction). Teardown stores full RLE world snapshots, about 80 MB. [P] — [quicksave](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/11/18/teardown-quicksave.html). RF Armageddon has "world anchoring" deciding what stays, and repair via Nano Forge. [I] — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-destructible-world-building-i-red-faction-armageddon-i-). Chaos debris is removed on sleep. [P] — [UE Python API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/GeometryCollection?application_version=5.4)
- **Networking/determinism:** Teardown uses fixed-point deterministic commands plus state sync at about 1 Mbit per client, and join-in-progress by command replay. [P] — [Teardown Multiplayer](https://blog.voxagon.se/2026/03/13/teardown-multiplayer.html). Crackdown 3 ran Havok in Azure, "lockstep full destruction". [I] — [Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/inside-crackdown-3s-azure-cloud-powered-destruction). Jolt cross-platform determinism costs about 8% and requires identical API call order. [P] — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/index.html)
- **Navmesh/AI:** the only evidence is anecdotal. A UE developer reports about a 0.5 s hitch when the navmesh regenerates after objects are destroyed, and suggests partial regeneration. [W] — [UE forum: Navmesh Regeneration Lag](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/navmesh-regeneration-lag/451257)
- **Budgets/limits:** "you might not want thousands of pieces on the initial break" (Epic). Stress solver cost is linear in iterations (Blast). Teardown has about 0.5 billion voxels per large level. RFG shard density reached hundreds of thousands of polygons, a known failure. BF6 "all destruction at once" broke performance. Sources: [Epic](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/chaos-destruction-overview?application_version=4.27), [Blast](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Blast/blob/master/docs/api/extensions/ext_stress.rst), [Teardown quicksave](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/11/18/teardown-quicksave.html), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-destructible-world-building-i-red-faction-armageddon-i-), [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/battlefield/battlefield-6-devs-have-a-big-button-that-blows-up-everything-on-a-map-but-it-isnt-just-for-fun-dice-uses-it-to-make-sure-maps-are-fun-in-all-three-different-states-of-destruction/)
- **Scope limits everyone uses:** indestructible ground (The Finals, Teardown bedrock), no terrain destruction (RFG), girders and reinforced concrete stay (BC2), small maps (The Finals, Teardown). [I/S] — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/the-finals-interview-embark-talks-balancing-destruction-and-more); [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2024/02/02/video-games/destruction-video-games-battlefield-bad-company-red-faction-battlebit-teardown-the-finals); [Wikipedia RFG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Faction:_Guerrilla)

### Inferences (for Wallhammer Zero)
- **Recommended shape:** a two-tier destruction model aligned with D28/D74.
  1. Tier A, gameplay-relevant: a bond graph between kit parts, emitted by the building grammar. Damage and stress are evaluated in integer or fixed-point math. Results are "part P broken / damage state k", stored as an edit delta keyed by input-space anchors (D79 rule 1).
  2. Tier B1/cosmetic: debris rigid bodies in Jolt, fracture geometry generated from (part uid, seed) on demand, removed on sleep, never saved. Rubble piles that matter for gameplay (cover, blocked paths) would be a tier A "rubble state" per part, regenerated as static geometry, as in BF6's persistent debris piles.
- **The open-world twist none of the sources solves:** a cell that streams out and back in must regenerate its building and then re-apply the destruction delta. This is Teardown's command replay, but with the D79 orphan queue for when a generator version changes the building.
- A destroyed region needs a navmesh tile re-bake for AI. No shipped source documents this, so plan a bounded local re-bake, consistent with D79 "bounded work".

### Gaps
- No primary source anywhere on navmesh or AI response to destruction in these titles (Siege, BF, The Finals bots).
- No shipped open-world game documented that saves building destruction over a regenerated world. Closest: Teardown (full snapshot), Minecraft (full chunk after edit), RFG (unknown).
- Frame-time budgets for destruction (ms) are absent from every primary source found, apart from PS3 masking GPU costs, which were in a table not extractable as text.
