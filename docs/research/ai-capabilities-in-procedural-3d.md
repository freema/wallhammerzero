# AI builds the engine and the city, the eye stays yours

Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code writes an engine in C17 on top of SDL_GPU and most of the graphics generators. The bottleneck will not be code. It will be visual judgment. In 2026 the runnability of code that generates 3D is practically solved. In the 3DCodeBench benchmark, Claude Opus 4.7 has the highest runnability of all models (0.910), and with the traceback fed back it reaches 1.000. In human evaluation of appearance, however, Claude models end up in the middle of the field, and Opus's typical flaw is "simplified floating primitives" ([3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057)). For your quality bar this gives the following split. **City, roads, terrain, buildings, props, light and atmosphere are strong categories.** They reach the GTA III bar and exceed it in lighting. **Vehicles, vegetation and the hand-painted WoW-style look (the latter through generated images with code post-processing) are doable for AI with iterations.** **Characters, faces, animals and animation "feel" are risky.** Without your parametric templates and your eye they stay below the bar. Your role is art director with veto power: you decide style, proportions, references and the acceptance of every asset family. Vision models, Claude included, do not reliably see proportions, overlaps or timing, and they overrate their own output. The workflow must therefore stand first on oracles: numeric lints, deterministic golden hashes and headless multi-view renders. Only then comes a separate judge that sees only pixels and references, and finally you. The MacBook Pro M4 Pro with the 16-core GPU is at the level of an RTX 4050 Laptop. It can run a stylized textured open world **at 60 fps with an internal resolution of 1512×982 up to 1080p and a custom upscale**. Native Retina 3024×1964 at 60 fps in a dense city is not realistic. The engine should stay within ≤6 GB of GPU memory and 2–3 GB of CPU memory. You cannot test D3D12 locally, and hosted macOS CI has no GPU. Textures are made as a hybrid, as you decided. Every material has either a procedural program or a once-generated image stored in the repository with recorded provenance. Both sources go through the same code post-processing and the same lints, review and approval. The hybrid pipeline is also the shortest path to a hand-painted look at the WoW bar, which code alone cannot reliably deliver.

## Writing code is solved, seeing is not: what the 2025–2026 benchmarks say

The most important finding of the research is the difference between two kinds of feedback. In 3DCodeBench (Google DeepMind and USC, May 2026), a vision-language model writes a Blender script for objects from 212 Infinigen categories. **A loop over error messages raised runnability from 0.702 to 0.974**, and Opus 4.7, GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 reached 1.000. **A visual self-critique loop brought only +0.003 to +0.009 SigLIP-2 points** for text prompts. For image prompts it was even slightly negative, because fixing one leg can worsen the overall impression ([3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057)). The height of the thinking budget for Pro-class models matters less than 5 points (same source). "Think harder" therefore does not fix visual flaws. Shape quality differs little between models (SigLIP-2 0.761–0.834), and the new model generation buys mainly reliability, not fidelity. Another figure is crucial too: **85–90% of failures were caused by Blender API drift**. The engine's own stable API, which the model has in context, removes this class of errors completely.

The second weakness is spatial composition. Code4Scene (September 2026, 190 tasks in Unreal) rated it the weakest family of requirements across all 14 tested configurations. The best average was only 0.543. In 20.6% of cases the model had the right entities in the wrong relations, and **35.8% of "complete fixes" also changed unrelated content** ([Code4Scene](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36777v1)). Claude Opus 5 reached 0.718 there when building a scene, but only 0.468 when editing it. Editing existing content is much harder for a model than building from scratch, and the workflow must account for that. BlenderGym (CVPR 2025) showed that in 2025 humans beat every model in all five tasks, most of all in deformations and organic geometry: the blend shape error was roughly 10× higher than a human's ([BlenderGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01786)). WorldBench (October 2026) adds that elements with motion scored 47% and static ones 70%. **No model implemented small life (undergrowth, ferns, pollinators).** Code and screenshots contradicted each other in 32% of the requested items, and a judge that read only the code awarded 5.44 out of 7.11 points to features that had been deliberately removed ([WorldBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10622v1)).

Model-based evaluation of renders has known limits. In the test "Vision language models are blind", the best model reached 77.84% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) on tasks such as "do two circles overlap", where humans score around 100%. The authors show that the information is in the vision encoder, but the language model cannot decode it ([BlindTest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581v6)). The research found no newer measurement for Claude 5.x models, so it is not known whether this gap has closed. In BlenderGym, a model as verifier agreed with humans in 0.66 of cases, while humans agreed with each other in 0.79 and chance gives 0.5 ([BlenderGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01786)). **Pairwise comparison with a checklist**, on the other hand, works well. GPT-4V over a multi-view render and normal renders "strongly matches human preferences" ([GPTEval3D](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Wu_GPT-4Vision_is_a_Human-Aligned_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)), and ArtifactsBench reports over 90% pairwise agreement with experts ([ArtifactsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04952)). Asset Forge offers a practical lever: when the judge received an exact text representation (an ASCII grid) next to the PNG, its judgment of spatial properties improved "dramatically" ([Asset Forge](https://matthewdeaves.github.io/assetforge)). For motion the state is worse. According to a NeurIPS 2026 study, VLM video judges do not react enough to physically significant temporal errors, while they do react to superficial visual cues ([NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/175831)).

Practice confirms the same. The publicly known 3D games built with AI are clean low-poly scenes made of primitives, and their extra polish comes from non-code assets. Simon Willison had Claude Fable 5 create the game "Raccoon Heist" in 34 minutes. The characters were made of spheres and cylinders, **but seven textures and the title image were generated by gpt-image-2**. The agent also called a cropped title image, in which the main hero was missing, "gorgeous". Willison's verdict: "impressive starting point, but it's not a *good* game" ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/aug/5/raccoon-heist)). Among the sponsors of Vibe Jam 2026 is Tripo AI, a generator of 3D models from images ([Vibe Jam](https://vibejam.com/2026/press)). A review of Opus 5 launch demos says the model "sped up implementation but did not solve the pipeline of custom 3D assets" ([SoonLab](https://www.soonlab.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-game-development/)). Dave Snider describes that Claude "struggles with visual spatial reasoning in 3D" and that it once invented the content of a binary STL file that it claimed to have read ([Snider](https://davesnider.com/claude-3D)). The closest analogy to your project is citygen, a procedural city generator "built with Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code" that a human "designed and directed". It has a seeded core (spec → mesh), seven historical Baltic house styles, interiors and a headless CLI with tests ([citygen](https://github.com/tomasliubinas/citygen)). **The research found no publicly documented, AI-written native C/C++ engine with fully procedural graphics.** You are therefore moving beyond the available evidence, and the estimates below are extrapolations.

The engine code itself is feasible if it has oracles. Anthropic had 16 parallel Opus 4.6 instances build roughly 100,000 lines of a C compiler in Rust. The project took about 2,000 sessions and just under 20,000 USD, and the compiler builds Linux 6.9 and Doom. The result, however, stood on almost perfect verifiers (test suites, GCC as the oracle), and in the late phase fixes broke other features ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)). Graphics has no such oracle for "looks good", which is why you must build part of it yourself (see the workflow section). A specific Opus 5.5 benchmark for generating 3D with code does not exist. An aggregator reports that Opus 5.5 was released on 22 September 2026 and performs roughly like Claude Fable 5.1 ([technews.tw](https://technews.tw/2026/09/23/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-opus-5-5-model/)). Fable 5.1 leads WorldBench (210.79/280) and scene building in Code4Scene (0.788). Opus 5.5 is therefore probably at the top in code correctness, but it will need visual guidance just like its predecessors. One practical note: according to reports, Claude Code with Opus 5.5 has safeguards against copying specific existing games ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-confirms-new-claude-code-limits-to-prevent-game-cloning-users-report-opus-5-5-is-also-actively-blocking-reverse-engineering-tasks)). In prompts, therefore, describe your own open world with a quality bar, not a "GTA clone".

## City and light above the GTA III bar, characters below it

Both reference bars have a low polygon count, and their look is carried mainly by textures and art direction, not by geometry. That plays in favor of code. Triangle budgets from 2001–2004 are easy for code to meet, so the difficulty lies in surfaces and characters. Hard-surface and structured objects are the zone where programs work best. Nova3D, where "the program is the asset", satisfied 51 of 52 numeric constraints, with the best baseline at only 11, and it wins structured domains. However, it concedes texture realism to systems with baked PBR ([Nova3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22738)). Complex organic categories (a bird in flight, a crab, a dragonfly) need over 400 lines of reference code, and models fail on them more often ([3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057)). A practical test with Blender MCP says it directly: primitives, transforms and basic lighting work, while organic shapes, characters, animation curves, rigging and "judgment of proportions and aesthetics" reliably fail or need heavy corrections ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-blender-mcp-real-world-performance)).

| Category | Rating | Expected quality against the GTA III / WoW bar | Main risk and what contains it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine core (RHI, jobs, streaming, math) | **strong** | invisible; a precondition for everything | math conventions, regressions; contained by unit tests, TSan, null GPU, replay |
| Shaders: lighting, CSM shadows, fog, post | **strong** | **above GTA III** (modern shadows, fog, bloom) | backend-specific bugs, register layout; contained by compile → retry, validation layers, per-backend golden images |
| Terrain, relief, rocks | **strong** | at the bar and above | cell seams, erosion only offline; contained by hash seeds and golden hashes |
| Road network, blocks, lots | **strong** | at the GTA III bar | spatial composition → topology errors; contained by graph invariants (connectivity, widths, angles) |
| Buildings (CGA-style facade grammars) | **strong** | silhouette and mass at the bar; surface depends on the texture source | "oatmeal" (all houses the same); contained by district style parameters and human-chosen landmarks |
| Street furniture, props | **strong** to doable with iterations | at the bar | floating and disconnected parts; contained by a connectivity lint and dimensional specifications |
| Vehicles | **doable with iterations** | GTA III reachable if a human approves the silhouette | proportions, "boxiness"; contained by a parametric template and reference views |
| Vegetation | **doable with iterations** | stylized trees at the bar, dense undergrowth below it | dense organics (WorldBench); contained by space colonization, a seed library, impostors |
| Stochastic materials (stone, soil, concrete, asphalt, rust) | **strong** | at the GTA III bar | "noisy" look; contained by histogram-preserving tiling and macro variation |
| Structured materials (brick, paving, windows, roofing) | **doable with iterations** | at the bar | visible repetition; contained by per-cell hashing and masks from the generator |
| Hand-painted WoW-style look | **doable with iterations** (generated image + code post-processing); risky with code alone | close to the WoW bar with the hybrid pipeline | style consistency across a batch; contained by palette remap and LUT, shared masks and human approval |
| Light, atmosphere, day cycle, night windows | **strong** | **above GTA III** | taste in tone mapping; the LUT is tuned by a human |
| Human bodies | **risky** | GTA III level only with a human-made template | organics, proportions; contained by a skeleton → body generator and anthropometric constants |
| Faces | **risky (most)** | below the WoW bar | expression, uncanny valley; contained by stylization and a small set of blendshapes |
| Locomotion (walk, run, foot IK) | **doable with iterations** | GTA III level reachable | timing and "feel", foot sliding; contained by metrics and your playtest |
| Physical reactions (active ragdoll) | **risky** | optional | stability and tuning; contained by Jolt motors and metrics |
| Animals | **risky** | below the bar | variety of morphology; one locomotion class per type |
| Scene composition, variability, art direction | **human** | determines the overall impression | AI will not decide this |

The strong categories are strong because they are programmatic structure that models know from the literature: CGA split grammars for buildings ([Müller et al. 2006](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1141911.1141931)), tensor fields and OBB lot subdivision, dithered placement as in Horizon Zero Dawn ([Guerrilla](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)) and WFC for bounded domains such as facades ([Townscaper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). The weakness in spatial composition from Code4Scene is neutralized here by the fact that composition is not free placement. It is the result of rules whose invariants can be tested with numbers. CityGrow, a Cities: Skylines-style city written by Opus 5 in roughly 4 hours and 178.77 USD, had rivers, zoning, lit windows and cars with lights. At the same time, cars drove through each other and the specification went stale ([CityGrow](https://note.com/tank_ai/n/n3be99a4ad80e?hl=en)). That is the typical profile: the systems work, while details and consistency need supervision.

Textures are the category where the bar is decided. At native resolution and with a free camera, materials need 512²–1024² with mips. Code has a solid arsenal for this. Histogram-preserving tiling and blending by Heitz and Neyret synthesizes an unlimited area from a stochastic sample without ghosting and loss of contrast, but only for "random" materials such as moss, granite, sand or bark ([Heitz & Neyret](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/)). Tiny Glade makes plaster wear around windows from a single vertex attribute with the signed distance to the window edge, with no UVs and no baked texture ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade)). This is exactly the pattern "the generator knows the geometry, so it hands out masks for free". The weak spot is elsewhere. Academic systems where an LLM or VLM writes material graphs achieve only plausible approximations and explicitly fail where the target needs a complex graph ([VLMaterial](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18623v2)). The research found no human-evaluation study of LLM procedural materials at all. Hence: stone, concrete, asphalt and rust are delivered by code at GTA III level. A painterly, hand-stylized WoW-style surface is not something code alone can reliably deliver. That is why the pipeline has a second source, described in the next section.

Characters are the riskiest because they combine both weaknesses, organic shape and motion timing. A manageable path exists and is purely code-based. Overgrowth built fluid motion from a very small number of key poses, springs and IK ([GDC Vault, Rosen 2014](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020049/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)). Holden's critically damped springs are closed-form formulas that a model rewrites into C without errors ([Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). Jolt has joint motors and ragdolls, so an "Euphoria-lite" layer for reactions is feasible ([Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/)). Euphoria itself has not been licensed since 2017 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software))). Generate the body first as a skeleton from parameters and only then as a mesh around it. Skinning weights then follow from the construction, because the generator knows which primitive belongs to which bone. This sidesteps rigging, where AI fails the most. **But you must set the proportions, silhouette and "character" of the figure yourself**, as numbers and reference views. The model alone ends up with floating primitives. The realistic ceiling without image models is a GTA III-level character: low polygon count, readable silhouette, simple face. Expressive WoW-style characters are a risk, not a plan.

Rejecting pixel art is correct technically, not just aesthetically. Low-resolution stabilization by snapping works only for an orthographic camera. For perspective, "no single snap fixes all depths", and the proposed solution (Texel Splatting, 2026) has open trade-offs ([arXiv 2603.14587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14587)). The risk of "10,000 bowls of oatmeal" remains: mathematically unique but perceptually identical variants ([Emily Short](https://emshort.blog/2016/09/21/bowls-of-oatmeal-and-text-generation/)). Its solution is a design one. Variability needs to be tied to meaning, that is, to district identity, landmarks and gameplay. Stylization with a flat or limited palette holds cohesion, as the small teams behind Astroneer and Sable confirm, who compensated for the loss of texture detail with light, fog and lines ([Astroneer](https://blog.astroneer.space/?p=7); [Sable](https://gamedeveloper.com/gdc2022/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)).

## Textures from code and from an image model, always through one post-processing

You decided that both sources are allowed. The evidence supports this choice. Practice shows that **polished AI games got their gloss mainly from generative image and 3D models**: Willison used gpt-image-2 for textures and the title image ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/aug/5/raccoon-heist)), and Tripo sponsors Vibe Jam 2026 ([Vibe Jam](https://vibejam.com/2026/press)). Research in turn shows that LLM-written materials are plausible only for shallow graphs ([VLMaterial](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18623v2)) and that code-native 3D loses in texture realism ([Nova3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22738)). The hybrid pipeline **does not change the engine architecture**. Texture arrays by format, BC/ASTC compression, hash-keyed caches and derived maps are the same for both sources. Only the first node of the graph differs: a program with a seed, or an image from the repository.

The pipeline has three fixed rules. **First, every material has a declared source** in the manifest: `source = procedural` (program, parameters, seed), or `source = generated` with a full provenance record (model and its version, prompt, seed if the model returns one, date, references used and the hash of the source file). A generated image is a cooked source asset. It is committed and becomes the source of truth, because you cannot regenerate the same output again. You must verify the license terms of the models used and the provenance of the content yourself, the research did not address them. **Second, a generated image never goes into the game directly**, it always goes through code post-processing that keeps a unified style and variability. That has five steps: (a) make the image tileable, for stochastic surfaces with histogram-preserving tiling and blending, which works exactly for "random" materials such as moss, granite, sand or bark ([Heitz & Neyret](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/)); (b) remap colors to the palette and color grading from the style bible; (c) derive height, normal and roughness from the albedo, which is an approximation but suffices for a stylized look; (d) add procedural wear and dirt masks from geometry, as Tiny Glade does from a vertex attribute with the distance to the edge ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade)); (e) create cheap variants through hue shift and masks instead of further generations. Post-processing is a program like any other, so the output is deterministic and cached by `hash(source image ‖ recipe ‖ parameters)`. **Third, both sources go through the same lints, the same separate judge and the same human gate.** For images, three lints are added: a seam measurement after wrap (difference of opposite edges), a histogram distance from the palette and a test for baked low-frequency lighting (a brightness gradient across the whole texture). The latter catches shadows and highlights that do not belong in a texture. Willison's metal texture was probably applied wrongly and the agent did not notice ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/aug/5/raccoon-heist)). That is why the application onto the mesh (texel density, orientation) is checked too, not just the image itself.

| Material category | Recommended source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Stochastic natural and urban surfaces: stone, concrete, asphalt, soil, sand, gravel | **procedural** | a strong zone of code; unlimited variability via seed, no seams |
| Structured regular surfaces: brick, paving, planks, roofing, window grids | **procedural** | exact pattern geometry, per-cell variation via hash, tied to dimensions from the building generator |
| Terrain layers and splat masks, wear, dirt, moss, rust as overlays | **procedural** | must follow geometry and share masks; not possible from an image |
| Hand-painted WoW-style "hero" look: plasters with brush strokes, stylized wood, landmark stone | **generated** + post-processing | code cannot reliably deliver the painterly layer |
| Bark and leaf cards of vegetation | **hybrid**: leaf shape and atlas by code, painterly detail from an image | leaf geometry is code, surface painting is painterly |
| Fabrics, clothing, character skin, stylized faces | **generated** + palette remap | organic and painterly drawing is the weakest zone of code |
| Posters, graffiti, logos, ads, paintings | **generated** | unique graphics without repetition; consumes an atlas, not the material array |
| Text on signs and markers | **procedural** (font rasterization) + optionally a generated background | exact and localizable text; text in generated images is unreliable |
| Sky and distant background | both; procedural sky + optionally a generated panorama | the day cycle requires a parametric sky |

The main risk of the hybrid is style inconsistency across batches of images. According to practice, consistent characters and environments "still need additional tools and human guidance" ([SoonLab](https://www.soonlab.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-game-development/)). The pipeline answers with three safeguards: remap to a common palette and LUT, shared procedural masks across both sources and your approval of every family. Generated images have a second use that costs nothing extra: they serve as **references for the separate judge** even for purely procedural materials. Godogen compares screenshots with generated references ([Godogen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htdt/godogen/master/README.md)), and BlenderAlchemy anchors a text prompt with "imagined" reference images ([BlenderAlchemy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17672v3)).

## The human decides style, proportions and acceptance

The evidence defines the human role precisely. VLMs are reliable in semantic and pairwise judgments ("does the render contain X", "is A closer to the reference than B"), but not in exact geometry, counting nearby objects and timing. Aesthetic models lean toward the generally popular style, and smaller models toward stronger stereotypes ([AesBiasBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11620v1)). The taste of Opus 4.7 that Anthropic claims concerns interfaces and slides, not stylized 3D ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-7)). The following decisions result, which the agent must not make for you:

| Decision | When | Why not AI |
|---|---|---|
| Style bible: palette, albedo ranges, texel density, silhouette rules, degree of proportion stylization | before the first generator | aesthetic judgment; VLMs lean toward a generic style |
| A set of references per category (photographs, screenshots, generated reference images) | before every asset family | without a target the judge has nothing to compare against |
| Numeric proportions of characters, vehicles and city scale (floor height, street widths, body ratios) | within the family specification | the model neither sees nor creates proportions reliably |
| Math conventions (handedness, quaternion order, winding, NDC depth range, UV origin) | once, at the start | plausible errors in code that show up only visually |
| Acceptance of every asset family into the library; approved renders become golden references | after each iteration | the authoring agent overrates itself |
| Animation feel: timing, weight, anticipation | playtest | VLMs do not see temporal errors |
| Variability priorities: district identity, landmarks, rare hero variants | world design | "oatmeal" is a design problem |
| Source of each material (program or image) and approval of the provenance record | when specifying a material family | a choice by category and your bar; AI will not guard style consistency |
| Performance trade-offs (internal resolution, cascades, density) | after measurements 1–3 | the quality vs. smoothness trade-off is a product decision |
| When to stop iterating | continuously | the model cannot recognize "good enough" |

## Workflow: oracles first, then eyes, then you

The workflow follows from the main asymmetry: error and numeric feedback works reliably, visual self-critique only weakly. Convert every question that can be turned into a number into a number. Leave pixels only for what a number cannot express. Successful practical loops had the same elements. The agent itself controls the camera, places debug markers and reads the machine state of the scene alongside the renders ([Snider](https://davesnider.com/claude-3D)). The principle "proof over claims" applies, and visual QA is done by an agent that sees only screenshots, never code ([Godogen](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/htdt/godogen/master/README.md)). The specification and contract are created first, but they must be kept current ([CityGrow](https://note.com/tank_ai/n/n3be99a4ad80e?hl=en)). Test output is terse, a fast sampled mode exists and the work state is kept in files ([Carlini](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)).

| # | Rule | Concretely | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A stable high-level API for meshes and materials in context | primitives, extrude, lathe, sweep, bevel, mirror, array, named parts with pivots; material operators in the style of OpenKTG | 85–90% of failures in 3DCodeBench were API drift; strong systems (MeshCoder, LL3M) give the model a higher-level API |
| 2 | SDL3 and shadercross source as a vendored copy in the repository, the agent reads headers, not memory | mandatory in CLAUDE.md | SDL_GPU is newer than most training data; the lesson from Godogen with Bevy |
| 3 | CONVENTIONS.md + unit tests of conventions | e.g. "rotating +X by 90° around +Y gives −Z", a test triangle for the front face | handedness and quaternion order are known traps ([stride3d #378](https://github.com/stride3d/stride/issues/378)) |
| 4 | Asset family specification in numbers | dimensions, ratios, triangle budget, palette, texel density, references; no "polished" | testable requirements instead of wishes |
| 5 | Generators pure, seeded, without GPU | `-ffp-contract=off`, no fast-math, own transcendental functions, integer hashes for discrete decisions ([Dawson](https://randomascii.wordpress.com/2013/07/16/floating-point-determinism/)) | enables golden hashes and parallel generation |
| 6 | Numeric lints before any render | connectivity components, floating parts, ground contact, bounding box ranges, triangle counts, normals, palette and albedo histogram, texel density | the cheapest and most reliable oracle; catches "floating primitives" |
| 7 | Headless capture with a JSON report | `--capture --asset X --seed N --cam front\|side\|top\|3q --out`; contact sheet beauty + normals/matcap + albedo only; debug markers | multi-view and normal renders are a proven input for the judge (GPTEval3D); numbers next to the image (Asset Forge) |
| 8 | A separate visual judge | fresh context, sees only images, references, style bible and checklist; pairwise "A vs B vs reference", not absolute scores; critical decisions cross-checked by a model from another vendor | the author overrates itself; pairwise judgments with a checklist correlate with humans |
| 9 | N variants and selection instead of long self-critique rounds | e.g. 4–8 seeds or parameter variants, the judge selects | scaling the verifier is better documented than sequential critique (BlenderGym) |
| 10 | Narrow edits and golden hashes of untouched assets | every generator change produces a diff of the hashes of all other families | 35.8% of fixes in Code4Scene changed unrelated content |
| 11 | Animation is measured, not observed | foot sliding (foot speed in the stance phase), loop seam (diff of first and last frame), center-of-mass trajectory, onion-skin into one image; you judge feel by playing | VLMs do not see temporal errors |
| 12 | Golden images only for regressions, with tolerance, separately for each backend | perceptual metric (FLIP/SSIM), Metal and Vulkan each have their own set | GPU float outputs are not bit-exact across vendors ([Vulkan spec](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/1.3/html/chap37.html)) |
| 13 | Test output in a few lines, a `--fast` sample, progress files, an updated specification | ERROR on the same line for grep, details into a file | context pollution and the agent's time blindness (Carlini) |
| 14 | Parallel agents only on independent modules | no "make the city look good" for several agents at once | a monolithic goal leads to collisions and overwriting |
| 15 | Human gate | a family enters the library only after your approval; approved renders become the new references | the final aesthetic verdict belongs to a human |

The order of checks is fixed: compilation and validation layer, then lints, then golden hashes, then render and judge, and finally you. The judge never reads code. WorldBench showed that a judge that sees only code credits even features that do not exist ([WorldBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10622v1)). For images going to the model, use the higher input resolution: since Opus 4.7, Claude accepts up to 2,576 px on the longer edge ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-7)). A 2×2 contact sheet then fits into one image at usable detail.

## The M4 Pro handles 60 fps at ~1080p internal, not native Retina

The 16-core GPU of the M4 Pro scores 1620 in 3DMark Steel Nomad, the RTX 4050 Laptop 1613 and the RTX 4060 Laptop 2178. Cyberpunk 2077 runs on it at native 1080p at roughly 60 fps on low and 38 fps on ultra ([Notebookcheck GPU](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-M4-Pro-16-Core-GPU-Benchmarks-and-Specs.937474.0.html)). Memory bandwidth is 273 GB/s and is shared by CPU and GPU ([Notebookcheck analysis](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Apple-M4-Pro-analysis-Extremely-fast-but-not-as-efficient.915270.0.html)). Single-thread CPU performance is among the world's best: Geekbench 6 single ~3,840, multi ~20,400 (same source). Two constraints follow from SDL_GPU. First, **MetalFX is unavailable**, because SDL_GPU deliberately does not expose native Metal objects ([SDL Discourse](https://discourse.libsdl.org/t/metal-texture-access/42337)), so upscaling must be a custom shader. Second, multi-draw indirect on Metal is a CPU loop with one call per draw ([SDL_gpu_metal.m](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libsdl-org/SDL/main/src/gpu/metal/SDL_gpu_metal.m)), and bindless was rejected again in June 2026 ([SDL #11148](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/issues/11148)). The draw count is therefore a CPU budget, not a GPU one. All numbers in the tables below are **estimates** calibrated against Cyberpunk unless stated otherwise. The assumption is that a GTA III / WoW-class stylized renderer costs roughly ⅓–½ of Cyberpunk on low per pixel.

Memory follows a single rule: **textures are a shared library, never unique per object**. Unique uncompressed textures and high-poly SDF meshes without LOD are the two things that break the budget. On TBDR GPUs from Apple, load/store discipline also matters. SDL_GPU maps `DONT_CARE` 1:1 to Metal ([SDL wiki](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/SDL_GPUStoreOp)), so every intermediate result that is not read again should have `DONT_CARE`. Forward+ with MSAA resolved at store suits TBDR well. SDL_GPU exposes neither memoryless attachments nor tile shaders.

Tools and testing have clear limits on this machine. Xcode GPU Frame Capture and Metal validation work with SDL_GPU if you run the executable under Xcode with the "GPU Frame Capture: Metal" option enabled ([SDL wiki](https://wiki.libsdl.org/SDL3/CategoryGPU)). On Metal, Tracy can do GPU zones only at encoder level and needs an `MTLDevice` ([Tracy PR #793](https://github.com/wolfpld/tracy/pull/793)), which SDL_GPU hides. In practice you therefore use Tracy for CPU and jobs, Instruments and Metal System Trace for the GPU, and a rough frame time from fences. You can get the Vulkan backend running on the Mac through the Vulkan loader with KosmicKrisp, Vulkan 1.3 and `shaderDrawParameters` enabled. Loading MoltenVK directly failed in 2026 on the SPIR-V to MSL conversion ([SDL #15349](https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL/issues/15349)). You cannot test D3D12 here, since Windows 11 ARM in Parallels has only DirectX 11-class graphics ([tech2geek](https://www.tech2geek.net/how-to-run-windows-11-on-mac-parallels/)). Hosted macOS arm64 GitHub Actions runners have no Metal, and GPU runners (T4) exist only for Linux and Windows ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners)).

| Path | Where to test | What runs there |
|---|---|---|
| Metal | local Mac | rendering, golden images (Metal set), profiling, review renders for the judge |
| Vulkan | local Mac via KosmicKrisp; paid Linux GPU runner | validation layers, SPIR-V path, golden images (Vulkan set) |
| D3D12 | a real Windows x64 PC, a cloud GPU VM or a paid Windows T4 runner | runtime verification of DXIL; on the Mac only offline compilation via shadercross |
| Hosted CI (macOS, Linux, Windows) | no GPU | build, unit tests, null GPU, headless replay, **generator golden hashes across macOS-ARM64 / Linux-x64 / Windows-x64**, shader compilation into all three formats |

Overall: development itself, meaning builds, tools and Claude Code, is light for this machine, because the model runs in the cloud and locally only a Node process, a compiler and review renders remain. Running the engine in a dense city is what is heavy. The ranking of bottlenecks is in this order: geometry and shadows, CPU draw submission through SDL_GPU, pixel cost at native Retina, streaming hitches (texture compression, uploads, pipeline creation), heat under simultaneous load and memory pressure with open tools.

## What to measure first, once implementation starts

None of the numbers above is measured on your machine, and no benchmark measured Opus 5.5 on 3D generation. The following measurements belong in the first implementation steps, each as a permanent test or measurement scene in the repository. None of them assumes a throwaway prototype.

| # | What to measure | How | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GPU cost for a synthetic city cell | 10k textured instances + 3 cascades at 1512×982, 1080p, 1440p and native resolution; Instruments | default internal resolution and number of cascades |
| 2 | CPU cost per draw in SDL_GPU on Metal | encoding 1k/3k/10k draws × 4 passes; empty indirect draws | draw cap, need for static merging, a possible native backend |
| 3 | Thermal load | a 30-minute soak with generation on 4–6 workers, `powermetrics`, High Power Mode on and off | cap on generation workers and driving speed |
| 4 | `recommendedMaxWorkingSetSize` on 24 GB | a five-line Metal snippet | hard cap of the GPU budget |
| 5 | BC7/ASTC encoder throughput | ms per 1024² texture on P-cores | whether to encode during streaming or only at cook time |
| 6 | Upscaler cost | an FSR1-style pass and a custom TAAU in ms | image quality on Retina |
| 7 | Generator determinism across platforms | golden hash of the same seeds on macOS-ARM64, Linux-x64 and Windows-x64 in CI | whether caches and networking can rely on regeneration |
| 8 | Calibration of the separate judge | ~30–50 pairs of renders, the judge against your verdict; threshold e.g. ≥80% agreement | what weight the judge gets; below the threshold you approve everything yourself |
| 9 | Procedural vs. generated materials after post-processing | 10 key materials (asphalt, plaster, brick, metal, roof, bark, fabric…) from both sources, a blind comparison against your bar and a batch consistency test | default source for each category and tuning of post-processing |
| 10 | Baseline capability of Opus 5.5 by category | first pass and number of iterations until your approval for a building, vehicle, tree and character | a realistic schedule and where to tighten templates |

| 12 | Vulkan on the Mac | a run through the loader + KosmicKrisp with validation layers | whether to catch Vulkan regressions locally |

## Conclusion

The project does not have a capacity problem, it has a verification problem. The model writes more and faster than you can judge. The deciding investment is therefore not the choice of model or the thinking budget, but three things: a stable API of your own for geometry and materials, numeric lints that translate your aesthetic requirements into numbers, and a calibrated separate judge. Each of them moves part of your eye into automation. The 2026 benchmarks show that a model improves mainly reliability, not visual fidelity. This infrastructure will therefore survive the next model generations too, whereas waiting for a "better-seeing" Claude is a bet without a documented trajectory.

The GTA III bar is realistic for a code-generated world, and in light and atmosphere you will beat it. The WoW bar is different from a technical one: it is a bar of art direction and hand-painted surface, exactly the layer that neither code nor a VLM can reliably produce without a human. The hybrid texture pipeline supplies the painterly layer from an image model. Code post-processing, however, must subordinate it to one palette and one set of masks, otherwise the hybrid turns into a collage. The real product of the pipeline is therefore not a texture, but unity of style across both sources. Plan characters as stylized and parametric from the start, not as a weakness you patch up later.
