# Procedural worlds and a striking look in Three.js (2025-2026), mapped onto New Hope

*Research date: 2026-10-14. Status: proposal only.*

Scope: procedural cities, terrain and voxel meshing, sky and atmosphere, fog and colour grading,
the No Man's Sky (NMS) / Light No Fire (LNF) look, cheap neon night lighting, procedural
textures, stylised shading, particles and weather. Everything is read against what New Hope
(`packages/new-hope-play`, three 0.180, `WebGLRenderer`) does today and against D30 ("a No Man's
Sky-like look ... graphics stay code").

## 1. Summary

1. **The NMS look is a rule system plus a review loop, not a shader.** Grant Duncan (NMS art
   director): avoid black, 1970s sci-fi covers (Chris Foss, Ralph McQuarrie) as the reference, a
   "really complex kind of color theory system so they all obey rules", complementary colours
   between e.g. leaves and grass, and a drone that flies to generated planets and records GIFs
   for review. When a result looks wrong he changes the rules so it becomes less likely
   [S: GamesBeat 2015/2025, Engadget 2016, Killscreen 2015]. That is our verdict loop already.
2. **For New Hope the biggest visual wins are cheap:** a seeded *look preset* (palette, fog, three
   lights, grade) generated by rules; Inigo Quilez's coloured, height-based and sun-tinted fog;
   neon light baked into vertex colours at chunk-build time; mirrored neon "streaks" on wet
   ground instead of SSR; interior-mapped windows on the skyline; GPU-side rain. None needs
   WebGPU [O].
3. **Physically based skies are cheap but Earth-shaped.** Hillaire 2020 decouples sky cost from
   screen resolution with small LUTs (Bevy's defaults: transmittance 256×128, multi-scattering
   32×32, sky-view 400×200, aerial perspective 32×32×32) [S: Bevy source]. Hillaire's slides
   quote 0.14 ms to render sky and atmosphere at 720p and 0.31 ms total on the PC path [S]
   [refuted 2026-10-14 in part: the slide (p. 21) is labelled "NVIDIA 1080" and shows "Lut cost
   = 1.03ms", "Render sky & atmosphere @720p = 0.14ms", "Total cost = 0.31ms" together; the
   three figures do not add up in the extracted text, so treat the 0.31 ms "total" as
   [unconfirmed] and the 1.03 ms as the PC LUT cost]. A
   stylised dome gives more art control for a neon night city [O].
4. **Many real lights are the wrong tool on WebGL.** Light probes do not emit light [S: three.js
   docs]. On WebGPU, `TiledLighting` handles up to 1024 lights in 32-px tiles by default
   [S: three.js docs]. On WebGL, fake it: emissive + bloom, baked vertex light, additive ground
   pools [O].
5. **SSR is a WebGPU node with real knobs, but still a cost centre.** r180 gave `SSRNode` a
   `quality` uniform, `resolutionScale` and half-float buffers [S: r180 release notes]
   [refuted 2026-10-14 in part: r180 added `quality` (#31576), the half-float reflection buffer
   (#31646) and blurred mipmaps/roughness (#31649, #31663); `resolutionScale` already existed on
   `SSRNode` in r179 (default 0.5). The r180 note "Add `resolutionScale`" (#31629) is about the
   WebGL `SSRPass`, which does exist in three 0.180 under
   `examples/jsm/postprocessing/SSRPass.js`]. Threejs-Punk
   still removed SSR for a planar mirror. For neon on wet asphalt, a fake mirrored-sign layer is
   cheaper still [O].
6. **City generation:** shape grammars (CGA) for tower silhouettes and facades, recursive-OBB lot
   subdivision for the skyline, WFC only where tiles have local rules. Marian Kleineberg's
   infinite WFC city shows chunked WFC fails at chunk borders and backtracking is fragile
   [S: marian42 2019]. Our `@whz/citygen` grid with invariants is the better core. WFC is at
   most a facade-module picker [O].
7. **Procedural textures:** keep canvas atlases for anything the owner should review as an asset
   (the studio can show them). Use shader noise for large-scale variation: grime, wetness, window
   life. TSL texture libraries (`tsl-textures`, MIT) need `three/webgpu` [S].
8. **WebGPU is now in all major browsers** (Chrome/Edge 113, Firefox 141 on Windows, Safari 26)
   [S: web.dev, Nov 2025]. But Android WebView is not covered (see ref-threejs-punk), and New
   Hope's renderer is WebGL. Recommendation: stay on WebGL for now. Write new shaders so they
   port to TSL, and switch when we need MRT bloom, SSR or tiled lights [O].

## 2. The NMS / Light No Fire look, and how to get it in WebGL

### What is known

- **No black.** "One of Duncan's goals from the beginning was to stay away from the color
  black". NMS uses NASA imagery to avoid a "generic black star field" [S: GamesBeat].
- **References:** 1970s sci-fi paperback art (Foss, McQuarrie), Hubble's "Pillars of Creation"
  [S: GamesBeat].
- **Rules:** "a really complex kind of color theory system so they all obey rules ... like leaves
  having complimentary colors compared to the grass ... the same goes for silhouettes". Hot
  planets get dried-out colours and sharper shapes [S: Engadget 2016].
- **Atmosphere colour from the planet:** Sean Murray said the atmosphere colour comes from the
  liquid present, and light refraction is modelled [S: Daily Beast quoting MIT Technology Review,
  2nd-hand] [unconfirmed: the Daily Beast page (2015-09-03) fetched on 2026-10-14 contains no
  such quote and does not cite MIT Technology Review; treat as [U]].
- **Review loop:** drones visit planets and record GIFs onto a review microsite. Bad outcomes lead
  to rule changes [S: Killscreen, GamesBeat].
- **Shading:** lighting from face normals, texturing from smooth normals, which gives the
  "slightly low poly look" [S: via ref-nms-procgen, McKendrick GDC 2017 42:27].
- **Light No Fire:** official material shows a "colorful", inviting look [S: GamesRadar]. I found
  no technical talk on LNF rendering. Everything else about LNF's look is [U].

### Translating it into code (all [O] unless marked)

The NMS look reduces to five controllable things:
1. a saturated, *bright-floored* palette;
2. complementary accent pairs;
3. coloured fog that ties far geometry to the sky;
4. faceted shading;
5. big sky objects.

A concrete recipe:

- **Palette generator per seed.** Use IQ's cosine palette `col(t) = a + b·cos(2π(c·t + d))`
  (vec3 a, b, c, d) [S: iquilezles.org/articles/palettes]. With `a = b = 0.5` it spans the full
  range [O: that sentence is arithmetic, not on the page; the page only says a and b set
  contrast and brightness, and its table also has rows with a ≠ b]. The example sets (e.g.
  `d = (0, 0.33, 0.67)`, `d = (0, 0.10, 0.20)`, both with a = b = 0.5, c = 1) are ready-made
  starting points.
  - A seed picks `d` (hue phase) and `c` (spread).
  - Rules then pick colours at fixed `t` slots: sky zenith, sky horizon, fog, key light, fill
    light, neon A, neon B = complement of A (hue + 180°, or `t + 0.5` when `c = 1`).
  - The formula is maths; implement it from the formula. The page states no licence, and its
    Shadertoy demos may carry Shadertoy's default CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 [U].
- **No-black rule as a lint.**
  - Minimum luminance for fog colour and for the darkest shadow (the ambient/hemisphere floor),
    e.g. ≥ 0.04 linear.
  - Saturation of fog ≥ 0.3.
  - A planet-dependent tint in every shadow (IQ's penumbra trick below).
  - These are numbers a check can assert on the preset, in the same spirit as NMS's "change the
    rule so the bad case is less likely".
- **Complementary rule:** neon A and B differ by ~150-180° hue. Fog sits near A's hue at low
  value. Window light is warm when neon is cold, and the other way round. New Hope's current
  `sky.ts` already does a pink horizon / violet mid / cyan key by hand. The proposal makes it a
  seeded preset.
- **Faceted shading:** keep `flatShading: true` on models (New Hope already does). For city
  geometry the cell faces are flat anyway.
- **Big sky objects:** New Hope's ringed gas giant and moon are exactly the NMS move. Make their
  colours come from the same palette, so planet, fog and neon agree.
- **A look preset is an asset.** Store it as a small JSON (or TS) object: palette params,
  fog density/height/sun-tint, the three light colours and intensities, bloom, grade LUT params.
  The studio can show it as a contact sheet of fixed camera shots per seed, and the owner can give
  verdicts. That is NMS's drone-GIF loop in our terms.

**Recommendations for `sky.ts`** (all [O]):
- **Replace `fract(sin(dot(...))*43758.5453)`.** It loses precision on some mobile GPUs [U].
  Use an integer hash (`uint` PCG/xxhash-style; GLSL ES 3.0 has `uint` in WebGL2) so the stars
  don't shimmer or clump.
- **Better nebula:** 4-5 octaves of value noise along the view direction (domain-warped once)
  coloured by the seeded cosine palette. The current product of three sines reads as stripes.
- **Seam hider:** the horizon colour of the dome must equal the fog colour at the horizon. Today
  `horizon = #d8437e` and `fog = #3a1c5c` differ, so far towers end in a violet wall under a pink
  band. Either compute the dome's lower part from the same fog function (§4), or make fog
  sample the sky colour in the view direction ("sky-coloured fog"). ref-nms-procgen §3.8 makes
  the same point for LOD.
- **Planet light:** use the gas giant as the "sun" direction for IQ's sun-tinted fog. Fog
  toward the planet glows in the planet's colour, which sells scale.

## 4. Fog and colour grading

### Fog formulas (IQ, [S: iquilezles.org/articles/fog])

- Exponential: `f = 1 − exp(−t·b)`; `col = mix(col, fogCol, f)`.
- Sun-tinted: `sunAmount = max(dot(rd, sunDir), 0)`;
  `fogCol = mix(blue, yellow, pow(sunAmount, 8))`. The exponent can vary with distance to fake
  glow.
- Per-channel: separate extinction `be` and in-scattering `bi` (vec3 each, six parameters). The
  consistent form is `col·exp(−t·be) + fogCol·(1 − exp(−t·bi))`. This is how you get
  "red fades first, blue lingers" alien air.
- Height fog, analytic, for density `a·e^(−b·y)`:
  `fogAmount = (a/b)·exp(−ro.y·b)·(1 − exp(−T·rd.y·b))/rd.y`. That is one extra division over
  constant fog. Guard `rd.y ≈ 0`.

### For New Hope

- Today: `FogExp2(#3a1c5c, 0.0031)`, one colour, no height [S: `sky.ts`].
- **Height fog is the single most "NMS + Blade Runner" change** [O]. The undercity drowns in thick
  coloured haze, the Union levels sit in clear air, and towers rise out of a glowing layer. That
  also matches the story's vertical structure ("up is the Union, down is the past",
  `games/new-hope/STORY.md`).
- In three 0.180 WebGL: override `THREE.ShaderChunk.fog_pars_fragment` and `fog_fragment`
  before the first compile. They then apply to every built-in material, and the custom sky
  shader calls the same function. The world-position varying needs `fog_pars_vertex` /
  `fog_vertex` too [O; the chunk names exist in three's ShaderChunk set, U for exact 0.180
  contents] [verified 2026-10-14: three 0.180 ships `fog_pars_fragment.glsl.js` (uniform
  `fogColor`, varying `vFogDepth`, `fogDensity` or `fogNear`/`fogFar` under `USE_FOG`) and
  `fog_vertex.glsl.js`, which sets only `vFogDepth = - mvPosition.z`; there is no world-position
  varying, so it must be added as the report says].
- Keep the fog function in one GLSL string exported from `sky.ts`, so dome, skyline, district
  and particles agree.

### Grading and tone mapping

- **Tone mapping.** New Hope uses `ACESFilmicToneMapping`. ACES pushes saturated neon toward
  white and shifts hues. Magenta and cyan signs lose their identity at high values. Try
  `AgXToneMapping` or `NeutralToneMapping` (both in three since ~r160-r162) and compare
  [U for versions; O for the effect] [verified 2026-10-14: AgX in r160 (2023-12-22), Khronos
  Neutral in r162 (2024-02-29); both constants (6 and 7) are in three 0.180's `constants.js`].
- **Grade LUT generated in code.** Write a 32³ `Data3DTexture` from a few rules: lift shadows
  toward the palette's shadow hue (the no-black floor), split-tone highs toward the complement,
  saturation curve.
  - On WebGL: `LUTPass` from `three/examples/jsm/postprocessing/LUTPass.js` [U path]
    [verified 2026-10-14: the file exists in three 0.180; `new LUTPass({ lut: Data3DTexture,
    intensity })`, extends `ShaderPass`].
  - On WebGPU: `Lut3DNode` (`lut3D(input, lutTex, size, intensity)`) [S: three.js docs].
  - The LUT is an asset the studio can preview (a strip of graded reference shots).
- **Lighting rig (IQ "outdoors lighting")** [S]:
  - Key ≈ 1.0-1.5 with penumbra tint `pow(vec3(shadow), vec3(1.0, 1.2, 1.5))`.
  - Sky fill ≈ 0.2 from above, multiplied by occlusion.
  - Bounce ≈ 0.3 from the anti-sun horizontal direction, coloured sun × albedo, more saturated.
  - Keep albedo ≈ 0.2 and raise lights, not materials.
  - New Hope's rig is close: hemisphere 0.9, ambient 0.6, key 1.6, rim 0.7 [S: `sky.ts`]. The
    fill (hemi 0.9 + ambient 0.6) is too strong relative to the key by IQ's rule. That flattens
    the image and lifts it toward grey instead of toward a hue [O]. Replace the ambient with the
    palette's shadow tint and lower the hemisphere.

## 5. Neon night lighting at low cost

### Emissive + bloom

- New Hope already does it the WebGL way: emissive colours ×2.4-3 with `toneMapped: false`
  push signs over `UnrealBloomPass`'s threshold 0.86 (strength 0.7, radius 0.5) [S: `main.ts`,
  `city.ts`].
- On WebGPU the documented path is a scene pass with `mrt({ output, emissive })` and `bloom()`
  only on the emissive attachment. `bloomTintColors` gives per-mip tints, and
  `setResolutionScale()` is the only documented cost lever [S: BloomNode docs].
- Threejs-Punk ran bloom at 0.5× [S: ref-threejs-punk].
- **Cheap wins on WebGL** [O]:
  - Run the bloom at half resolution: construct it with `innerWidth/2`. It still blurs, and
    cost drops ~4×.
  - Tint bloom toward the palette.
  - Keep the threshold high, so only intentional emitters bloom. Facade atlas emissive should
    stay < 1.

### Fake light on surfaces (the expensive part, done cheaply)

- **Light probes are not lights.** They are SH irradiance and "do not emit light" [S: three.js
  docs]. Per-object probe volumes sample only the mesh centre, which is useless for buildings
  [S: forum].
- **WebGPU many-lights:** `TiledLighting` / `TiledLightsNode(maxLights = 1024, tileSize = 32)`,
  set on `renderer.lighting` [S: docs] [verified 2026-10-14 against the r180 source
  (`examples/jsm/tsl/lighting/TiledLightsNode.js`) and the `webgpu_lights_tiled` example
  (`import { TiledLighting } from 'three/addons/lighting/TiledLighting.js'`,
  `renderer.lighting = lighting`); the docs pages themselves returned 404]. This is the future
  path for real sign lights.
- **WebGL now, recommended** [O]:
  1. **Bake neon into vertex colours at chunk-build time.** `city.ts` builds static chunk
     geometry, and citygen knows where every blade sign, beacon and shopfront is. For each
     vertex, add Σ signColour · intensity / (1 + d²/r²) over signs within ~12 m, times
     `max(0, dot(n, toSign))`. That is a few thousand signs × local vertices once per chunk, in
     integer-friendly maths, deterministic, and zero runtime cost. This is the classic static
     light-bake, and the forum's answer for static neon is exactly "bake lightmaps" [S].
  2. **Additive ground pools.** These are flat quads with a radial falloff under signs and cars.
     New Hope's car underglow `pool` is already one [S: `models.ts`]. Instance them per sign.
  3. **Two or three real `PointLight`s that follow the player** and take the colour of the
     nearest signs. On WebGL every light costs on every lit fragment, so keep ≤ 4 [O].

### Fake volumetrics

- **Light cones:** a cone mesh with additive falloff along its axis and toward its edges, plus a
  depth-based soft fade where it meets geometry ("soft particle"). That is how drei's
  `SpotLightMaterial` / threex volumetric spotlight work [S: forum threads]. Use them for Union
  searchlights, lift shafts and the body-passing beam. Cost: one transparent draw each.
- **Raymarched post-process volumetrics** (Maxime Heckel, June 2025) [S]:
  - world position from depth via the inverse projection and view matrices;
  - Henyey-Greenstein phase and Beer's law;
  - a shadow map from the light: 256² default, 512² chosen;
  - step count cut from 250 to 50 by raising the step size, with blue-noise jitter per frame to
    hide banding.

  Cost scales with steps × pixels × lights, and each light needs its own camera and render
  target. Use it for one hero light at most, at half resolution [O].
- **Height fog (§4) does 80 % of "volumetric" for free** [O].

### Reflections on wet ground (rain)

| Technique | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Env map + low roughness | ~free | Reflects the sky only, not the neon [O] |
| Planar mirror (Reflector / `MeshReflectorMaterial`) | One extra scene render (half res in Threejs-Punk) | drei: resolution is the main lever, default 256 [S]. Forum: ~12 FPS on an Android phone [S]. Only flat ground, one plane per height. New Hope has many deck heights [O] |
| `SSRNode` (WebGPU) | `quality`, `maxDistance`, `blurQuality` (kernel (2s+1)²) and `binaryRefine` drive cost; `resolutionScale 0.5`; needs MRT normals, metalness and roughness [S: docs] [refuted 2026-10-14 in part: the docs describe the current version; in the r180 source `SSRNode` has `quality` (0.5), `blurQuality` (2), `maxDistance` (1), `resolutionScale` (1) and `metalnessNode`/`roughnessNode`, but **no `binaryRefine`**, which came later] | Threejs-Punk removed SSR anyway [S]. Not on WebGL in three [refuted 2026-10-14: three 0.180 ships the WebGL `SSRPass` (`examples/jsm/postprocessing/SSRPass.js`, with `resolutionScale` since r180); only the TSL `SSRNode` is WebGPU/node-only] |
| **Mirrored emitters** | One extra instanced draw | **Recommended** [O]. For every sign/beacon instance, also draw a copy mirrored about the floor below it, stretched vertically ×2-3, alpha by wetness, UV-wobbled by a ripple noise, depth-tested against the floor. Neon streaks on wet asphalt are mostly this. A puddle mask (world-space noise) modulates it |

The ripple normal itself must be written clean-room. The Threejs-Punk ripples match an
uncredited Shadertoy, and its rain-drop graph is CC BY-NC-SA ([licence flags](README.md#licence-flags)).

## 6. Procedural cities

- **Shape grammars (CGA, Müller et al.)** split a mass into floors, then bays, then windows,
  with repeat rules and random/parameterised choices [S: gamedev.net article].
  - A 2026 agent-skill doc for three.js puts it as: the generator should "produce an
    inspectable plan before it produces triangles", then emit merged geometry or `BatchedMesh`
    per chunk [S: third-party skill page, ideas only].
  - That matches our citygen → ASCII → mesh split.
- **Lot subdivision:**
  - CityEngine's recursive OBB: split along the minimum-area oriented bounding box, and recurse
    only while both halves meet the constraints.
  - Its straight-skeleton mode slices face groups perpendicular to the street and merges small
    lots [S: ArcGIS docs].
  - The Glasgow thesis (Kelly) generalises the skeleton for parcels and roofs [S].
- **WFC:**
  - `three-wfc` (MIT, WIP, April 2025): typed arrays, bitmask adjacency, min-heap entropy and a
    circular propagation queue took a 100×100 grid from minutes to ~2-3 s with bitmasks, then
    to hundreds of ms with the heap. No backtracking yet [S] [verified 2026-10-14 with a
    correction: per the forum post (2025-04-28) grids over 50×50 took minutes; typed arrays
    brought 100×100 to ~30 s, bitmasks to ~2-3 s, the min-heap to "hundreds of milliseconds" on
    big grids; the propagation structure is called a "circular stack"; backtracking is listed
    under "next steps"; the repo is MIT and marked WIP].
  - Kleineberg's infinite WFC city (Unity, MIT, 2019): ~100 modules with face connectors.
    Chunk-based generation failed at chunk borders. Backtracking "usually works" but sometimes
    regenerates the player's own slot [S].
  - Townscaper: marching-cubes-style module placement on a relaxed irregular quad grid
    [S: Boris the Brave tutorial].
- **Interior mapping for windows:** the window shader ray-casts into a virtual room box and
  samples a room texture, so windows get depth with no geometry.
  - `three-fenestra` (MIT, v0.3.0) is a `MeshStandardMaterial` subclass [S].
  - Many Godot versions exist (ideas only) [S].

**For New Hope** [O]:
- citygen's grid with invariants (walkability, lane networks) stays the core of the district.
  WFC would fight those invariants.
- **The endless skyline is where generation is weakest.** It is boxes of hashed height with an
  optional crown, and every tower is a 1×1×1 box scaled (`sky.ts`).
  - Upgrade it with a tiny grammar per tower: base podium → 1-3 setback tiers (each 70-90 % of
    the one below) → crown type (stepped / spire / antenna cluster / pad).
  - Keep it as several `InstancedMesh`es of unit parts. That is still only a handful of draw
    calls.
  - Use recursive-OBB lots instead of the fixed 22 m grid, so blocks vary in width.
  - Seed: `hash3(ix, iz, k)` as now.
- Keep the shader windows (already `onBeforeCompile` on the instances), and add interior mapping
  for the near ring of skyline and district facades.
  - Rooms come from a hash: back-wall colour from the palette, one of 4 furniture silhouettes
    drawn in the shader, lit/unlit from a time-varying hash.
  - It is roughly 15 lines of GLSL: ray-box in tangent space [O]. Write our own; fenestra is a
    reference.

## 7. Terrain and voxel meshing in Three.js

- **Greedy meshing (0fps, 2012):** an 8×8×8 solid cube needs 3072 quads naive, 384 culled and 6
  greedy. A sphere: 4770 → 2100; noise: 2198 → 1670. Greedy is at most 8× worse than optimal
  [S]. Code licence: informal "yes" in a comment only [S].
- **Binary greedy meshing (cgerikj):** bitwise rows. 50-200 µs per chunk, average 74 µs
  single-threaded on a Ryzen 3800X [S]. A fork's table shows 1.1 ms for hills and 37 ms for a
  checkerboard, so content matters [S] [unconfirmed: no such table in the cgerikj README on
  2026-10-14 and the fork is not named; a search found nothing]. Licence [U] [verified
  2026-10-14: the repo is MIT]. Read for ideas.
- **Workers:** voxel-three/voxelized-js does greedy meshing in a worker into one
  `InstancedMesh` [S]. The noa author found worker transfer gave hiccups and instead throttled
  chunks meshed per frame on the main thread [S: 2022 Reddit] [refuted 2026-10-14: the thread's
  poster is not noa's author (fenomas) but a developer who started from noa-engine and rewrote
  most of it; the worker hiccups were reported by a commenter, and the poster says they use no
  workers and throttle how many chunks are meshed between frames (one chunk ≈ 1/3-1/2 of a
  16 ms frame). Read via a search summary of the rtrace mirror; reddit itself could not be
  fetched]. Lesson: budget meshes per frame
  either way.
- **LOD and seams:** see ref-nms-procgen §3.3-3.6 (CDLOD morph, Surface Nets, overlap borders).
  Nothing newer changes that.

**For New Hope** [O]:
- `city.ts` emits one quad per visible cell face against an atlas, in 16-tile chunks, built on
  the main thread at load.
- Greedy merging would cut quads (a 20-storey facade of one style becomes 1 quad instead of 20),
  but it breaks per-cell atlas UVs. To merge, either:
  - (a) switch the atlas to a `DataArrayTexture` (one layer per tile) and repeat UVs with
    `fract` in the shader, which also fixes mip bleeding between atlas tiles; or
  - (b) keep per-cell quads, which is fine at 48×48×20.
- **Measure first** with `renderer.info.render.triangles` and the build time in `__nh.info()`.
  Move the chunk build into a worker only when districts stream (more than one district, or
  flying to the next one).

## 8. Procedural textures: shader/TSL vs canvas atlas

| | Canvas atlas (current `textures.ts`, 8×8 tiles × 64 px, 16 px/m) | Shader noise (GLSL now, TSL later) |
|---|---|---|
| Review | It is an image: the studio can show it, the owner gives a verdict, a hash pins it | Only visible in context. Needs fixed shots |
| Resolution | Fixed. Blurs or aliases up close | Unlimited, but needs filtering (`fwidth`) or it shimmers [O] |
| Cost | Texture fetch | ALU per pixel. Fine for 2-4 octaves [O] |
| Determinism | Canvas `fillRect` on integer coordinates is safe. AA paths may differ by browser [S: code-graphics] | GPU float maths. Visual only, so acceptable |
| Variation | Repeats per tile | World-space, never repeats |

`tsl-textures` (MIT): a library of TSL procedural texture functions assigned to `colorNode`.
It requires `three/webgpu` [S].

**Recommendation** [O]: a hybrid.
- **Atlas for designed detail:** facade modules, signage glyphs, decals. These are assets with
  verdicts.
- **Shader for world-scale modulation:** grime and streaks by world height, wetness mask,
  window life over time, sign flicker.
- Fix the atlas bleeding: use a texture array, or pad each 64-px tile and clamp UVs inside it.

## 9. Stylised shading: toon, rim, outlines

- **Toon:** `MeshToonMaterial` needs a nearest-filtered `gradientMap`, 3-5 bands [S: three.js
  docs]. code-graphics §6 adds the key trick: hue-shift the shadow band toward cool violet.
- **Rim:** a fresnel term `pow(1 − dot(n, v), 3) · rimColour` added to emissive via
  `onBeforeCompile`. It costs nothing [O]. Against bright fog, rim in the fog's complement keeps
  characters and cars readable. This matters more for New Hope than outlines.
- **Outlines:**
  - Inverted hull: a back-face shell offset along the normal, constant pixel width when offset in
    clip space [S: code-graphics §6].
  - Screen-space Sobel on depth + normals: Heckel's Moebius post weights depth ×25 and uses
    thickness 3, with extra normal and depth passes [S, March 2024].
  - Surface-ID buffer: Omar Shehata, Oct 2022, which fixes most depth/normal artefacts and costs
    one ID pass [S: search summary; the article itself returned 403].
  - Three's `ToonOutlinePassNode` (WebGPU) outlines only `MeshToonMaterial` objects [S]
    [verified 2026-10-14: `MeshToonMaterial` and `MeshToonNodeMaterial`].
- **For New Hope:** NMS has no outlines. Keep lit flat shading, add rim and the hue-shifted
  shadow tint, and save outlines for the HUD/radar targets (an inverted hull on the job target
  or a wanted cruiser) [O].

## 10. Particles and weather

- **Current rain:** 4000 `LineSegments` drops moved on the CPU each frame, seeded with
  `Math.random`, wrapped in a 70 m box around the camera [S: `fx.ts`].
- **Better (WebGL, no compute)** [O]:
  - Make it a static buffer of per-drop seeds (from `@whz/det` or a hash) and compute the
    position in the vertex shader:
    `y = top − mod(seed·H + t·speed, H)`, with x/z wrapped into the camera's cell.
  - Hash in world space so camera motion does not change a drop's phase. One third-party skill
    note makes the same point [S, ideas only].
  - Result: zero CPU, 20-50k drops affordable. Stretch streaks by velocity relative to the
    camera, which matters when flying.
- **Splashes without a depth pass:** citygen knows every floor height (`world.ts` floor query).
  Spawn ring sprites on floor cells near the camera by hash and time. This is the analytic ring
  from ref-threejs-punk §3.2 (`1 − smoothstep` on |r − r(t)|), written clean.
- **WebGPU later:** three's official `webgpu_compute_particles_rain` (MIT) does collision via a
  top-down height render [S: ref-threejs-punk].
- **Weather as look:**
  - A storm should change the look preset: fog density up, fog height up, key light down, the
    wet-reflection layer on, and lightning as a 100-ms key-light + fog-colour flash with thunder
    from `audio.ts`.
  - Dust motes in sky lanes and steam from vents are instanced quads with soft depth fade.
- Snow / ash for other districts (the 80.lv snow system uses GPU-instanced flakes plus an
  FBM world-space accumulation mask) [S] [verified 2026-10-14; the article is dated 2026-06-30].

## 11. WebGL or WebGPU for New Hope

- WebGPU ships in Chrome/Edge 113+, Firefox 141 (Windows, then Apple Silicon Mac) and Safari 26
  on all Apple OSes. Chrome on Android 12+ supports it, but Android WebView does not [S: web.dev
  Nov 2025; ref-threejs-punk].
- `WebGPURenderer` falls back to WebGL2, but `onBeforeCompile` patches and `ShaderMaterial` GLSL
  do **not** carry over. Node materials are a rewrite [O, U for edge cases].
- Recommendation [O]:
  - Stay on WebGL (`WebGLRenderer`, `EffectComposer`) for the next builds.
  - Keep each shader feature (fog, windows, rim, neon bake, rain) as one small GLSL function in
    one file, so a later TSL port is mechanical.
  - Revisit when we need MRT bloom, `TiledLighting` or `SSRNode`, and pin the three version at
    that time (`PostProcessing` → `RenderPipeline` rename at r183).

## Open questions (owner)

1. **Day/night or eternal night?** A neon night city with a fixed sky keeps the stylised dome
   (cheap, art-directed). A day/night cycle or flying to orbit needs a Hillaire-style atmosphere.
2. **One look per district seed, or one hand-picked New Hope look** with only small seeded
   variation? NMS varies per planet. A city-planet might want a single identity.
3. **How far toward stylisation:** lit flat shading (NMS) vs toon bands and outlines?
   Recommendation: NMS-like lit flat shading with rim, no outlines.
4. **WebGL now, WebGPU when?** Mobile/WebView targets (Capacitor) argue for WebGL longer.
5. Should look presets and atlases go through **studio verdicts** like the 2D assets?
