- **Repo:** https://github.com/mike007jd/voxel-musou (shallow clone, HEAD `26ea581`, "Merge pull request #6 from mike007jd/campaign")

- **Analysed:** 2026-10-02, for Wallhammer Zero (WZ).

  - **Fact** means it was verified in code; a file:line citation follows.

  - The 2D pixel-art Kairosoft-style sims come first; the voxel games come later.

## Executive summary

1. **What it is.** A browser "musou" (Dynasty Warriors-like) brawler: one hero against hundreds of soldiers. It is plain ES modules plus vendored Three.js r186, with no build step and **no asset files at all**: models, textures, sky, sound and voices are all generated by code. It is about 25.8k lines, not the ~9.3k the README implies. *(Fact: `index.html` importmap, `vendor/three`, `src/audio/bank.js:1-6`)*
2. **Strict sim/render split.**
   - A fixed 60 Hz accumulator loop runs at most 4 sim steps per frame.
   - Sim modules run in a fixed order. Render modules only read sim state; they never write it.
   - There are two seeded RNGs, `rng` for the sim and `vrng` for visuals, plus a stateless `hash01` for detail. Audio uses `Math.random`, outside both.
   - A synchronous event bus carries sim to view.
   - Because the sim never touches the renderer, it runs headless in Node (`checks/*.mjs`, including a balance bot). *(Fact: `src/main.js:97-127,273-285`, `src/core/rng.js`, `src/core/events.js`)*
3. **Voxel models are code: integer box lists per rig joint.**
   - A box is `{a, b, c, paint?}`.
   - `c` is a hex colour, `-1` to carve, or a function `(x,y,z) → colour|null`. The function form is how procedural patterns work: lamellar armour, brocade, chain mail.
   - Later boxes win, and `paint` boxes only recolour voxels that already exist.
   - Boxes are rasterised into an `Int32Array` grid and meshed with **culled faces only (no greedy meshing)**, 3-neighbour vertex AO, an AO-aware quad flip, and hash colour jitter. *(Fact: `src/hero/model.js:28-101`, `src/core/voxel.js:64-124`)*
4. **Rigging is rigid, not skinned.**
   - There is one voxel mesh per joint.
   - A pose is a `Float32Array(45)` in root space.
   - Clips are keyframes sampled with Catmull-Rom or monotone cubic interpolation.
   - Arms and legs use analytic 2-bone IK; hands slide along the weapon shaft.
   - Planted feet are baked per sim frame for attacks. *(Fact: `src/hero/rig.js:31-45,93-134,218-243`, `src/hero/anims/author.js:29-116`)*
5. **Secondary motion is render-only Verlet chains of voxel slabs** (capes, hair, feathers, tassels). Each chain has gravity, gusting wind, drag, a stiffness pull toward rest, a cone limit, and sphere colliders on the body. *(Fact: `src/hero/secondary.js:1-105`)*
6. **Crowds: one `InstancedMesh` per body part.**
   - The CPU builds hierarchical matrices from a 26-channel pose per soldier.
   - There are 3 LODs. Mid re-voxelises the *same boxes* at twice the voxel size; far uses unvoxelised boxes, about 450 tris instead of about 4.9k.
   - The shadow pass draws cheap proxy boxes instead of the voxel mesh.
   - Idle soldiers are recomputed every 4th frame and replayed in between.
   - The default is 300 soldiers; `?enemies` allows up to 2000. *(Fact: `src/crowd/view.js:181-217,410-432,632-747`, `src/main.js:55-56`)*
7. **Game feel is data-driven.**
   - Per-hitbox hitstop is hero-local: 1–4 sf scaled by victim count, 6–8 sf on heavy hits.
   - Victims flash; launched bodies get an apex float; a "lens cut" stops bodies flying into the camera.
   - Camera micro-kicks are aged in sim frames.
   - Audio ducks the mix on hits (sidechain), and swing whooshes are cued ahead of the hit frame. *(Fact: `src/combat/combat.js:23-42,85-95`, `src/camera/camera.js:138-154`, `src/audio/audio.js:140-187`)*
8. **The "retro pixel look" is a texture, not pixel art.**
   - The full-resolution HDR chain runs: atmosphere, then DoF, then bloom, then god rays, then a final grade.
   - The final grade ends with a **2-px Bayer dither and per-channel quantisation to 72 levels** (≈373k colours; no palette), plus animated grain.
   - The canvas renders at device-pixel-ratio 1, upscaled with CSS `image-rendering: pixelated`.
   - *(Fact: `src/post/post.js:1-12,25-37,186-200,261-266`, `index.html:13`)*
   - *Opinion:* the reusable parts for real pixel art are the grade → dither → quantise order, the `bayer4` matrix, and "look as uniforms". We must add a low-res render target, a palette LUT and outlines (§5(ii)).
9. **Audio is 100% procedural.**
   - Every SFX and voice is baked at boot with `OfflineAudioContext`: a formant voice synth, layered whooshes, modal "clanks", and loops.
   - Runtime mixing uses buses, convolution reverb, a compressor, a soft clip and sidechain. *(Fact: `src/audio/bank.js:1-160`, `src/audio/audio.js:68-150`)*
10. **Top 3 to take, re-ranked for "2D sims first" (Opinion):**
    - **(a)** the box-list → grid → AO-mesher and its per-joint model format, as the source of every sprite and, later, every 3D model;
    - **(b)** the flat pose array, the clip sampler, 2-bone IK and Verlet chains, which turn one model into animated sprite frames. `ui/stage.js` (posed officer) and `snapArt()` (render once, read back) are the precedent;
    - **(c)** the post's grade → Bayer dither → quantise chain with "look as uniforms", re-targeted to a palette LUT at sprite resolution.
    - Later, for the voxel game: per-part `InstancedMesh` crowds with re-voxelised LODs.
11. **Gaps for the planet game (Fact, then Opinion).**
    - Facts: worlds are flat heightfield maps rasterised from authored 2D pieces. There is no chunking, streaming, curvature or floating origin. The only rig is a biped, and the only non-humanoid is a path-following dragon. Models are JS closures, not data.
    - Opinion: we must add a quadruped/avian rig, a data format, and the planet tech ourselves.
12. **2D sims (Opinion).** Pre-rendering voxel models to pixel-art sprite sheets with this builder and rig is **feasible and recommended**, with these conditions:
    - an orthographic fixed view;
    - 1 voxel = 1 sprite pixel, with one voxel size per model (the repo's finer head grid would break the pixel grid);
    - flat or banded light over the baked vertex AO;
    - a nearest-palette snap and a 1-px outline;
    - no MSAA, grain, DoF or bloom.

    The repo has no sprite baker. Its officers (~150 voxels tall) are far too fine for sims, so sim-scale coarse models are needed. Its 20×20 ASCII portraits (`chars/index.js:55-101,238-243`) are a ready-made LLM-authorable format for pixel icons and portraits.

### 1.2 Lifecycle (Fact, `src/main.js:1-22,132-156,219-255`)

1. The page boots under an "ink" overlay and loads the HOME map.
2. Screens are DOM states: title → select → loading → (prologue) → battle → result → title.
3. `deploy()` calls `startBattle()`. This resets every sim module, reseeds both RNGs (`vrng.seed(7936); rng.seed(1)`, main.js:143) and loads the map.
4. It then calls `post.compile()`, which runs `renderer.compileAsync` into the scene render target, and warms up a few frames, all hidden under the loading card. The effect is that no shader compiles mid-battle.
5. The sim steps only while `state === 'battle'` and the game is not paused.

### 1.3 The deterministic 60 Hz tick (Fact)

```js
// src/main.js:274-285
const d = Math.min(0.1, Math.max(0, (now - last) / 1000));
acc += d * (game.timeScale ?? 1); last = now;                // story: victory slow-mo
...
while (acc >= 1 / 60 && n < 4 && state === 'battle') { step(); acc -= 1 / 60; n++; }
if (n === 4) acc = 0;                                         // drop time instead of spiralling
render();
```

**Step order (`main.js:97-108`):**
1. `input.sample()`
2. `cam.step` (yaw only)
3. `hero.step`
4. `combat.step`
5. `crowd.step`
6. `actors.step`, then `pickups.step`
7. `musou.step`
8. `story.step`
9. `game.frame++`
10. `vfx.afterStep()`, which samples the slash trail from the pure `heroPose` once per sim step

**Separation rules:**
- **Render dt comes from sim frames, not wall time.** `render()` uses `dt = (game.frame - lastRenderFrame)/60` (main.js:112-115). Animation, VFX and chains therefore advance exactly with the sim. A capture that renders every second frame matches real-time play (`vfx.js:505-507` comment).
  - *Opinion:* there is **no render interpolation**. On a 120/144 Hz display, frames with no sim step have dt = 0; chains treat that as "paused" (`secondary.js:51`). WZ should interpolate with alpha = acc·60.
- **Input** is latched between steps and sampled exactly once per step (`core/input.js:1-3`).
- **Hitstop is sim state.** `if (game.hitstop > 0) { game.hitstop--; return; }` (`hero/hero.js:58-75`). Telegraphs, kicks and VFX ages all count sim frames, so they hold through hitstop.
- **RNG discipline:**
  - `rng` is the sim RNG (crowd, story).
  - `vrng` is visual only.
  - `hash01(i,j,k)` is a stateless hash for per-voxel jitter and procedural detail.
  - `projectiles.js` consumes no RNG state at all; spreads use `hash01`, "so a kit without shots leaves the sim bit-identical" (`combat/projectiles.js:4-5`).
  - Audio uses `Math.random` (`audio/bank.js:6`).
- **The event bus** (`core/events.js:67-86`) is a synchronous `on` / `emit`. `collect()` records the subscriptions a factory makes so per-kit views can unsubscribe them. The header (lines 6-63) catalogues every event, e.g. `hits`, `ko`, `land`, `hero:hurt`, `musou:burst`, `arrow:*`, `story:*`.

### 1.4 Dependencies (Fact)

- `vendor/three/` contains `three.module.js` and `three.core.js` (REVISION '186'), plus 4 addons: `Pass.js`, `UnrealBloomPass.js`, `CopyShader.js`, `LuminosityHighPassShader.js`. They are mapped by an importmap in `index.html`.
- There is no npm, no bundler and no runtime fetches.
- `checks/*.mjs` (campaign, campaign-walk, trials, trials-bot, shared-originality) are Node regression checks and a balance bot that drive the headless sim.
- `CanvasTexture`s use system fonts for CJK glyphs on banners and flags. The look is therefore platform-dependent in that one spot.

## 2. Rendering pipeline (Fact, `src/post/post.js`, `src/world/*`)

### 2.1 Passes and render targets

| # | Pass | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Shadow map | 2048², PCF | Sun only (`world/world.js:57`, `post.js:207-208`). The crowd casts proxy boxes (§3.4) |
| 1 | Scene | `sceneRT`: full resolution, **HalfFloat**, 4× MSAA, depth texture | Linear HDR working space |
| 2 | Atmos | `atmosRT`, full resolution | Aerial haze plus sun in-scatter computed from depth. Alpha = distance, reused by DoF (`post.js:52-80`) |
| 3 | DoF | `dofRT`, half resolution | Gather with square "bokeh"; focus is the hero (`post.js:85-105`) |
| 4 | Bloom | UnrealBloom, half resolution | Custom prefilter that keeps hue (`post.js:219-234`) |
| 5 | God rays | `raysRT`, half resolution | Skipped when the sun is off screen (`post.js:110-126`) |
| 6 | Final | Canvas | Sharpen, chromatic aberration, streaks, split-tone, Lottes tone curve plus knee, hot desaturation, vignette and darker bottom band, flash, grain, **2-px Bayer dither, quantise to 72 levels** (`post.js:128-200`) |

- `setLook()` swaps per-map grading uniforms (`post.js:250-258`).
- An **auto quality tier** watches frame time. If the frame-time EMA stays above 20 ms for about 2 s, MSAA steps down 4× → 2× → 0 and never climbs back (`post.js:272-282`). `?hq` pins full quality.

### 2.2 Materials (Fact)

- **Voxel bodies.**
  - `MeshStandardMaterial({vertexColors, flatShading})`, with AO and colour jitter baked into vertex colours.
  - `heroLook` patches the shader via `onBeforeCompile` to add a camera-side fill light and a Fresnel rim (`hero/model.js:276-290`).
  - Officers get `body`, `metal` and `blade` materials (`model.js:300-308`).
- **Crowd.**
  - The same material, patched for a per-instance `aHit` attribute that drives the hit flash (`crowd/view.js:337-368`).
  - `nearFade()` adds a dithered dissolve near the lens, plus a *sight cone* that fades soldiers standing between camera and hero (`crowd/view.js:299-325`).
  - Flags use a CanvasTexture glyph plus a vertex-shader wave (`crowd/view.js:220-236`).
- **World.**
  - `installHaze` replaces Three's fog chunks with sun-aware haze driven by shared `ATMO` uniforms (`world/sky.js:53-62,110-139`). The sky is a dome with voxel clouds (`sky.js:142-188`).
  - The ground is a heightfield plane with a splat shader (paving or road masks, CanvasTextures generated in code) (`terrain.js:64-261`).
  - `voxelGrain()` adds world-space voxel-cell shading to smooth surfaces (`terrain.js:394-404`).
- **Musou dragon.** Shaded boxes on an `InstancedMesh`, plus an additive rim "glow shell" and a back-face "ink outline" (`musou/view.js:30-38,167-190`).

### 2.3 Where the pixel look comes from (Fact)

```glsl
// src/post/post.js:195-198
c += (hash(gl_FragCoord.xy + fract(uTime * 7.31) * 97.0) - 0.5) * uGrain;
c += bayer4(floor(gl_FragCoord.xy * 0.5)) * uDither / uLevels;   // 2-px ordered dither
c = floor(c * uLevels + 0.5) / uLevels;                           // 72 levels/channel
```

- There is no `setPixelRatio` anywhere in the code (grep).
- `renderer.setSize(innerWidth, innerHeight, false)` (`post.js:262`) plus `canvas#c { image-rendering: pixelated }` (`index.html:13`) means that on HiDPI screens the frame is rendered at CSS-pixel resolution and nearest-upscaled.
- The "voxel" read comes from the geometry: voxels are 1.25–4.2 cm, and every face is flat-shaded with AO.

**Fact: the defaults** (`post.js:25-37`) are `levels: 72`, `dither: 0.6` (in units of one level), `grain: 0.025` (re-seeded about 7×/s), `rowNoise: 0.01` on 2-px rows, `ca: 1.3`, `sharpen: 0.35`, `streak: 0.05`. The header calls the step "palette quantisation" (`post.js:10`), but the code is a **uniform per-channel quantise**, not a palette.

**Opinion:**
- It is a cheap, good-looking *retro texture* on a cinematic image.
- It is **not pixel art**: full resolution, MSAA edges, soft DoF and bloom gradients, time-varying grain, and no palette.
- §5(ii) breaks down what to keep and what to change for the sims and the pixel-art voxel game.

## 3. Key techniques in depth

### 3.1 Voxel builder and the hero model format

**Files:**
- `src/hero/model.js` — mesher `vox()` 28-75, helpers 78-82, `lamellar()` 84-101, hero body 104-228, weapon 231-267, `buildBody` 310-337, `buildDef` 339-349.
- `src/chars/defkit.js:1-37` — the data contract.
- `src/chars/parts.js` — shared parts at `FV = 0.0125`, plus `symH`.
- `src/chars/lubu/model.js` — a full example.

**How it works (Fact):**

1. **A model def** is
   `build() → { parts: {jointName: boxes[]}, head: boxes[], bv, hv, pauldron?(sx) → boxes[], weapon: [{geo, mat}] }`
   plus `chains() → [...]` for cloth and hair (`defkit.js:7-14`). Joint names are fixed by the rig: `hips`, `spine`, `chest`, `neck`, `head`, `upperArmL/R`, `foreArmL/R`, `handL/R`, `thighL/R`, `shinL/R`, `footL/R`, `weapon`, plus helper `pauldronL/R`.
2. **A box** is `B(a, b, c, paint)`.
   - `a` and `b` are integer voxel coordinates in the joint's local frame; `b` is exclusive.
   - `c` is `0xRRGGBB`, `-1` (carve), or `fn(x,y,z) → colour | null`, where `null` leaves the voxel untouched.
   - `P(a, b, c)` is a paint-only box that recolours existing voxels: eyes, trim, rouge.
   - `mirX(box, sx)` mirrors a box for the left/right side; parts are authored with +x outward.
   - Later boxes overwrite earlier ones.
3. **Procedural patterns are colour functions.**
   - `lamellar(a, b, {base, rowH, pw, trim, jag, lipX, lipZ})` emits a base box with staggered plate seams, plus one protruding "lip" row per plate row. The AO under each lip makes the scales read (`model.js:84-101`).
   - Officers define their own patterns. Lü Bu's `brocade` is gold four-petal flowers on a staggered lattice; his `mail` is iron rings in staggered rows (`chars/lubu/model.js:33-44`).
4. **Voxel sizes per part.**
   - Hero: body `V = 0.025` m, head `HV = 0.0175` m.
   - Def officers: body `FV = 0.0125`, head ≈ 0.013.
   - Weapons: shaft 0.02, fittings 0.012, blade 0.011.
   - Crowd: 0.042.
   - A ≈1.85 m officer at FV is ≈150 voxels tall.
5. **Weapons and blades** are often built by loops that emit 1-voxel-thick slices, e.g. a leaf-shaped spear point via per-z width, and the crescent halberd blade via two circles plus engraved line and holes (`lubu/model.js:176-221`).

**Excerpt (Fact, `src/chars/lubu/model.js:33-38,276-283`):**

```js
const brocade = (x, y, z) => {          // a colour function = a procedural texture in voxel space
  const u = x + z, v = y + (md(Math.floor(u / 6), 2) ? 3 : 0), a = md(u, 6), b = md(v, 6);
  if (a === 3 && b === 3) return C.goldL;
  ...
};
T['upperArm' + s] = [B([-6, -24, -6], [6, 2, 6], brocade), B([-7, -19, -7], [7, -8, 7], brocade)];
export const LUBU_DEF = {
  build: () => ({ parts: limbs(torso()), head: head(), bv: FV, hv: HV, pauldron, weapon: weaponGeo() }),
  chains() { /* {joint, anchor, rest, n, len, stiff, drag, wind, cone, sway, face, seg(i,n) → geometry, hit:[...]} */ },
};
```

**Cost (Fact).** Meshing happens once at kit load. `vox()` is O(grid volume × 6 faces), with 4 AO neighbour tests per corner.

**Limits (Fact, then Opinion):**
- Facts: models are **JS code with closures**, not data. There is no file format, schema or validation.
- Opinion: this expressiveness is exactly why the officers look so detailed. It is also why an LLM-authored *data* format must replace closures with **named pattern ops plus parameters** (see Appendix A).
- Opinion: the coordinate conventions are implicit: odd-width offsets, `off: [-0.5, 0, 0]` for heads, joint-centred "fine" parts. Our format must state them explicitly.

### 3.2 Rasterise → mesh (Fact)

There are **two meshers, both culled-face only (no greedy merge):**

| | `core/voxel.js` `sculpt()` (94-124) | `hero/model.js` `vox()` (28-75) |
|---|---|---|
| Input space | Metre-space boxes, snapped to voxel size `v` | Integer voxel-unit boxes |
| Colour fn | `(wx, wy, wz, i, j, k)` | `(x, y, z)` |
| AO | **None.** It calls `quad()` with no AO factors (`voxel.js:69-84`) | **Per-vertex 3-neighbour AO**, levels `[1-ao, 1-0.6ao, 1-0.25ao, 1]`, plus an AO-aware diagonal flip (`model.js:56-73`) |
| Jitter | `shade(c, 1 ± jitter/2 · hash01)` per voxel | Same |
| Used by | Crowd soldiers and LOD re-voxelisation | Hero, officers, chain segments, VFX arrows |

Both feed `makeBuilder()` (`voxel.js:18-38`), an indexed quad soup with per-corner brightness `k` (the baked AO) and a `flip` flag.

A third path, `boxesGeometry([{s, p, c, r?, skip?}])` (`voxel.js:44-62`), merges oriented boxes without voxelising. It is used for far LODs, shadow proxies, castles, debris clumps and the dragon. `skip` drops hidden faces: debris went from 84 to 48 tris per chunk (`vfx/vfx.js:50`).

```js
// src/hero/model.js:63-72 — classic per-corner AO (side1, side2, corner)
const a = full(...s1), b = full(...s2);
lv[q] = a && b ? 0 : 3 - a - b - full(...cc);
kq[q] = AO[lv[q]];
...
vb.quad(corners, f.n, _col.r, _col.g, _col.b, kq, lv[0] + lv[2] < lv[1] + lv[3]);   // flip to avoid AO anisotropy
```

**Cost (Fact, from comments).** A near crowd soldier is ≈4,900 tris; its far-LOD box version is ≈450 (`crowd/view.js:198-200`).

**Opinion:**
- Culled faces are fine for characters: AO per vertex needs unmerged quads anyway, and greedy meshing would break per-voxel colour jitter.
- For terrain, villages and fortresses on a planet we need greedy meshing, or at least merged-face runs per colour, plus chunking.

### 3.3 Rig and animation system

**Files:**
- `src/hero/rig.js` — DIM 20-24, CH 31-45, P() 60-70, clip/sample 93-124, blend 128-134, blendStep 162-173, spearElev 183-194, IK 218-243, createRig 246-364.
- `src/hero/anims/author.js` (29-116), `anims/locomotion.js` (85-279), `anims/attacks.js`.
- `src/hero/secondary.js`.
- `src/chars/loco.js`.

**How it works (Fact):**

1. **Skeleton.** `createRig` builds a fixed `Object3D` hierarchy with segment lengths from `DIM` (thigh 0.44, shin 0.44, upper arm 0.29, …). Every voxel part is a child mesh of its joint, so there is no skinning.
2. **Pose = `Float32Array(45)` in root space.**
   - hips xyz plus rotation; spine, chest and head angles;
   - foot L/R as ankle position, pitch and yaw;
   - the weapon as position, yaw, elevation and roll;
   - grip offsets along the shaft; free-arm FK flags and angles;
   - a whole-body `spin`, and a `plant` flag.

   ```js
   // src/hero/rig.js:31-45
   export const CH = { hips: 0, hipsR: 3, spine: 6, chest: 9, head: 12, footL: 15, footR: 20, spear: 25,
     gripR: 31, gripL: 32, lfree: 33, armL: 34, spin: 38, plant: 39, rfree: 40, armR: 41 };
   export const POSE_SIZE = 45;
   ```

   - Poses are authored as small specs over a base `STANCE`, in degrees: `P({spine:[..], footL:[..]})`.
   - The **weapon is keyed directly**; hands are solved onto it.
3. **Clips** are `clip([[t, pose, ease], ...], loop, mono)`.
   - Sampling is cubic Hermite with **Catmull-Rom** tangents, or with **monotone (Fritsch–Carlson)** tangents. In mono mode, holds and reversals stop cleanly with no overshoot (`rig.js:86-124`).
   - Per-key easing curves are supported.
   - `blendPose` lerps, with angles taking the shortest way round (`rig.js:128-134`).
4. **IK.**
   - `solve2()` is analytic 2-bone IK: law of cosines plus a pole vector (`rig.js:218-234`).
   - Arms reach the weapon line, and `reachOnShaft` slides each hand along the shaft to the nearest reachable spot instead of letting it float (`rig.js:237-243`).
   - Legs reach foot targets, and the pelvis drops when targets are out of reach.
   - The weapon's ground contact (`spearElev`) keeps blades out of the ground.
5. **Authoring helpers** (`anims/author.js`):
   - `clipF` keys clips in *sim frames* anchored to the move's hit window, so animation and hitboxes cannot drift.
   - `bakeFeet` / `plantFoot` bake planted feet per sim frame against the move's lunge. Feet are ground spots in move-start coordinates, so a lunge does not skate them, and every touchdown adds a pelvis dip.
6. **Procedural locomotion** (`anims/locomotion.js`):
   - `runPose(phase, k, out, lean)` is stride maths matched to ground speed, with no foot skating and a banked lean (85-129).
   - The dodge is a dive roll applied at the rig root, with squash and stretch (138-177).
   - Dodge "ghosts" are afterimage shells with a back-face rim (189-279).
   - `blendStep` makes the feet *step* during transitions instead of sliding (`rig.js:162-173`).
7. **Moves are data** (`hero/moves.js:1-54` documents the format):
   - `{frames, next, charge, cancel, branch, dodgeCancel, steer, lunge:[[f0, f1, m]], hits:[{f:[a, b], shape:'arc'|'circle'|'line', range, ang, dir, dmg, kb, force, hitstop, every, sweep, heavy, ...}]}`
   - Combat is resolved from these shapes in the sim, never from bones, so animation is purely cosmetic and determinism holds.
8. **Secondary motion** (`hero/secondary.js:19-105`). Per chain, per frame, with sub-steps of `round(dt·120)`, capped at 6:
   1. A Verlet step with drag.
   2. Gravity scaled by `grav`.
   3. A wind gust (sum of sines) streaming behind the body.
   4. Two constraint iterations, each doing a length constraint, a lerp toward the rest direction (`stiff`), a cone limit around rest, and push-out from sphere colliders on named joints.
   5. Each segment is a voxel slab mesh oriented along the chain, with local −Y along it.

   Pauldrons are a helper joint that turns halfway with the upper arm. Chain parameters live in the def (Lü Bu: capes n=7, braids n=5, pheasant feathers n=13 with `grav: 0.45`; `lubu/model.js:276-310`).

**Cost (Fact, then Opinion):**
- Fact: rig evaluation is ~20 joint transforms, 4 IK solves and N chain steps per character per frame, all on the CPU.
- Opinion, computed from `LUBU_DEF.chains()`: **Lü Bu has ≈93 chain-segment meshes plus ≈20 body meshes plus 3 weapon meshes, so ≈115 draw calls (×2 with shadows)**. That is fine for 1–3 officers, not for a village.

**Limits (Fact, then Opinion):**
- Fact: there is only one biped rig layout, with hard-coded channel offsets and spear-centric arm logic. There is no quadruped, wing or tail rig. The only creature is the dragon (§3.8).
- Opinion: the *design* (a flat pose vector, named channels, keys as partial specs over a stance, procedural gaits, IK on ends, physics only for cosmetic chains) generalises well. The *code* would need a per-archetype channel layout.

### 3.4 Crowds with InstancedMesh

**Files:**
- `src/crowd/crowd.js` — SoA sim 74-90, squads 135-183, step 245+, spatial hash 672-712.
- `src/crowd/view.js` — parts 46-171, geometries and LOD 181-217, pose 239-285, mk 410-432, write and replay 632-717, update 720-747.
- `src/crowd/armies.js`.

**How it works (Fact):**

1. **Sim.**
   - Struct-of-arrays `Float64Array` / `Int32Array` per soldier: position, velocity, state, timer, HP, squad, etc.
   - Squads keep formations; a director hands out attack tokens and rings around the hero in distance bands; feints, grace windows and waves are scripted.
   - Separation uses a 400×400 grid of 1.2 m cells with `head` / `next` linked lists (`crowd.js:672-712`).
2. **Geometry.**
   - Each body part (hips, torso, head, crest, arm, thigh, shin, plus weapons and shields) is built from **metre-space boxes with colour functions**. It is sculpted three ways:
     - **near**: `sculpt(boxes, V = 0.042)`;
     - **mid** (8–28 m): `sculpt(boxes, 2V)`, the same silhouette with about ¼ of the faces;
     - **far** (> 28 m): unvoxelised `boxesGeometry`, with the trunk parts merged on one matrix.
   - Allies (Shu) get their own palette version of every LOD (`view.js:181-217`, distances at 461).
3. **One `InstancedMesh` per part per LOD** (`mk`, `view.js:412-432`): dynamic `instanceMatrix`, `instanceColor`, a per-instance `aHit` glow, and `frustumCulled = false`. `count` is rebuilt every frame.
4. **Animation is CPU hierarchical matrices.**
   - Each soldier has a 26-channel pose: torso, head, arms, thighs, shins, swing and weapons.
   - Target poses come from state (idle, march, windup, strike, recoil, knockdown, airborne tumble, …). Recoil variants are chosen by hash (`view.js:252-285`).
   - The current pose eases toward the target exponentially: `cur += (T - cur) * (1 - exp(-rate·dt))`.
   - `write()` composes parent × local matrices per part and packs them into the instance arrays (`view.js:654-717`).
5. **Tricks:**
   - **Idle replay.** Every write is also recorded per soldier (≤16 instances). Standing grunts are recomputed only every 4th frame, staggered by index, and copied from the record in between (`view.js:632-652, 738-740`).
   - **Shadow proxies.** Each near mesh has a twin `InstancedMesh` using unvoxelised boxes (~12 tris per part) that *shares* the instance-matrix buffer. Its `onBeforeRender` (called in the main pass only) sets `drawRange.count = 0`, so it draws only in the shadow pass (`view.js:196-201, 410-432`).
   - **Per-soldier frustum sphere cull** before writing (`view.js:733`).
   - **`m.visible = m.count > 0`**, so there are no empty draws.

**Excerpt (Fact, `src/crowd/view.js:421-428`):**

```js
const p = new THREE.InstancedMesh(shadow, proxyMat, n);
p.instanceMatrix = m.instanceMatrix; p.castShadow = true; ...
// perf r5: shadow pass only — the main pass calls onBeforeRender (the shadow pass doesn't), so the invisible proxy
// draws 0 indices there (it used to cost as many main-pass tris as its shadow: ≈ 110k at C6)
p.onBeforeRender = () => { shadow.drawRange.count = 0; };
p.onAfterRender = () => { shadow.drawRange.count = Infinity; };
```

**Cost (Opinion).** Draw calls are constant: about 3 LODs × ~10 parts × 2 armies, plus proxies. The CPU work is O(N × parts) matrix multiplies plus full buffer uploads every frame (`needsUpdate` on whole arrays). At 2000 soldiers I expect this to be JS-bound rather than GPU-bound; this is unmeasured.

**Limits (Fact):**
- Rigid parts only; humanoid only.
- No GPU animation (no bone or VAT textures).
- Each soldier is a palette variant of one body. Officer "looks" (helmet wing, horn, crest, cap) are separate geometry sets (`armies.js:25-45`).

### 3.5 Voxel debris and VFX

**Files:**
- `src/vfx/vfx.js` — header 1-28, clump 44-56, army debris 63-71, `makePool` 508-599, pools around 625-631, hit/KO handlers 950-1006, trail 1210+.
- `src/vfx/arrows.js`.

**How it works (Fact):**
- **`makePool(scene, n, mat, now, {geo, drag, shrink, fade, near})`** is an `InstancedMesh` ring buffer of SoA particles (`x`, `y`, `z`, `vx`…, `life`, `size`, `rot`, `kind`, colour).

- **Debris** is a generic **"clump"**: a 2×2×2 voxel cube with one corner knocked out and per-voxel shade, 48 tris (`vfx.js:44-56`).
  - KO debris is *not* the soldier's own voxels. It is clumps tinted from the foe army's palette, weighted by how much of the soldier each colour covers (`armyDebris`, `vfx.js:57-71`), plus a few bigger "body blocks": helmet, torso, shield, headband.

- **Other primitives:** needles, stars, beams, rings (voxel-stepped), crack decals, shock walls, point-light flashes. A **per-frame hit budget** caps spawns.
- Kits add their own render layers (`chars/kitview.js`, `chars/zhugeliang/fx.js`).
- Arrows are a SoA pool in the sim (`combat/projectiles.js`, 480 max) and voxel arrows in render (`vfx/arrows.js`).

**Opinion.**
- The clump trick is the right cost/benefit choice: real per-voxel shattering would need per-model voxel data at runtime.
- For WZ, keep the model's voxel grid around (it is small) and emit clumps coloured from it to make "your" debris.

### 3.7 Procedural WebAudio SFX (Fact)

**Files:**
- `src/audio/bank.js` — `bake` 78-96, `bakeLoop` 99-107, `voice` 124-160, LINES 164-186, grunt/cry 194-213, `whoosh` 215-234, `clank` 236-242, `impact` 249+, `bakeVoice` 366, `buildBank` 379.
- `src/audio/audio.js` — buses 68-100, play 110-121, sidechain 141-150, cues 166-187, Musou drone 338-382.

**How it works:**
- **Bake once at boot, play many.**
  - `bake(dur, fn)` renders a node graph in an `OfflineAudioContext` at 48 kHz.
  - It then normalises to a peak, trims trailing silence below −66 dBFS, and adds a 6 ms fade.
  - Loops render at 24 kHz with the tail folded back onto the start, cross-faded at equal power.
  - The comments put the whole bank at about 0.9 s; combat sounds are ready after about 0.1 s.
- **Formant voice synth.**
  - The source is a sawtooth with an f0 contour, jitter (lowpassed noise into frequency), vibrato, and optional "growl" (sub-harmonic AM).
  - It runs through formant bandpasses.
  - Each kit has its own `voice {pitch, fk, growl, gain}` for its battle cries.
- **Whoosh.** A bandpassed noise sweep with a fast attack, a highpassed "tear", a narrow "whistle" band and an optional sine sub.
- **Clank.** Modal synthesis with partials at 1, 2.76, 5.4 and 8.93 × base, each decaying faster than the last, plus a click.
- **Impacts** layer grains, thumps and metal.
- **Runtime:**
  - `play()` with random rate, gain and pan, so 50+ hits/s never repeat back to back.
  - Buses with sends to a convolution reverb (generated impulse response), EQ, compressor and soft clip.
  - Sidechain and bed ducking.
  - A Musou drone.

## 4. Performance

### 4.1 Facts (from code and its `perf rN` comments)

- **Enemy count:** default 300, adjustable 0–2000 via `?enemies` (`main.js:55-56`).
- **Crowd triangle budget:** near ≈4.9k tris per soldier, far ≈450; LOD switches at 8 m and 28 m (`crowd/view.js:198-200,461`).
- **Main-pass and shadow-pass levers already pulled:**
  - shadow proxies (the old proxy cost about 110k wasted tris at chapter 6);
  - idle replay every 4th frame;
  - frustum sphere cull per soldier;
  - debris clump 84 → 48 tris (×1,300 chunks × 2 passes);
  - pools packed so `count = live` (`vfx.js:50,591`, `arrows.js:489`).
- **Shader stalls:** `compileAsync` into the real render target during loading, and a new actor is shown only once its programs are ready (`post.js:289-294`, `actors/view.js:5-6`).
- **GPU safety valve:** MSAA 4 → 2 → 0 on a sustained frame-time EMA above 20 ms (`post.js:272-282`).
- **CPU safety valve:** at most 4 sim steps per frame; on overflow, time is dropped, so it never spirals (`main.js:281-282`).
- **Fixed GPU costs:**
  - a full-res HalfFloat MSAA scene target plus a full-res atmos pass and the final pass;
  - half-res DoF, bloom (mip chain) and rays;
  - a 2048² PCF shadow map.

### 4.2 Likely bottlenecks (Opinion, not measured; we did not run it)

1. **Crowd CPU.** Per visible non-idle soldier: 26-channel easing, about 10–14 `Matrix4` multiplies and copies, and record writes. Then full-array `needsUpdate` uploads for every instanced mesh, each frame. At 2000 soldiers this is the main-thread limit.
2. **Fill rate.** HalfFloat MSAA plus 3 full-screen full-res passes. On integrated or mobile GPUs this, not geometry, sets the frame time. The auto tier only touches MSAA.
3. **Officer draw calls.** About 115 meshes per richly dressed officer (chains are one mesh per segment), doubled with shadows.
4. **Non-greedy meshes.** Fine at character scale; wasteful for any large static voxel content.

### 4.3 Levers for WZ (Opinion)

- **Greedy or run-merged meshing** for static content (terrain chunks, buildings). Keep culled-face plus AO for characters.
- **Chains:** merge segments into one dynamic `BufferGeometry` per chain set, or render them as instanced slabs.
- **Crowds:** move the instance animation to the GPU. Options are per-instance pose channels in an `InstancedBufferAttribute`, with joint matrices composed in the vertex shader for rigid parts, or a bone texture. Upload only dirty ranges (`addUpdateRange`).
- **Post:** offer a "low" tier that skips DoF and rays and renders the scene at 0.5–0.75 scale with the existing dither/quantise.
- **Add render interpolation** (alpha = acc·60) so 120+ Hz displays don't show repeated frames.

#### B. What it lacks for sprites (Fact)

- There is no orthographic sprite camera, no low-res render target, no palette mapping, no outline pass, and no sheet packer or atlas metadata.
- The code has a single voxel size per part, but **mixes sizes inside one character**: body 0.0125 or 0.025, head 0.013 or 0.0175, weapon 0.011–0.02.
- The whole render path is HDR, MSAA and cinematic. Grain and row noise change over time, so two renders of the same pose differ.

#### C. Recommended sprite baker (Opinion)

1. **Model.**
   - Use one voxel size for the whole model, with no finer head grid.
   - Target height is 16–32 voxels for a Kairosoft-scale person, with chibi proportions: the head is about 40% of the height.
   - Snap jitter to palette steps, or disable it.
   - Use a named palette of 16–32 colours per game (Appendix A `sprite` block).
2. **Pose.**
   - Sample clips at the sprite frame count, e.g. walk 4–6, idle 2, work 4, carry 4.
   - Run chains at a fixed dt with a seeded wind phase, after a pre-roll.
   - Snap the model root to whole voxels per frame so the body does not sub-pixel jitter.
3. **Camera.**
   - Orthographic, fixed preset.
   - Kairosoft-like ¾: pitch about 30°, front or 4 directions.
   - Iso builds: true 2:1 pixel dimetric. Yaw 45° with pitch 30° (= arcsin 0.5) projects the ground axes onto the 26.57° (2:1) pixel slope.
   - Scale the frustum so 1 voxel projects to exactly an integer number of pixels.
4. **Raster.**
   - Use no AA (`samples: 0`, `NearestFilter`), with flat colour plus the baked AO.
   - Use at most 2–3 quantised light bands from one key light (cel), not PBR.
   - Optionally add a rim term (à la `heroLook`) only on silhouette pixels.
5. **Post on the small image.**
   - Snap to the nearest palette colour (OKLab distance).
   - Draw a 1-px outline from alpha and ID edges, darkened per neighbour rather than pure black.
   - Dither: none on sprites. Use `bayer4` only for large gradients (sky, water), reusing the repo's `bayer4` and dither-before-quantise order.
   - Keep everything deterministic: no grain or row noise.
6. **Pack.**
   - Trim, pack and write `sheet.png` plus `sheet.json`: frame rects, a **foot pivot** (the rig's ground point), direction, clip, and frame duration in sim frames.
7. **Two render backends.**
   - **GPU** (Three.js in the studio) for interactive preview.
   - **A CPU triangle rasteriser** of the `vox()` mesh (z-buffer plus flat colours, a few hundred lines) for the actual bake. It runs in Node and is **bit-exact on every machine**, so a sprite sheet can be regression-tested by hash.
   - At 16–32 px per sprite, CPU cost is negligible.
   - *Opinion:* this avoids GPU and driver variance and headless-WebGL issues.
8. **Mixel control.**
   - Rotated rigid parts at tiny scale produce single stray pixels.
   - Mitigations:
     - limit limb rotations in sprite clips to a few angles, or snap them to multiples of 15–22.5°;
     - prefer **translation-heavy poses** (bob, step, arm swing) over large rotations;
     - add an optional "orphan pixel" cleanup pass that merges one-pixel islands into a neighbour colour.

#### D. Gotchas specific to this repo's techniques (Opinion)

- **Chains and loops.** The Verlet chains are history-dependent. For a looping walk, bake two cycles and keep the second, or blend the last k frames into the first.
- **Patterns at sim scale.** Patterns such as `lamellar` (rowH 2–3, pw 3–4) need 6–10 voxels to read. At 16–24-voxel characters, use 1-voxel accents instead: belt, collar, trim (`P` paint boxes).
- **Per-voxel jitter** (`shade(c, 1 ± j/2)`) is pleasant in 3D. In sprites it creates off-palette colours, so palette-snap it or set jitter to 0.
- **Props and buildings.** Box builders (`castle.js`) give flat-coloured faces. Add a per-face light-band rule (top light, left mid, right dark) — the classic pixel-art cube shading — so they match the characters.

#### E. Verdict for the sims (Opinion)

| Asset class | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Characters (staff, customers, villagers) | **Good** | One coarse model per archetype; seed params give palette and part variants. 4 directions × (idle 2, walk 4–6, work 4) frames |
| Props, furniture, buildings | **Very good** | Static; 1–4 rotations; box builders are simple to author |
| Portraits and icons | **Use ASCII grids directly** | `paintPortrait` format; no 3D needed |
| Effects (sparkles, coins, smoke) | **Better hand-coded 2D** | The repo's VFX are 3D instanced pools: overkill for sims |

**Caveat.** Kairosoft sprites rely on intentional clusters and exaggerated chibi proportions. Expect a dedicated coarse-part library for sims rather than reusing 3D-game models. The **same format and baker** will still produce the later voxel game's distant LOD impostors and UI icons.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Code vs data for models.**
   - The repo's expressiveness comes from colour closures.
   - Do we accept JS model modules authored by the LLM (expressive; needs sandboxing and review), or a closed JSON op set (safe and validatable; less expressive)?
   - Appendix A proposes data with named patterns plus an escape hatch.
2. **Which skeleton templates for v1?** Biped, quadruped, avian/flyer and serpent? Do we generalise the 45-channel layout into a template-defined layout, or write one hand-tuned rig per template, as the repo does for its biped?
3. **Cross-engine determinism.**
   - The sim uses `Math.sin`, `Math.exp` and Float64. Bit-identical results across browser engines are not guaranteed.
   - The repo only needs same-machine determinism (replays and checks).
   - Do we need cross-machine (multiplayer, shared seeds producing the *same world geometry*)? If so, terrain generation must avoid transcendental drift: integer hashing, or fixed-point maths.
4. **Planet tech** is entirely outside this repo: chunked LOD, floating origin, curvature, spherical coordinates for builders and crowds.
5. **WebGL2 vs WebGPU.** The repo is `WebGLRenderer` only. GPU crowd animation and greedy meshing in compute would favour WebGPU.
6. **Performance baselines are unmeasured.** We did not run the repo. If we want numbers (soldiers vs frame time, per GPU tier), someone needs to run it locally with `?enemies=2000` and `?hq` on our target hardware. That is outside this research-only task.
7. **Fonts.** The repo uses system CJK fonts in CanvasTextures. For "graphics by code" with identical output everywhere, we would need code-defined bitmap glyphs, like its 20×20 ASCII portraits.
8. **Reuse vs reimplement.** The MIT licence allows copying with the notice kept. Do we vendor pieces (mesher, IK, chains) or reimplement from this analysis to keep WZ's code style uniform?
9. **Sim sprite spec (owner decision).**
   - Target sprite size: 16, 24 or 32 px tall?
   - View: front ¾ or 2:1 isometric?
   - Directions: 2 (mirrored), 4 or 8?
   - Palette size and whether it is shared across games.

   These fix the voxel budget per model.
10. **Bake backend.** A CPU rasteriser (deterministic, Node-testable) vs GPU readback (Three.js, faster to build, driver-variant)? Opinion: CPU for the bake, GPU for preview.
11. **How much hand cleanup is acceptable?** Is a fully automatic bake required, or can an LLM or human pass fix mixels per frame? Is a pixel-level "touch-up overlay" (paint boxes in sprite space) in scope?
12. **"Voxels rendered as pixel art" in the later 3D game.**
    - Real-time pixel art needs:
      - a low-res target;
      - a texel-snapped camera;
      - a palette LUT and outlines;
      - motion handled so it does not crawl.
    - This is a different render path from the sprite baker. Decide whether both share one palette and style preset (recommended).

## Appendix A: Proposed WZ voxel model and rig format (sketch, Opinion)

**Goals:**
- An LLM can author it reliably: integer coordinates, named colours, no code.
- It is validatable.
- It is deterministic from a seed.
- One file drives the 3D mesh, LODs, rig binding, chains and 2D sprite baking.

It is directly inspired by `hero/model.js` (`B` / `P` / `mirX` / lamellar), `chars/defkit.js` (`parts` per joint, `chains`), and `crowd/view.js` (LOD by re-voxelising).

### A.3 Rules the generator and validator enforce

- **Ops** are `box`, `paint` (recolour existing voxels only), `carve`, `mirror` (axis plus ops), `copyOf`, `mirrorOf`, and `if` on boolean params. Expressions are restricted to `param ± int`, `*`, `/`.
- **Colours** must be palette keys or pattern names; no raw hex inside parts. A pattern `fn` comes from a fixed library (`noise3`, `bands`, `checker`, `lamellar`, `scales`, `brocade`, `gradient`), each a pure function of `(x, y, z, seed)`.
- **Mesher:** culled faces plus 3-neighbour AO plus hash jitter for characters (as `vox()`); greedy meshing for static builders and terrain.
- **Determinism:** `resolve(params, seed)` and then `build()` is pure. The mesh hash is stored for regression checks, and so is the **sprite-sheet hash** from the CPU baker.
- **Sprite-targeted models:**
  - one `voxel` for all parts;
  - jitter 0, or snapped to palette steps;
  - every colour inside the `style` palette;
  - height budget checked against the style's sprite height.
- **Validation:**
  - every part's joint exists in the template;
  - the bounding box per joint fits the template's expected size;
  - voxel and op budgets are respected;
  - mirrored pairs stay within ±1 voxel of symmetric;
  - chains' `hit` joints exist.
